The quantity inside a physics-informed model is not the quantity it is named after

A diagnosis of the learned intermediate in a COSMO-SAC solubility model, and the apparatus that produced it.

Abstract

Physics-informed predictors combine learned physical quantities through a fixed thermodynamic relation. Their appeal is that the prediction can be taken apart and audited, and that a better value for any one of those quantities can be looked up and supplied without retraining. The promise holds only if the value a network places in the slot of a physical quantity is that quantity. We test it on a graph network that supplies the σ-profile consumed by COSMO-SAC to predict the solubility of solids, reading every measurement against a control. The solubility observations determine two of the fifty directions in which a profile can move, and in the directions they leave free the learned profile places screening-charge density where the reference carries none, concentrated in the windows that activate the model's hydrogen-bonding term. That departure is the most visible and the least consequential, because the error reaching the thermodynamics is in the magnitude of the profile where it is populated. The intermediate also does not decompose, so replacing part of it with reference values is frequently worse than replacing none. Supplying the whole reference profile improves the activity coefficients the profile exists to produce and degrades the solubility predicted from them.

What this is not

The focus is the diagnosis of the intermediate, not state-of-the-art solubility prediction. On the Bemis–Murcko scaffold-disjoint split used throughout, the physics path trails a matched direct control sharing the same encoder in the mean over seeds, and both sit near a random-forest baseline. No published model was retrained here, so the comparison with prior work is a comparison of design choices and not of performance.

Every departure is measured against one reference tabulation, VT-2005, and a zero in that table is a tabulated zero rather than a proven absence of density. The profile head reads a role-dependent representation, so a molecule receives two profiles; for the window displacement and the residual population, measurement settles that the defect is not a consequence of that choice, and for the reference-state violation it is one.

Reproducing the pipeline

Data preparation rebuilds the processed splits from cached public sources. One warning is worth repeating here, because it invalidates comparisons silently: a regenerated split is a new split. The seeded scaffold partition is not stable across pipeline versions — a rebuild has been observed to turn over about three quarters of which scaffolds land in test — so every metric computed on the previous split is orphaned by it.

Local runs are smoke configs and produce meaningless numbers; real results need a GPU run on the full corpus. A checkpoint also carries the machine it was trained on: the same arm and seed retrained on another GPU does not reproduce to within the spread across seeds.

Data

Solubility is BigSolDB 2.0, partitioned by Bemis–Murcko scaffold. Reference σ-profiles are the VT-2005 tabulation. The infinite-dilution activity coefficients that score the closure directly come from ThermoML. Crystal melting points and enthalpies of fusion come from an open melting-point pool plus a group-contribution prior. All sources are public. Model checkpoints, the processed split and the larger analysis artifacts are deposited in a Zenodo archive, and its DOI is published with the article.
