\title{The Grounding Paradox: Why True Physical Inputs Make a\\
Solubility Model Worse, and How to Measure It}
\author{N.~L. Polomoshnov \and \pending[co-authors]}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Physics-informed models for solid--liquid solubility factor the target
$\lnx=-\Phicry(T)-\lng$ into a pure-component crystal term $\Phicry$ and a pair
activity term $\lng$. The activity term is increasingly computed by a
\emph{differentiable thermodynamic closure}, here COSMO-SAC over a predicted
$\sigma$-profile, on the premise that grounding the closure on true physical
inputs improves it. We find that it does the opposite. Replacing the model's
learned $\sigma$-profiles with the true, externally measured ones (VT-2005) makes
both activity and solubility predictions \emph{worse} than a free learned head,
an effect we confirm three independent ways. We then use the paradox as a
measurement. Because the physical latent $\zstar$ has external ground truth, the
true-input excess risk splits exactly, with no model fit, into a
decoder-misspecification term $\Bclos$ and an input-insufficiency term $\Binsuf$
($B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$). On experimental infinite-dilution activity data, in the
VT-2005-matchable low-$\gamma$ (298\,K) regime, the closure is the binding term.
Because $\Binsuf$ is bounded from $(m,\zstar)$ alone---independently of the closure
convention---the total error already forces
$\Bclos\ge\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}\approx0.9>0.62\ge\Binsuf$ under the
model's \emph{deployed} residual-only convention; under the full COSMO-SAC
convention this sharpens to an assumption-free constant-offset bound
$\Bclos\ge0.72>\Binsuf$ ($\le0.62$), with solvent-clustered confidence
$\approx0.85$. The convention-robust corroboration is that restoring the physically
more complete combinatorial term \emph{enlarges} the error---what a misspecified map
does, not what missing information would---an effect we verify is robust to the
molecular-volume basis (recomputed on the true COSMO cavity volumes). Label noise is
unestimable in this replicate-free set and assumed zero; the load-bearing bounds are
invariant to zero-mean noise. A flexible regressor of the activity target on $\zstar$
fits it about $4\times$ better than the fixed true-input closure, placing the closure
well above the irreducible-variance floor. The ceiling also resists a
downstream fix, since a post-hoc output-space recalibration restores
\emph{calibration} without restoring \emph{accuracy}. We claim no new theory: the
non-identifiability of the split and the closure/inputs decomposition are read
against existing results on model discrepancy, sloppiness, and calibration. What
the paper offers is a reproducible way to \emph{measure where a physics prior's
ceiling lies}, and for this system an account of why more physics did not help.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% Frame: the field's default is "add physics / ground on real inputs -> better OOD".
% We show a clean counterexample and a measurement that says why.
Coupling a graph encoder to an explicit thermodynamic bottleneck is a common
recipe for out-of-distribution solubility prediction: the model must route its
prediction through interpretable physical factors, and each factor can in
principle be grounded on abundant single-component data that a black-box
predictor cannot exploit. The expectation is that the closer the model's physical
inputs are to the truth, the better it should predict. On the activity branch
this expectation fails. We characterize the failure quantitatively, and it is the
characterization, more than the failure itself, that this paper is about. That true
$\sigma$-profiles can hurt a COSMO-SAC pipeline will not surprise a thermodynamics
specialist who knows the model's limits; the contribution is not the failure but an
externally grounded procedure---demonstrated here on one closure and one low-$\gamma$
regime---that \emph{measures} where such a physics prior's ceiling lies and attributes
it to the closure rather than the inputs. Whether the finding of closure-dominance
generalizes beyond this regime we leave as a conjecture (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The grounding paradox (empirical, triple-confirmed).}
  Substituting true VT-2005 $\sigma$-profiles into the differentiable COSMO-SAC
  closure degrades activity ($\lngi$) and solubility ($\lnx$) prediction relative
  to a free learned activity head, the opposite of the intended benefit
  (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).
  \item \textbf{A measurement affordance, not a theorem.} Because the physical
  latent $\zstar$ (the true $\sigma$-profile) has external ground truth, the
  true-input excess risk splits \emph{exactly} as $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$ with no model
  fit (Prop.~\ref{prop:decomp}). The decomposition and its estimators are textbook;
  the new element is exploiting an externally measured latent to bound the split
  empirically. On the convention-robust evidence---a convention-independent bound
  and the more-physics-worse effect---$\Bclos$ (misspecified decoder) exceeds
  $\Binsuf$ (input insufficiency): the ceiling is the closure
  (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{A negative actionability result (Gate B).} A post-hoc
  output-space residual placed on the physics prediction recovers calibration but
  not accuracy; the closure binding cannot be undone downstream
  (\S\ref{sec:gateb}).
  \item \textbf{Framework and honest scoping.} We locate the split's structural
  non-identifiability (why a free head compensates; Prop.~\ref{prop:ident}),
  demote all theory to cited results on model discrepancy and calibration, and
  ship reproducible diagnostics. No new theorem is claimed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}
For a solute~$s$ in solvent~$v$ at temperature~$T$, the target is $y=\lnx$, the
log saturated mole fraction. Solid--liquid equilibrium gives
\begin{equation}
  \lnx = -\underbrace{\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\Big(\tfrac1T-\tfrac1\Tm\Big)}_{\Phicry(T)\ \text{(crystal, depends on $s$)}}
         \;-\;\underbrace{\lng(x_2,T)}_{\text{activity, depends on }(s,v)} ,
  \label{eq:sle}
\end{equation}
with the constant-$\Delta C_p$ correction omitted for clarity. The activity term
is produced by a \emph{differentiable closure} $g$: a graph encoder predicts a
$\sigma$-profile for each molecule, and COSMO-SAC maps the pair of profiles to
$\lng$. Write $\zstar$ for the \emph{true} physical latent, the externally
measured $\sigma$-profile (VT-2005 database), and $g(\zstar)$ for the closure's
output when fed the true profile. The model's own head instead predicts a
\emph{learned} profile $\hat z$ and evaluates $g(\hat z)$; a matched
\emph{DirectGNN} control shares the encoder but emits $\lnx$ with no closure and
is the reference for ``what the physics bottleneck costs'' (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figs/fig_architecture.pdf}
\caption{The model and the grounding intervention. Solute and solvent graphs pass
through a shared encoder to a crystal head (giving the ideal term $\Phi(T)$) and a
$\sigma$-profile head; the predicted profile $\hat z$ feeds the COSMO-SAC closure
$g$, which returns $\ln\gamma_2$, and an SLE solver combines the two into $\lnx$.
The $\sigma$-oracle replaces $\hat z$ with the true VT-2005 profile $\zstar$
(salmon). DirectGNN shares the encoder but bypasses the closure.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The grounding intervention.} Feeding $\zstar$ in place of $\hat z$
(a $\sigma$-oracle) isolates the closure from encoder error: if the closure were
well specified, $g(\zstar)$ should be the best available activity estimate. The
paradox is that it is not.

\paragraph{Data.} Solubility measurements are a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split of
BigSolDB \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb}, so test solutes are unseen at training; the
activity labels $m$ are experimental infinite-dilution coefficients $\lngi$
compiled from ThermoML, and the true $\sigma$-profiles $\zstar$ are the VT-2005
database.

\section{Theory: locating a physics prior's ceiling}
\label{sec:framework}

\subsection{Why a free head can compensate: structural non-identifiability}
The crystal/activity split is not identifiable from solubility data alone, which
is precisely why an unconstrained head can silently absorb crystal error into the
activity term.
\begin{proposition}[Structural non-identifiability of the SLE split]\label{prop:ident}
If the activity model carries a free $1/T$ term (as NRTL / van't~Hoff
temperature dependence does), then with $\Delta C_p=0$ the crystal sensitivity
$\partial\mu/\partial\dHfus$ lies in the span of the activity design columns
$(\mathbf 1,\,1/T)$; the map $\theta\mapsto\{\mu(T_k)\}_k$ is rank-deficient in the
crystal/activity directions and the Fisher information for $\dHfus$ vanishes. The
deficiency is closed only by rank-completing external constraints on a factor
(a direct crystal label, or fixing the activity slope).
\end{proposition}
\noindent This is the standard structural-identifiability picture for a
collinear design \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}; we state it in the SLE form and
audit it numerically in \S\ref{sec:supporting}. It is \emph{enabling background},
not our headline: it explains why grounding an under-determined factor on
external single-component data is necessary, and why compensation between the
factors is expected. By itself it says nothing about whether grounding helps.
That depends on whether the closure that consumes the grounded inputs is
correctly specified, which is the question we make measurable next.

The non-identifiability is not binary but graded by how much of the crystal
direction the activity class can absorb.
\begin{proposition}[Class-dependent information]\label{prop:semiparam}
Let the activity nuisance lie in a class $\mathcal H$ with tangent projection
$\Pi_{\mathcal H}$, and let $v=\partial\mu/\partial\dHfus$ be the crystal score.
The efficient information for $\dHfus$ is
$\mathcal I_{\mathrm{eff}}(\dHfus)=\sigma^{-2}\,\lVert v-\Pi_{\mathcal H}v\rVert^2$.
If $\mathcal H$ contains the crystal score's span $\{1,1/T\}$ (a free intercept and slope) then
$\Pi_{\mathcal H}v=v$ and $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{eff}}=0$, recovering
Prop.~\ref{prop:ident}; if $\mathcal H$ is restricted (the slope is known or
constrained) then $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{eff}}>0$ and the Cram\'er--Rao bound on
$\dHfus$ is finite.
\end{proposition}
\noindent The proof is the efficient-score construction: project the crystal
score onto the orthogonal complement of the nuisance tangent space; the squared
norm of the residual is the effective information. The numerical audit of
\S\ref{sec:ident} quantifies both regimes.

\subsection{The closure--insufficiency decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}
Let $m$ be the activity target (experimental $\lngi$) and $g(\zstar)$ the closure
evaluated on the true latent. The excess risk of the true-input path over the
Bayes predictor decomposes exactly.
\begin{proposition}[Measurement affordance: $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$]\label{prop:decomp}
For a \emph{fixed} (non-fitted) closure $g$,
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(m-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\text{MSE of the true-input path}}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[\Var(m\mid \zstar)\big]}_{\Binsuf\ \text{(inputs cannot resolve)}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\Bclos\ \text{(decoder mis-map)}} .
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The cross term vanishes identically because $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar)$ is
$\zstar$-measurable and $\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid\zstar])\mid\zstar]=0$. Both components
are separately \emph{estimable from data with no model fit}, because $\zstar$ is
externally observed.
\end{proposition}
\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is a bias--variance split of model
discrepancy in the sense of Kennedy--O'Hagan \cite{kennedy2001} and
Brynjarsd\'ottir--O'Hagan \cite{brynjarsdottir2014}: $\Bclos$ is the systematic
discrepancy of a structurally fixed physical map, and its dominance over
$\Binsuf$ is the empirical content of ``the ceiling is the closure, not the
inputs.'' We do not claim \eqref{eq:decomp} as new; the affordance is that the
external groundedness of $\zstar$ lets us \emph{measure} the split rather than
assume it. \textbf{The exactness is contingent on $g$ being fixed}: for a
\emph{fitted} decoder the cross term does not vanish and a plug-in point
decomposition is invalid (we observe a spurious $-0.36$ cross term when
$\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ is replaced by a fitted estimate). We therefore report
\emph{one-sided bounds}, not a point split:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Bclos \ge (\Ex[m]-\Ex[g])^2$, \emph{assumption-free in smoothness}
  (Jensen; a constant decoder offset, which cannot be input insufficiency)---it does
  assume $m$ and $g$ are on the same $\lngi$ reference state, which we check directly
  (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item $\Binsuf \le \Ex[\Var(m\mid \mathrm{bin}(g(\zstar)))]$, a valid
  \emph{upper} bound (law of total variance, since a binning of $g(\zstar)$
  coarsens $\zstar$).
\end{itemize}
Because $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ depends only on $(m,\zstar)$, it is
\emph{independent of the closure convention}: any disagreement between closure
variants is decoder disagreement and lands in $\Bclos$.

\subsection{Structured temperature extrapolation}
\begin{proposition}[Bounded extrapolation for the van't Hoff class]\label{prop:temp}
For same-pair extrapolation to a temperature $T^\star$ outside the training range
$[T_{\min},T_{\max}]$, the structured activity class that is affine in $1/T$ has
extrapolation error bounded by the fitted-slope variance times
$\lvert 1/T^\star-\overline{1/T}\rvert$, whereas an unstructured temperature
encoder is unconstrained beyond its training support.
\end{proposition}
\noindent Within the affine-in-$1/T$ family the prediction at $T^\star$ is an
affine extrapolation whose error is controlled by the bounded variance of the
fitted slope and intercept; no such constraint binds a free function approximator
outside its support. This is the one place the physical structure is expected to
help, and \S\ref{sec:supporting} reports the class-level margin.

\subsection{The geometry of compensation}
\begin{proposition}[Vanishing information cost at narrow $\Delta T$]\label{prop:geom}
The smallest eigenvalue of the Fisher metric in the crystal/activity plane tends
to zero as the training temperature window $\Delta T\to0$; compensating moves along
the resulting near-flat direction incur vanishing information cost.
\end{proposition}
\noindent As the temperatures collapse, $1/T$ becomes nearly constant, the crystal
and activity design columns become collinear, and the smallest Fisher eigenvalue
tends to zero (the $\Delta T$ sweep of \S\ref{sec:ident} shows the conditioning
reaching $10^{16}$ at $\Delta T=10$\,K). This is why an optimizer prefers to split
error between the two factors rather than fit either one correctly.

\subsection{A-optimal auxiliary weighting}
\begin{proposition}[Trace-optimal auxiliary weights]\label{prop:aopt}
Among nonnegative auxiliary-loss weights under a budget, the trace-optimal choice
places positive weight on every loss that adds information to a rank-deficient
direction; under the block-diagonal head structure enforced by stop-gradient this
selects exactly the crystal, activity-level, and activity-shape signals.
\end{proposition}
\noindent This is the design rule behind the auxiliary single-component streams:
each stream rank-completes a different deficient direction of
Prop.~\ref{prop:ident}. The rule prescribes which streams to add; it does not
promise that they improve end-task accuracy, and \S\ref{sec:e2} reports a
dose-limited case where a stream did not.

\subsection{What we do not claim}
There is no ``optimal grounding'' theorem here, and in particular no
necessary-and-sufficient condition for the decomposition to improve
out-of-distribution accuracy: the honest empirical answer (\S\ref{sec:results})
is that it does not, and the propositions above serve to locate why. A prior
search over candidate theorems returned nothing not already subsumed by known
results on model
discrepancy \cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}, parameter sloppiness
\cite{transtrum2015,gutenkunst2007}, structural and practical identifiability
\cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}, estimation under misspecification
\cite{white1982}, and underspecification \cite{damour2022}. The
calibration-versus-accuracy separation of \S\ref{sec:gateb} is the classical
refinement decomposition of a probability forecast \cite{murphy1973,degroot1983}.
We use these as the interpretive frame and keep the paper's own claims
measurement-first.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{The grounding paradox}
\label{sec:paradox}
% Artifacts: results/e5_sigma_grounding/seed_{42,43,44}/comparison.json (3-seed e5 lock, n=5608); aggregate THREE_SEED_SUMMARY.md via run_e5_comparison.py.
% Triple confirmation: eval-oracle / true-at-train / channel-swap.
Feeding true $\sigma$-profiles into the closure degrades it rather than improving
it, and it does so in both the activity and the solubility target. On the activity
target ($\lngi$, $n{=}60$ pairs with both true profiles present), a flexible
regressor of $\lngi$ on the true profiles---our irreducible-variance ($\Binsuf$)
estimator---attains MSE $\approx0.40$, whereas the fixed closure on the \emph{same}
true profiles attains MSE $1.47$--$1.80$ depending on convention (\S\ref{sec:measure}):
about $4\times$ above the irreducible-variance floor, and the true-input closure
under-predicts $\lngi$ with a systematic downward bias (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}).
In the solubility target the
effect is large enough to erase any positive $R^2$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox}, three seeds, mean$\pm$sd, $n{=}5608$): the model predicts
solubility \emph{better with its own learned $\sigma$-profile}
(grounded MAE $1.85\pm0.05$, $R^2=0.33\pm0.05$) than when \emph{given the true measured one}
(the $\sigma$-oracle, MAE $2.25\pm0.02$, $R^2=-0.03\pm0.04$, no better than predicting the
mean; one of three seeds is marginally positive). The robust form of the effect is the
MAE gap ($2.25$ vs $1.85$); the near-zero oracle $R^2$ shows the true input carries no
usable solubility signal through this closure. Adding the physically \emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim
combinatorial term also increases error (activity MSE $1.47\!\to\!1.80$;
solubility MAE $1.85\!\to\!1.88$), which is what a misspecified map does and what
missing information would not. We confirm the paradox three independent ways: the
evaluation-time oracle above, injecting the true profile \emph{at training}
(single-seed MAE $1.98$, still worse than the learned-$\sigma$ $1.80$), and a
channel-swap control (MAE $2.08$), ruling out a train/test wiring artifact.

\paragraph{The load-bearing separation (convention-independent, Tier~1).} Because
$\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ depends only on $(m,\zstar)$, its upper bound is the
same in every closure convention; subtracting it from the total therefore lower-bounds
$\Bclos$ even for the model's \emph{deployed} residual-only convention:
$\Bclos\ge\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{res}}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}\approx1.47-0.62=0.85$ (and
$0.91$ against the RF bound), well above $\Binsuf\le0.62$. This is the claim we lean
on, and it needs no smoothness assumption beyond the validity of the $\Binsuf$ upper
bound. Under the full COSMO-SAC convention the separation \emph{sharpens} to an
assumption-free constant-offset bound: a constant decoder offset alone accounts for
$\Bclos\ge0.72$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}), exceeding every $\Binsuf$ upper bound
($\le0.62$), and subtracting the insufficiency bound gives $\Bclos\ge1.24>0.62$
($\sim2\times$). That constant-offset bound is convention-specific and \emph{inverts}
under the deployed residual-only default ($0.43<0.62$; hedge~1), which is exactly why
we headline the convention-independent bound above rather than the constant offset.
Label noise is unestimable in this replicate-free set and assumed zero; the bounds we
rely on are invariant to zero-mean noise, so the ceiling is not label noise either.

\paragraph{Convention-robust corroboration (Tier~2).} The genuinely
convention-robust fact is that adding the physically \emph{more} complete
Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term \emph{increases} error (activity MSE
$1.47\to1.80$)---what a misspecified map does, not what missing information would.
This is robust to the size-factor's volume basis: recomputing the combinatorial term
on the true COSMO cavity volumes (VT-2005 $V_{\mathrm{cosmo}}$) rather than an RDKit
molar volume leaves it essentially unchanged (Jensen $0.714\to0.717$, MSE
$1.793\to1.800$), because the term depends on molecular-size \emph{ratios} in which a
near-uniform volume rescaling cancels. A second observation is consistent but is
\emph{not} independent of the decomposition: a flexible regressor of $m$ on $\zstar$
fits the activity target $\sim$4$\times$ better than the fixed true-input closure---but
that regressor is exactly our convention-independent $\Binsuf$ estimator
($\approx0.4$), so ``$4\times$ better'' restates that the closure sits far above the
irreducible-variance floor (that $\Bclos$ is large) rather than corroborating it by an
independent route. We therefore lean on the dimension-robust random-forest / ridge
out-of-fold and binning bounds and on the combinatorial effect, which agree that
$\Bclos$ exceeds $\Binsuf$.

\paragraph{Four hedges we disclose.} The adversarial audit (four independent
recomputations) could not invert the verdict but sharpened its scope:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Convention.} The deployed default is residual-only. Under it the
  \emph{constant-offset} (Jensen) lower bound inverts
  ($\Bclos\ge0.43<\Binsuf\le0.62$), so that particular assumption-free bound does not
  separate, and the $\sim2\times$ full-convention margin (the sharpened
  $\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf$ bound, $1.24$ vs $0.62$) is a full-COSMO-SAC-only result. What separates under the deployed convention is the convention-independent
  bound $\Bclos\ge\mathrm{MSE}_{\mathrm{res}}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}\approx0.91>0.62$,
  on which we headline, together with the combinatorial Tier~2 fact---so the headline
  does not rest on the arm-specific constant-offset arithmetic.
  \item \emph{Confound.} The closure error is predominantly a \emph{between-solvent}
  systematic bias ($\sim$64\% of total error); within the largest single-solvent
  stratum (pyridine, $n{=}20$) the closure is nearly correct (stratum MSE $0.07$).
  Because solvent $\sigma$ is part of $\zstar$, these offsets are still decoder error
  and give an independent solvent-conditional lower bound $\Bclos\ge0.89$ (full)/$0.56$ (res).
  \item \emph{Confidence.} Honest confidence on the full-convention constant-offset
  separation is the solvent-clustered bootstrap $P(\Bclos{>}\Binsuf)\approx0.85$
  ($0.83$ against the random-forest upper bound, $0.89$ against the
  law-of-total-variance bound); we report the solvent-clustered scheme rather than
  the less conservative i.i.d.-pair one because solvent dependence dominates. The
  convention-independent margin ($0.91$ vs $0.62$) is reported as a point estimate
  and is the direction we headline. The decomposition is checkpoint/seed-independent
  (the closure has zero learnable parameters).
  \item \emph{Selection/scope.} The 60 matchable pairs are the low-to-moderate
  $\gamma$ corner (matched mean $\lngi=0.75$ vs $3.56$ for VT-2005-unmatchable
  pairs, $t=-7.44$; the strongly non-ideal amide-solvent regime is untested
  because VT-2005 lacks those profiles). This narrows scope but is
  \emph{conservative}, since the untested regime is a harder closure test. The
  target is $\lngi$ at 298\,K, so the numeric ceiling is not claimed to transfer
  unchanged to the finite-composition solubility regime.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The ceiling is not fixable downstream (Gate B)}
\label{sec:gateb}
% Artifacts: results/e5_sigma_grounding/seed_42/grounded_a_truetrain_residual{,_v2}_predictions.summary.json (Gate B v1 gated-shut / v2 forced-open); model.py correction_force_open_gate.
If the closure binding were merely a bad point estimate, a learned output-space
correction on top of the physics prediction should recover accuracy. It does not.
An additive bounded residual with a learned confidence gate
($\hat y=\hat y_{\text{phys}}+(1-c)\,r$, with $y=\lnx$) leaves the valve
effectively shut (applied
residual $\approx0$, $R^2=0.236$). Forcing the gate open removes the systematic
\emph{bias} (from $+0.45$ to $+0.06$) but does \emph{not} recover \emph{accuracy}
($R^2$ $0.283\!\to\!0.274$). This is the classical calibration-versus-accuracy
separation: when the upstream map is misspecified, a monotone or affine post-hoc
map can fix reliability but not resolution. Recalibration buys calibration
without buying accuracy, which is what we expect if the closure, rather than the
estimate, is the binding constraint.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The result is a cautionary measurement for physics-informed modeling: grounding a
model on truer physical inputs is only as good as the closure that consumes them,
and when that closure is a fixed, mildly misspecified map, better inputs surface
its bias rather than help. The external latent does not make the physics path win. What it buys is
\emph{attribution}: on the infinite-dilution, low-$\gamma$ subset we can measure, the
ceiling sits in the decoder ($\Bclos$), with the inputs ($\Binsuf$) ruled out as its
source and label noise unestimable but assumed zero (and unable to manufacture the
constant offset the bounds rest on). We do not claim this numeric attribution transfers
unchanged to the finite-composition, high-$\gamma$ regime the solubility paradox itself
spans; that the solubility paradox shares the same closure-misspecification cause is a
conjecture supported by in-regime signs---the more-physics-worse effect on the full
$n{=}5608$ set and the chemistry map---not a measured fact. For practice this relocates the lever. Within this closure family, effort
spent on richer inputs or a better encoder is mis-targeted; the payoff is in the
closure itself, for example a corrected or learned residual on the map rather
than on its output (which \S\ref{sec:gateb} shows is inert).

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~$\Binsuf$ is bounded, never point-identified: there
are no true replicate measurements at fixed $\zstar$, so the insufficiency estimates
lean on a smoothness assumption. The constant-offset (Jensen) bound is smoothness-free
but assumes $m$ and $g$ share the $\lngi$ (mole-fraction, Lewis--Randall) reference
state; we check this two ways---the closure is nearly exact within the largest
single-solvent stratum (pyridine, MSE $0.07$), which a uniform reference-state offset
could not produce, and an independent recomputation reproduces the closure output---so
the mean offset is genuine decoder bias, not a units artifact. We headline the
convention-independent bound (\S\ref{sec:measure}) as the load-bearing claim.
(ii)~The assumption-free constant-offset margin is convention-specific (full COSMO-SAC)
and \emph{inverts} under the deployed residual-only default; there the separation rests
on the convention-independent bound $\Bclos\ge\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ and
the more-physics-worse effect (which we verified is robust to the molecular-volume
basis). (iii)~The measurement is at infinite
dilution and 298\,K on the VT-2005-matchable, low-$\gamma$ subset; the non-ideal
amide-solvent regime is untested. (iv)~Absolute scaffold $\lnx$ accuracy is
encoder-bound and near the aleatoric label-noise floor; this paper is not an
accuracy claim.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Feeding a physics-informed solubility model its true physical inputs made it
worse. What the paper contributes is a way to measure that failure: with an
externally grounded latent, the true-input excess risk splits into a
decoder-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term, and on the
infinite-dilution, low-$\gamma$ subset we can measure, the ceiling turns out to be
the closure rather than the inputs (label noise unestimable and assumed zero). The
practical implication is to fix the map itself, since within this closure truer
inputs surface its bias and an output-space correction is inert.
