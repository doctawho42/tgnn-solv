\title{When Reference Physical Inputs Hurt a Learned Solubility Model:\\
The Latent Becomes a Compensating Surrogate}
\author{N.~L. Polomoshnov\thanks{\pending[affiliation and full postal address]. E-mail: \pending[corresponding-author email]} \and \pending[co-author names and affiliations]}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Feeding a graph network's own learned, imperfect $\sigma$-profiles through a fixed COSMO-SAC
closure predicts solid--liquid solubility better than feeding external quantum-chemical reference
profiles (VT-2005) through the same closure---by $+0.13$ in $\ln x_2$ MAE when the model co-adapts to
the reference input (reference $\sigma$ injected at training, $1.85\!\to\!1.98$) and by $+0.40$ when
it is swapped in only at evaluation ($\to\!2.25$, maximally exposing the drift); the
reference-input arm's $R^2$ falls from $+0.33$ to $\approx0$ ($-0.03\pm0.04$), corroborating the MAE. This grounding paradox is robust
across three seeds ($n{=}5608$) and three injection controls. Mechanistically, end-to-end training drives the
``physical'' latent into a low-rank, out-of-sample-transferable surrogate that cancels the closure's
error rather than reporting the physical quantity (three seeds, mean$\pm$sd: $51\pm2\%$ departure,
$72\pm1\%$ in two modes, $4.1\pm0.5\times$ transfer).
Because the latent carries an external reference, we can \emph{measure} rather than assume where a
fixed closure's ceiling lies: an exact, fit-free split of the reference-input excess risk into
closure-misspecification and input-insufficiency terms. On a small matched corner it places the
ceiling in the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 closure, not its inputs, and a modern closure
(COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) removes the penalty---closure fidelity, not input fidelity, is the lever. That
corner is at infinite dilution, not the finite-composition regime the paradox spans, so we read the
attribution as a demonstration, not a certificate. A second closure, $\mathrm{p}K_a$ through a
Hammett relationship, reproduces the sign flip. We make no accuracy claim; the contribution is the
paradox, its compensating-surrogate mechanism, and the lesson that the lever is closure fidelity, not
truer inputs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% Frame: the field's default is "add physics / ground on real inputs -> better OOD".
% We show a clean counterexample and a measurement that says why.
Coupling a graph encoder to an explicit thermodynamic bottleneck is a common recipe for
out-of-distribution solubility prediction: the model routes its prediction through interpretable
physical factors, each groundable on abundant single-component data a black box cannot exploit. The
expectation is that the closer the physical inputs are to the truth, the better the model predicts. On
the activity branch this fails: feeding our model the external quantum-chemical reference
$\sigma$-profile makes it \emph{worse}. The reason we dwell on this failure is what its \emph{cause}
reveals. Because the latent has an external reference, we can \emph{measure} where the ceiling lies and
attribute it to the closure rather than the inputs (impossible for a fitted latent, which has no
oracle), and hold the learned latent against its reference---which shows that, trained end-to-end
through a misspecified closure, the latent is driven off the physical quantity into a systematic,
low-rank, transferable compensating surrogate (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}). Holding the reference input
fixed, a higher-fidelity closure removes the penalty: closure fidelity, not input fidelity, is the
lever (\S\ref{sec:measure}). The sign is not universal---a supervised-$\sigma$ model reports the
opposite---so whether grounding helps is shaped by two axes, closure fidelity and latent supervision,
which we anchor with three real closures and a synthetic dial; the composed-predictor framing
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) and physics-tax sign rule give the formal account, and the pKa closure
(\S\ref{sec:pka}) generalises it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our thesis is that whether grounding a learned latent in reference physics helps or hurts is \emph{shaped
by} two axes---how the latent is trained (supervised toward truth vs.\ shaped end-to-end through the
fixed closure) and the closure's fidelity. Three findings stand cleanly---the paradox, a reusable
measurement instrument, and the \emph{structure} of the compensating-surrogate mechanism (its sign
and low-rank, transferable form robust; its magnitude a three-seed estimate, mean$\pm$sd); the
closure-vs-inputs attribution and the two-axis map we present as, respectively, a caveated corner
measurement and a \emph{conceptual} organizing frame---not a measured phase diagram, since only the
closure-fidelity axis is swept directly (\S\ref{sec:dial}); the supervision axis rests on our own
warmup-manifold point and a literature anchor.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The grounding paradox.} Substituting reference VT-2005 $\sigma$-profiles into the
  fixed COSMO-SAC closure degrades $\lngi$ and $\lnx$ prediction relative to a free learned head---the
  opposite of the intended benefit ($3$ seeds, $n{=}5608$, three injection controls; \S\ref{sec:paradox}).
  \item \textbf{A measurement instrument.} Because the latent $\zstar$ (the reference $\sigma$-profile)
  has external ground truth, the reference-input excess risk splits \emph{exactly}, with no model fit,
  as $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$ (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}); on the matched corner the closure term binds
  (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{The mechanism where grounding hurts.} The end-to-end ``physical'' latent becomes a
  low-rank, out-of-sample-transferable closure-\emph{compensating} surrogate, not the physical
  quantity---why grounding hurts here, and why a supervised-$\sigma$ model finds the opposite
  (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}).
  \item \textbf{Closure fidelity is the lever.} Holding the reference input fixed, swapping the
  low-fidelity 2002 closure for 2010 removes the penalty ($1.26\!\to\!0.77$, insufficiency-free): the
  ceiling is the closure, and the fix is a better closure, not truer inputs (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{Not fixable downstream.} A post-hoc output residual recovers calibration but not
  accuracy, so the closure binding cannot be undone downstream---a \emph{structural} ceiling within
  this closure family (\S\ref{sec:gateb},~\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item \textbf{A general account.} The method holds for any composed predictor $\hat y=g(h(x))$ with a
  fixed closure over a learned intermediate; two structural lemmas are in the main text, the
  grounding-gap identity and physics-tax sign rule in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}. It transfers to a
  synthetic closure dial (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and a second real closure---pKa through a Hammett
  relationship, where the same oracle swap flips sign across strata (\S\ref{sec:pka}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}
For a solute~$s$ in solvent~$v$ at temperature~$T$, the target is $y=\lnx$, the
log saturated mole fraction. Dissolving a solid trades two costs against each
other---breaking the crystal lattice ($\Phicry$) and mixing the freed molecules
against non-ideal solute--solvent interactions ($\lng$)---and solid--liquid
equilibrium (SLE) sets the log-solubility to their negative sum:
\begin{equation}
  \lnx = -\underbrace{\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\Big(\tfrac1T-\tfrac1\Tm\Big)}_{\Phicry(T)\ \text{(crystal, depends on $s$)}}
         \;-\;\underbrace{\lng(x_2,T)}_{\text{activity, depends on }(s,v)} ,
  \label{eq:sle}
\end{equation}
with the constant-$\Delta C_p$ (heat-capacity-of-fusion) correction omitted for
clarity. The activity term is produced by a \emph{differentiable closure} $g$:
a graph encoder predicts a $\sigma$-profile---the distribution of screening
(surface) charge density over a molecule, the histogram a COSMO model
consumes---for each molecule, and COSMO-SAC (the COnductor-like Screening
Model--Segment Activity Coefficient \cite{lin2002}) maps the pair of profiles to
$\lng$. Write $\zstar$ for the \emph{reference} physical latent, the externally referenced
$\sigma$-profile from the VT-2005 database (the Virginia Tech 2005 compilation of
quantum-chemically computed screening-charge profiles for ${\sim}1{,}400$
compounds \cite{mullins2006}), and $g(\zstar)$ for the closure output when fed the
reference profile. The model's own head instead predicts a \emph{learned} profile
$\hat z$ and evaluates $g(\hat z)$; a matched \emph{DirectGNN} control shares the
encoder but emits $\lnx$ with no closure, and is the reference for ``what the
physics bottleneck costs'' (schematic in SI, Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\paragraph{The oracle substitution.} Feeding $\zstar$ in place of $\hat z$
(a $\sigma$-oracle) isolates the closure from encoder error: if the closure were
well specified, $g(\zstar)$ should be the best available activity estimate. The
paradox is that it is not.

\paragraph{Data.} Solubility measurements are a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split of
BigSolDB \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb} (grouping molecules by ring-system-plus-linker
scaffold, side chains stripped), so test solutes share no scaffold with training;
the activity labels $m$ are experimental infinite-dilution activity coefficients
($\lngi$, IDAC) compiled from ThermoML (the NIST/IUPAC archive of experimental
thermophysical data \cite{frenkel2011thermoml}), and the reference $\sigma$-profiles
$\zstar$ are the VT-2005 database.

\section{Theory: locating a physics prior's ceiling in a composed predictor}
\label{sec:framework}

The results below are stated for a general \emph{composed predictor} $\hat y=g(h(x))$
in which a fixed, non-fitted map $g$---the \emph{closure}---consumes a learned physical
intermediate $z=h(x)$, and are compared against a matched free predictor that shares the
encoder but drops $g$. Solubility, with $g$ the COSMO-SAC closure and $z$ the
$\sigma$-profile, is one instance; \S\ref{sec:pka} gives a second (pKa through a fixed
Hammett closure), and \S\ref{sec:dial} a controlled synthetic family. Nothing in
Lemmas~\ref{prop:ident}--\ref{prop:decomp}, Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, or
Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} is specific to COSMO-SAC. Two quantities organize the section:
the closure/insufficiency split $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$, which is the assumption-light
\emph{instrument}, and the observable grounding gap $\Gamma$, which is a \emph{symptom} of
closure misspecification whose validity we characterize.

\subsection{Why a free head can compensate: structural non-identifiability}
The crystal/activity split is not identifiable from solubility data alone, which
is precisely why an unconstrained head can silently absorb crystal error into the
activity term.
\begin{lemma}[Structural non-identifiability of the SLE split]\label{prop:ident}
Consider the infinite-dilution SLE observable $\mu(T)=-\Phicry(T)-\lngi(T)$ with
$\Delta C_p=0$, so that $\Phicry(T)=(\dHfus/\Rgas)(1/T-1/\Tm)$ is affine in $u=1/T$,
and a symmetric two-suffix activity class ($\lng=\lngi\,(1-x_2)^2$) in which
$\lngi(T)$ is affine in $u$. Then, for any design with at least two distinct
temperatures, $\mu$ depends on the parameters only through the two-dimensional
statistic (its slope and intercept in $u$), and the activity intercept and slope
already span that plane;
hence the crystal parameters $(\dHfus,\Tm)$ are unidentified, the profiled Fisher
information for $\dHfus$ is exactly zero, and the indeterminacy set is
two-dimensional. The deficiency is closed only by a rank-completing external
constraint (a direct crystal label, or a fixed activity slope). At finite dilution
the composition factor $(1-x_2)^2$ breaks the exact affinity, leaving a non-zero but
near-degenerate information of order $x_2^2$ (numerically, condition number
$2.3\times10^{5}$; \S\ref{sec:ident}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent This is the standard structural-identifiability picture for a
collinear design \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}; we prove it in the SLE form
(App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}) and audit it numerically in \S\ref{sec:ident}. It is
\emph{enabling background},
not our headline: it explains why grounding an under-determined factor on
external single-component data is necessary, and why compensation between the
factors is expected. By itself it says nothing about whether grounding helps.
That depends on whether the closure that consumes the grounded inputs is
correctly specified, which is the question we make measurable next.

The non-identifiability is not binary but graded by the activity class: the efficient
information for $\dHfus$ is zero when the activity nuisance spans the crystal score's directions
$\{1,1/T\}$ and strictly positive (a finite Cram\'er--Rao bound) when the slope is constrained
(Lemma~\ref{prop:semiparam}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). The numerical audit (\S\ref{sec:ident})
shows the honest corollary: merely restricting the activity slope leaves
$\mathrm{sd}(\dHfus)\approx8{,}251$\,J/mol---finite but unusable---whereas external crystal labels,
which contribute a score direction outside the activity tangent space, close it to
$\approx1{,}689$\,J/mol.

\subsection{The closure--insufficiency decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}
Let $m$ be the activity target (experimental $\lngi$) and $g(\zstar)$ the closure
evaluated on the true latent. The excess risk of the reference-input path over the
best-possible predictor $\Ex[m\mid\cdot]$ (the Bayes predictor) decomposes
exactly---into a part the intermediate cannot resolve and a part the fixed map gets
wrong.

\begin{lemma}[Exact closure--insufficiency decomposition and its one-sided bounds]\label{prop:decomp}
For a \emph{fixed} (non-fitted) closure $g$,
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(m-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\text{MSE of the reference-input path}}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[\Var(m\mid \zstar)\big]}_{\Binsuf\ \text{(inputs cannot resolve)}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\Bclos\ \text{(decoder mis-map)}} .
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The cross term vanishes identically because $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar)$ is
$\zstar$-measurable and $\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid\zstar])\mid\zstar]=0$. The split is exact with no fitting of the closure $g$---the cross term vanishes because $g$ is
fixed and $\zstar$ is externally observed---so the total and $\Bclos$'s Jensen lower bound are
fit-free; $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$, however, must be estimated with a conditional-variance
smoother whose choice affects the verdict (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is a bias--variance split of model
discrepancy in the sense of Kennedy--O'Hagan \cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}:
$\Bclos$ is the systematic discrepancy of a fixed physical map, and its dominance over
$\Binsuf$ is the empirical content of ``the ceiling is the closure, not the inputs.'' We
do not claim \eqref{eq:decomp} as new. The affordance is that an externally grounded
$\zstar$ lets us \emph{measure} the split rather than assume it: unlike
model-discrepancy calibration, which \emph{fits} a correction from held-out
observations, here $g$ is fixed and $\zstar$ externally observed, so the map's error is
\emph{attributed}, not corrected---available only where an external latent oracle exists
(LFERs, COSMO / group-contribution closures, thermodynamic cycles), not in gray-box or
PINN hybrids. \textbf{Exactness is contingent on $g$ being fixed}: for a \emph{fitted}
decoder the cross term does not vanish and a plug-in point split is invalid (a spurious
$-0.36$ cross term appears when $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ is replaced by a fitted estimate). We
therefore report \emph{one-sided bounds}, not a point split (each proved in
App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Bclos \ge (\Ex[m]-\Ex[g])^2$ by Jensen: a constant offset between the
  decoder's mean output and the target is misspecification, not input insufficiency, and
  this bound needs \emph{no smoothness assumption}. Its one requirement is that $m$ and $g$
  share the $\lngi$ reference state, which we check directly (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item $\Binsuf \le \Ex[\Var(m\mid \mathrm{bin}(g(\zstar)))]$, a valid
  \emph{upper} bound (law of total variance, since a binning of $g(\zstar)$
  coarsens $\zstar$).
\end{itemize}
Because $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ depends only on $(m,\zstar)$, it is
\emph{independent of the closure convention}: any disagreement between closure
variants is decoder disagreement and lands in $\Bclos$.

\paragraph{The instrument's resolution is $\dim(\zstar)/n$.} Whether the split can be
\emph{point}-identified or only bounded is set by the latent dimension relative to sample size.
$\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ is a conditional variance in $\dim(\zstar)$ dimensions, and
estimating it from $n$ observations is a nonparametric problem governed by $\dim(\zstar)/n$. On the
solubility corner $\zstar$ is a $102$-bin $\sigma$-profile with $n{=}60$ matched pairs
($\dim(\zstar)/n\approx1.7$): the conditional variance is unestimable, so every $\Binsuf$ entry of
Table~\ref{tab:decomp} is a one-sided \emph{bound} and $\Bclos$ is only lower-bounded---the
one-sidedness above is a consequence of this ratio, not a modelling choice. Where the latent is
low-dimensional the same instrument point-identifies: the pKa Hammett constant is a \emph{scalar}
with $n{=}1198$ ($\dim(\zstar)/n\approx10^{-3}$), so $\Binsuf$ is a point estimate (\S\ref{sec:pka}),
not a bound. The decomposition is identifiable exactly where $\dim(\zstar)/n$ is small---which is why
the pKa domain, not the solubility keystone, is where the split is pinned rather than bounded.

\subsection{The grounding gap: a symptom of closure misspecification}
\label{sec:gap}
The paradox---true inputs hurting---is a measurable quantity, not a curiosity. Let
$\mathcal E$ be the encoder class ($h:x\mapsto z$) and define three population risks of
the activity target $m$: the oracle risk $R_{\mathrm{orc}}=\Ex[(m-g(\zstar))^2]$, the best
end-to-end risk $R_{\mathrm{e2e}}=\inf_{h\in\mathcal E}\Ex[(m-g(h(x)))^2]$, and the
free-predictor (black-box) risk $R_{\mathrm{free}}=\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid x])^2]$. The
\emph{grounding gap} is $\Gamma:=R_{\mathrm{orc}}-R_{\mathrm{e2e}}$; the paradox is
$\Gamma>0$ (a free learned head beats the true input pushed through the closure). Under
(A1) a representable encoder ($\phi^\star\in\mathcal E$ with $\zstar=\phi^\star(x)$) and
(A2) a lossless bottleneck ($\Ex[m\mid x]=\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$), the gap is sandwiched,
$0\le\Gamma\le\Bclos$ with $\Gamma=\Bclos-(R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{free}})$, so a
well-specified closure ($\Bclos=0$) forces $\Gamma=0$ and, under A1--A2, an observed
$\Gamma>0$ certifies $\Bclos>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). Then
$\Gamma$ is exactly the closure bias the encoder can \emph{absorb} by feeding $g$ a
wrong-but-useful intermediate---the formal content of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}'s ``a free
head silently compensates.''

\paragraph{$\Gamma$ is a symptom, not a certificate.} Both assumptions are load-bearing and
neither holds cleanly here. A1 is \emph{empirically doubtful}: the encoder is transfer-limited (a linear probe onto
descriptors drops from train $R^2\approx0.76$ to test $R^2\approx0.45$ out of distribution, a proxy
for---not a direct measure of---representability of $\zstar$), making $\phi^\star\in\mathcal E$
unlikely across scaffolds, so effectively $\phi^\star\notin\mathcal E$ and even $\Gamma\ge0$
is not guaranteed. A2 is unverifiable without the oracle and physically dubious (the
$\sigma$-profile is a lossy summary of the graph). When A2 fails, $\Gamma>0$ \emph{conflates}
two causes: closure misspecification ($\Bclos>0$) and input insufficiency (a correct closure
driven off-support to recover information the intermediate discarded). A controlled construction
with a \emph{well-specified} closure and a lossy intermediate gives $\Bclos=0$ yet $\Gamma>0$
(\S\ref{sec:dial}, and the pKa ortho sweep of \S\ref{sec:pka}). Certification of
closure-dominance therefore rests on the convention-independent \emph{decomposition}
(Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}), which needs neither A1 nor A2: in solubility the Jensen bound and
the insufficiency estimates place $\Bclos$ above $\Binsuf$ \emph{directly}
(\S\ref{sec:measure}), and the grounding gap only corroborates it. This is the paper's method:
\emph{the decomposition is the instrument, $\Gamma$ is the cheap symptom that motivates it, and
separating its two causes is what an externally referenced latent buys.}

\subsection{The physics tax and the critical data budget}
\label{sec:tax}
The gap is a population statement; the deployed question is whether the \emph{trained} physics
model beats the \emph{trained} black box at sample size $n$. Writing each trained risk as an
asymptote plus estimation variance, the \emph{physics tax}
$\mathcal{T}(n)\approx\Delta_\infty+[V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]$ (with
$\Delta_\infty:=R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}$) is negative---physics helps---only
when the estimation variance a constrained prior saves exceeds its asymptotic bias; if
$\Delta_\infty>0$ and the variance gap vanishes there is a critical budget $n^\star$ beyond which
physics always loses, and as the control nears the aleatoric floor the tax tends to $\Delta_\infty$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:tax}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). When the control is asymptotically Bayes and
A2 holds, $\Delta_\infty=\Bclos-\Gamma$, the \emph{un-compensatable} part of the closure bias. The
novelty inversion---the tax heaviest on the \emph{best-covered} solutes (\S\ref{sec:map},
exploratory)---points in a direction the proposition allows, but is not a controlled test.

\paragraph{Three informal consequences.} The same collinear geometry has three further consequences we
use informally (details in the SI): within the affine-in-$1/T$ activity family, same-pair temperature
extrapolation is controlled by the fitted-slope variance---the one place the physical \emph{structure}
is expected to help (\S\ref{sec:supporting}, a hypothesis-class margin, not a claim for the trained
model, which trails the van't~Hoff baseline); as the training window $\Delta T\to0$ the crystal and
activity columns become collinear and the smallest Fisher eigenvalue $\to0$ (\S\ref{sec:ident}), so an
optimiser splits error between the factors rather than fit either; and each single-component grounding
stream rank-completes a different deficient direction of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident} (\S\ref{sec:e2}).

\subsection{What we prove}
Two lemmas carry the measurement: the split's non-identifiability (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}) and the
exact closure/insufficiency decomposition with its one-sided bounds (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}). The
class-dependence refinement, the grounding-gap sandwich (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}), and the physics-tax
sign rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) are in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}, all elementary. Their reach is
deliberate: Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} certifies misspecification only under (A1--A2), which fail or are
unverifiable here, so we rest on the assumption-light decomposition and read $\Gamma$ as a
corroborating symptom. There is no ``optimal grounding'' theorem, and the decomposition does not
improve out-of-distribution accuracy (\S\ref{sec:results}). We read these against known work on model
discrepancy \cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}, sloppiness \cite{transtrum2015,gutenkunst2007},
identifiability \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}, misspecification \cite{white1982}, and
underspecification \cite{damour2022}.

\subsection{The grounding paradox}
\label{sec:paradox}

Feeding reference $\sigma$-profiles into the closure degrades it rather than improving it,

We separate the load-bearing evidence---a bound that holds under \emph{any} COSMO-SAC
configuration---from corroborating checks that are robust across the conventions we tried but not
strictly independent of the model fit.

\paragraph{The load-bearing separation (leakage-immune).} Subtracting the leakage-immune
law-of-total-variance $\Binsuf$ upper bound (the most conservative estimator; the $z^\ast$-neighbour
$k$-NN/RF estimators are deflated by near-duplicate leakage on the crossed $41$-solute$\times17$-solvent
design and only \emph{bracket} $\Binsuf$) from the total error gives, on the deployed residual-only
convention, $\Bclos\gtrsim0.85$ against $\Binsuf\lesssim0.62$ (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). This is the
claim we lean on. It is stable---surviving leave-one-solvent, -solute, and -pair folds and adversarial
deletion of the eight highest-error pairs---but not certified: a two-way cluster bootstrap gives
$P(\Bclos>\Binsuf)\approx0.78$ with a $90\%$ margin spanning zero, and with only $17$/$41$ clusters
this small-$G$ figure is a coarse band (mid-$0.7$s to high-$0.8$s), so we present the separation as a
\emph{supporting} measurement---likely but not certified---not a stand-alone certificate. The
convention, leakage, $\gamma$-stratification, and leverage stress-tests are in
App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}. Label noise is unestimable here (replicate-free) and assumed zero; the bounds
are invariant to zero-mean noise, and between-method bias does not arise because the matched IDAC is
essentially single-method ($158/165$ gas chromatography), leaving only a within-method systematic
exposure that against the ${\approx}0.23\,(\ln\gamma)^2$ margin would have to be implausibly large to
close the separation.

\paragraph{The closure defect is low-rank and correctable.} That $\Bclos$ dominates locates the
ceiling in the map; it does not say the map is \emph{fixably} wrong. It is. A small
structure-preserving residual on the fixed COSMO-SAC exchange-energy kernel
($\Delta=B\,\mathrm{diag}(a)\,B^{\!\top}$ over the $\sigma$-grid, symmetric, zero-initialised), fit on
the \emph{reference} VT-2005 profiles, removes $49\%$ of the total reference-input error out-of-sample (five-fold
cross-validated, activity MSE $1.47\!\to\!0.75$---most of the reducible closure component above
the $\Binsuf$ floor) at \emph{rank one} ($52$ parameters; rank~2 adds
nothing, rank~3 over-fits). At $52$ parameters against $n{=}60$ this is provisional, and we have not
separated the kernel correction from a per-solvent offset. So $\Bclos$ is not diffuse model error but
a low-dimensional, correctable defect of the map---addressable with a handful of parameters on the
closure, whereas the same error is only made worse by feeding the inputs truthfully
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}) and untouched by an output residual (\S\ref{sec:gateb}); whether an end-to-end
model realises the gain is a compute-gated question we leave open.

\paragraph{Where the closure fails, chemically.} The residual error is not an implementation
artifact: our differentiable layer reproduces the NIST benchmark COSMO-SAC-2002~\cite{bell2020cosmosac}
to RMSE $0.003\,\ln\gamma$ on identical VT-2005 profiles, with self-solvation $\ln\gamma^\infty(i,i)$
exactly zero. Yet $90\%$ of the squared error falls on strong hydrogen-bond-donor solvents
(acceptor-only near-exact), placing $\Bclos$ squarely in COSMO-SAC's documented single-parameter
H-bond kernel---a known limitation, not a layer idiosyncrasy, and the specific defect the
donor/acceptor-typed 2010 closure was built to fix. Convention-robustly, adding the physically
\emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term \emph{increases} error ($1.47\to1.80$)
on size-asymmetric pairs---what a misspecified map does, not what missing information would (details in
App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}).

\paragraph{The ceiling moves with closure fidelity: a standard-closure control.} The 2002
closure-dominance above is not a general fact about physical inputs; it is specific to the deployed
map's single-parameter hydrogen-bonding kernel, and the field already has better-parameterized
closures built for exactly that defect. We run the NIST reference implementation of COSMO-SAC-2010
(donor/acceptor-typed HB) and -dsp on the same matched pairs, now under the reference Delaware (UD)
$3$-profile database---on which the reference 2002 map scores $\mathrm{MSE}=1.26$, against $1.47$ for
our deployed residual-only layer on the VT-2005 profiles used elsewhere. The better-parameterized
closures cut the error to $\mathrm{MSE}=0.77$ (2010) / $0.79$ (dsp, $n{=}57$). The cleanest read is the
\emph{same-database} contrast, which holds the latent $\zstar$ fixed and is therefore
insufficiency-free by construction: on the identical UD $3$-profiles, swapping the 2002 kernel for
2010 cuts the error from $\mathrm{MSE}=1.26$ to $0.77$, a ${\sim}70\%$ drop attributable to the closure
alone---the fidelity axis, measured directly. Charging that against the $\Binsuf$ floor, this
\emph{dissolves the closure-dominance verdict}---$P\approx0.79$ under two-way solute$\times$solvent clustering that $\Bclos$ no longer exceeds
$\Binsuf$, the near-mirror of the $P\approx0.78$ that it \emph{does} bind under 2002 (same clustering,
opposite verdicts). This last step charges a UD-based error against a $\Binsuf$ estimated on the
VT-2005 latent used elsewhere, so the $P\approx0.79$ figure is a \emph{cross-database} quantity: we
report it as indicative, not exact, and rest the conclusion on the database-consistent
\emph{same-database} contrast above ($1.26\to0.77$ on identical UD profiles, insufficiency-free by
construction). A $\Binsuf$ re-estimate on the UD latent is the exact test; absent it the mismatch can
move $P\approx0.79$ but not its sign, since a richer $3$-profile latent would if anything \emph{lower}
$\Binsuf$, reinforcing rather than weakening the closure attribution. We do \emph{not} claim a flip to the inputs: we hold only an \emph{upper} bound
on $\Binsuf$, so ``the inputs bind'' would require a lower bound we do not have---what the modern
closure establishes is only that our own 2002 attribution no longer holds. The decision-relevant
reading is thus not ``the closure always dominates'' but ``on the low-fidelity 2002 closure it does, and the
fix is a better closure, not truer inputs''---closure fidelity is the second axis of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Scope.} This measurement is a low-$\gamma$/$298$\,K/VT-2005-matchable corner and does not
transfer to the finite-composition regime the paradox lives in (\S\ref{sec:scope}); the full audit is
in App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}.

\subsection{What we do not measure, and why}
\label{sec:scope}
The paper's strongest claim and its causal attribution live in \emph{different regimes}, and we state
the gap plainly rather than distribute it across scope footnotes. The grounding paradox is measured
where the model operates---finite composition, all $\gamma$, $n{=}5608$. Its \emph{cause}---the
closure/insufficiency attribution (\S\ref{sec:measure}) and the compensating-surrogate mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:surrogate})---is measured only on the matched external-reference corner: infinite
dilution, $298$\,K, low $\gamma$, $n{=}60$ (attribution) and $n{=}44$ (mechanism). So the cause of the
headline result is \emph{not} measured in the headline's own regime; we transfer it by in-regime
signs---the more-physics-worse effect on the full $n{=}5608$ set and the chemistry map
(\S\ref{sec:map})---which is a conjecture, not a measured fact, and is the first place a referee
should press. Two structural limits keep us from closing it. The external-reference corner cannot be
enlarged: only $74$ VT-2005-matchable IDAC pairs exist ($60$ at $298$\,K plus $14$ off-temperature),
so the $n{=}60$ attribution cannot be pushed to significance on this data; and the matched IDAC is
essentially single-method (gas chromatography), so a method-stratified re-estimate is unavailable.
What \emph{does} hold in any regime, needing neither bounds nor cross-regime transfer, is the
same-database fidelity contrast of \S\ref{sec:measure} ($1.26\to0.77$, closure alone). We therefore
lead with that, present the attribution as a demonstration, and mark its regime transfer as the open
question.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The result is a cautionary measurement for physics-informed modeling: grounding a
model on truer physical inputs is only as good as the closure that consumes them,
and when that closure is a fixed, mildly misspecified map, better inputs surface
its bias rather than help. The external latent does not make the physics path win; what it buys is a \emph{lever}. Used as an
instrument on the corner we can measure, the decomposition places the ceiling in the closure rather
than its inputs, and a higher-fidelity closure (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) removes the penalty---so within
this closure family effort spent on richer inputs or a better encoder is mis-targeted, and the payoff
is in the closure itself (a corrected or learned residual on the map, not on its output, which
\S\ref{sec:gateb} shows is inert). This attribution is a demonstration on a low-$\gamma$,
infinite-dilution corner, not a certificate: we do not claim it transfers unchanged to the
finite-composition regime the paradox spans, where its shared closure-misspecification cause is a
conjecture supported by in-regime signs (the more-physics-worse effect on the full $n{=}5608$ set and
the chemistry map), not a measured fact.

\paragraph{The accuracy case, and its boundary.} That the physics prior does not improve accuracy has
no confirmed exception: the grounded model trails the matched black box on the full corpus, the
external SOTA (FastSolv, SolProp) does no better on a difficulty-bounding by-solute split
(App.~\ref{sec:si-baselines}), and grounding the crystal factor on external labels did not move the
ceiling (\S\ref{sec:e2}). The one place the physical \emph{structure} helps is temperature
extrapolation---but as a hypothesis-class property the trained models do not exploit
(\S\ref{sec:supporting}). These point to a \emph{structural} ceiling: the crystal/activity split is
non-identifiable from solubility alone (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}), and the activity closure is
misspecified where we can measure it (\S\ref{sec:measure}), so within this family better inputs, more
data, and auxiliary grounding do not recover an accuracy the factorization cannot express. What a
misspecified closure costs is thus not only accuracy but the interpretability the bottleneck appeared
to buy.

\paragraph{When a fixed-closure prior helps versus hurts.} The tax rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) predicts
\emph{when} to expect these negatives: a fixed-closure prior helps only when the estimation variance it
saves exceeds its asymptotic bias $\Delta_\infty$ (both measurable). The variance benefit is largest at
small $n$ and vanishes as data accumulate or as the black box nears the aleatoric floor, whereas
$\Delta_\infty$ is set by the closure; a prior is therefore predicted to hurt precisely in the
mature-data, low-noise, well-covered regime. The synthetic dial (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and the pKa closure
(\S\ref{sec:pka}) exercise the fidelity and sufficiency behaviour directly. Whether grounding helps is
shaped by \emph{two} axes---closure fidelity and how the latent is trained (supervised vs.\
end-to-end); mapping that plane in full, and sweeping each axis directly on real systems, is deferred
to future work.

\paragraph{The novelty inversion (exploratory).} The sharpest---and most unexpected---confirmation of
the tax rule runs opposite to the usual coverage intuition. Resolving the physics tax by solute
novelty, the closure is neutral on the most \emph{novel} solutes (nearest-train Tanimoto $\le0.3$:
$\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=-0.04\pm0.18$) and heaviest on the most \emph{familiar}, best-covered ones
(Tanimoto $>0.8$: $+0.51\pm0.09$)---exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} predicts, since the variance
a prior saves vanishes precisely where the black box is already accurate, leaving a structural closure
bias as the dominant residual. The tax likewise concentrates in strongly hydrogen-bonding solvents
(water $+0.52\pm0.35$) and reverses sign in the acceptor-dominated amides ($-0.22\pm0.11$) the closure
was built for (\S\ref{sec:map}). Both patterns are exploratory---no multiplicity control across strata
families---but they land where the tax rule predicts, and where much of modern machine learning
operates. We claim no universal boundary; we \emph{propose}, but do not here cleanly validate, a diagnostic for the sign of the physics tax:
measure $\Bclos$ through the decomposition, read $\Delta_\infty$ off the large-$n$ asymptote, and
the sign should follow. In this sense the failure is not idiosyncratic to
solubility; it recurs whenever a misspecified closure is coupled to an expressive encoder on
abundant, low-noise data.


\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~$\Binsuf$ is upper-bounded, not point-identified (no replicates at fixed
$\zstar$); the smoothness-free Jensen bound assumes $m$ and $g$ share the $\lngi$ reference state,
which we verify (\S\ref{sec:measure}), so the mean offset is genuine decoder bias, not a units
artifact. (ii)~The constant-offset margin is convention-specific and inverts under the deployed
residual-only default; there the separation rests on the convention-independent bound and the
more-physics-worse effect. (iii)~The measurement is the infinite-dilution/$298$\,K/low-$\gamma$ corner,
not the finite-composition regime the paradox lives in (\S\ref{sec:scope}). (iv)~Absolute scaffold
accuracy is encoder-bound but well \emph{above} the inter-lab noise floor ($0.15$--$0.31$ in $\lnx$;
\S\ref{sec:supporting}), so label noise does not explain the ceiling. (v)~The compensating-surrogate
result is three-seed mean$\pm$sd ($n{=}44$; \S\ref{sec:surrogate}); its load-bearing content is the
\emph{sign} and low-rank, transferable \emph{structure}, with the magnitudes
($51\pm2\%$/$72\pm1\%$/$4.1\pm0.5\times$) reported but varying across pipeline versions.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Feeding a physics-informed solubility model its reference physical inputs made it
worse. What the paper contributes is a way to measure that failure: with an
externally grounded latent, the reference-input excess risk splits into a
decoder-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term, and on the
subset we can measure, the ceiling is most likely
the closure rather than the inputs ($P\approx0.78$ under two-way clustering, not certified at
conventional confidence). The
practical implication is to fix the map itself, since within this closure truer
inputs surface its bias and an output-space correction is inert---though this attribution is
$2002$-specific: a modern closure (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) dissolves it. Neither failure is universal: raising closure fidelity or supervising
the latent toward truth moves the system out of the hurts-corner, so whether grounding helps is
\emph{shaped by} two axes---closure fidelity and how the latent is trained. The same grounding also indicates,
where the closure binds, that the learned $\sigma$-profile is a closure-compensating surrogate rather
than the physical quantity---an interpretability cost of physics-informed bottlenecks that the
external latent makes measurable: a $51\pm2\%$, low-rank, transferable departure from truth across
three seeds.
