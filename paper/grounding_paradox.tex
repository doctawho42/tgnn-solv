\title{When Reference Physical Inputs Help or Hurt a Learned Solubility Model:\\
A Closure-vs-Inputs Diagnostic and a Two-Axis Map of Grounding}
\author{N.~L. Polomoshnov \and \pending[co-authors]}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Feeding a graph network's own learned, imperfect $\sigma$-profiles through a fixed COSMO-SAC
closure predicts solid--liquid solubility better (MAE $1.85$ vs $2.25$ in $\ln x_2$) than feeding
external quantum-chemical reference profiles (VT-2005) through the same closure, which erases the
positive $R^2$ ($+0.33\!\to\!-0.03\pm0.04$). This grounding paradox is robust across three seeds
($n{=}5608$) and three injection controls. Mechanistically, end-to-end training drives the
``physical'' latent into a low-rank, out-of-sample-transferable surrogate that cancels the closure's
error rather than reporting the physical quantity (a single converged-run illustration: the sign is
robust, the magnitudes---$53\%$ departure, $73\%$ in two modes, $3.3\times$ transfer---provisional).
Because the latent carries an external reference, the reference-input excess risk splits exactly,
with no closure fitting, into a closure-misspecification and an input-insufficiency term, letting us
\emph{measure} rather than assume where the ceiling lies; on $n{=}60$ matched pairs the closure term
binds ($P\approx0.78$), though a modern closure (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) dissolves it. Both the split and
the mechanism are measured at infinite dilution, not in the finite-composition regime where the
paradox lives, so the causal attribution is corroborative, not established. We organize the effect
on a two-axis map (closure fidelity $\times$ latent supervision), and a second closure---$\mathrm{p}K_a$
through a Hammett relationship---reproduces the sign flip: the same reference swap helps in the
well-specified meta/para regime and hurts in the ortho regime. We make no accuracy claim; the
contribution is the diagnostic and the interpretability caution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% Frame: the field's default is "add physics / ground on real inputs -> better OOD".
% We show a clean counterexample and a measurement that says why.
Coupling a graph encoder to an explicit thermodynamic bottleneck is a common
recipe for out-of-distribution solubility prediction: the model must route its
prediction through interpretable physical factors, and each factor can in
principle be grounded on abundant single-component data that a black-box
predictor cannot exploit. The expectation is that the closer the model's physical
inputs are to the truth, the better it should predict. On the activity branch
this expectation fails on our model---yet a recent supervised-$\sigma$ model reports the
\emph{opposite}, so the sign of grounding is not a fixed fact. The reason we dwell on our failure is
what its \emph{cause} reveals. Having the latent's reference value externally does two things: it lets us
\emph{measure} where the ceiling lies and attribute it to the closure rather than the inputs (the only
setting in which that attribution is possible, since a fitted latent has no external oracle), and it
lets us hold the learned latent against its truth---which shows that, trained end-to-end through a
misspecified closure, the latent is driven off the physical quantity into a systematic, low-rank,
out-of-sample-transferable compensating surrogate (confound-free control, \S\ref{sec:surrogate}).
Both organize into one map: whether grounding helps or hurts is shaped by two axes---how the latent
is trained (supervised vs.\ end-to-end) and the closure's fidelity---which we anchor with three real
systems and a synthetic dial, and a standard higher-fidelity closure (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) moves the
ceiling and dissolves the closure-dominance verdict (\S\ref{sec:measure}). The composed-predictor framing
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) and the physics-tax sign rule give the formal account of the sign; the
synthetic dial (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and the pKa closure (\S\ref{sec:pka}) trace the same two axes.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our thesis is that whether grounding a learned latent in reference physics helps or hurts is \emph{shaped
by} two axes---how the latent is trained (supervised toward truth vs.\ shaped end-to-end through the
fixed closure) and the closure's fidelity. Three findings stand cleanly---the paradox, a reusable
measurement instrument, and the \emph{structure} of the compensating-surrogate mechanism (its sign
and low-rank, transferable form robust; its magnitude a single converged-run estimate); the
closure-vs-inputs attribution and the two-axis map we present as, respectively, a caveated corner
measurement and an organizing frame.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The grounding paradox (empirical).}
  Substituting reference VT-2005 $\sigma$-profiles into the differentiable COSMO-SAC
  closure degrades activity ($\lngi$) and solubility ($\lnx$) prediction relative
  to a free learned activity head, the opposite of the intended benefit
  (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).
  \item \textbf{A measurement instrument: the closure/insufficiency decomposition.} Because the
  physical latent $\zstar$ (the reference $\sigma$-profile) has external ground truth, the
  reference-input excess risk splits \emph{exactly} as $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$ with no model
  fit (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}). The decomposition and its estimators are textbook;
  the new element is exploiting an externally referenced latent to bound the split
  empirically. On the convention-robust evidence---a bound that holds however the
  COSMO-SAC closure is configured, plus the more-physics-worse effect---$\Bclos$
  (misspecified decoder) exceeds $\Binsuf$ (input insufficiency) on the low-$\gamma$ subset we
  can measure. This is robust across the tabulated $k$-NN, binning, random-forest, and ridge insufficiency
  estimators (a more conservative ridge stress-test is the sole non-certifying case) and to leaving out
  any solvent, so the binding term is \emph{likely} the closure---but as a \emph{sub-threshold}
  ($P\approx0.78$, margin CI spanning zero) measurement on the $n{=}60$ low-$\gamma$ corner, not the
  finite-composition regime where the paradox lives (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{The mechanism where grounding hurts: a compensating-surrogate latent.} On the
  end-to-end, low-fidelity anchor the network's ``physical'' input does not stay physical: on a
  confound-free one-model control the predicted $\sigma$-profile departs from the reference one by $53\%$ in
  a low-rank (top-two modes $73\%$), out-of-sample-transferable ($3.3\times$) pattern---a systematic
  closure-compensating surrogate, not the physical quantity (a $\sigma$-grounded model \emph{can}
  recover the reference profile; the objective pulls it away). The magnitudes are a single
  converged-run estimate---sign and structure robust, magnitude provisional. This is why grounding
  hurts here, and why a \emph{supervised}-$\sigma$ model finds the opposite (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}).
  \item \textbf{The closure-fidelity axis: our own attribution self-dissolves under a modern closure.}
  On the same matched pairs, evaluated under a reference 3-profile database (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp, NIST
  implementation), the standard better-parameterized closures no longer show closure-dominance
  ($P\approx0.79$ that $\Bclos$ no longer exceeds $\Binsuf$ under two-way clustering)---so our own 2002
  attribution self-dissolves, locating it in the 2002 single-parameter H-bond kernel specifically, and
  the actionable lesson is to raise closure fidelity, not enrich the inputs. (We do \emph{not} claim
  the inputs then bind: that would need a lower bound on $\Binsuf$, which we lack.)  (\S\ref{sec:measure})
  \item \textbf{A negative that is not fixable downstream.} A post-hoc output-space residual on the
  physics prediction recovers calibration but not accuracy---the closure binding cannot be undone
  downstream (\S\ref{sec:gateb}); and while the physical hypothesis \emph{class} extrapolates in
  temperature, the trained model does not exploit it (\S\ref{sec:supporting}). Read against the lemmas
  and the closure measurement, these point to a \emph{structural} ceiling within this closure family
  rather than an incidental one (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item \textbf{A general account, and honest scoping.} The method is not tied to
  COSMO-SAC: it is stated for any composed predictor $\hat y=g(h(x))$ with a fixed closure
  $g$ over a learned intermediate. We prove two structural lemmas
  (Lemmas~\ref{prop:ident}--\ref{prop:decomp}) in the main text; the grounding-gap identity
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}) and the physics-tax sign rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) are deferred to
  App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}.
  The grounding-gap identity bounds the gap by the closure bias, but both its assumptions
  (A1 representability, A2 lossless bottleneck) fail or are unverifiable in our setting, so it is
  \emph{not} load-bearing; we read the gap as a \emph{symptom} and rest certification on the
  assumption-light decomposition (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}) alone. The sign rule's critical-budget and aleatoric-floor
  corollaries predict the two negatives above. The account transfers to synthetic closure
  families (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and a second chemical closure (pKa through a Hammett relationship,
  \S\ref{sec:pka}, where the same oracle swap flips sign across strata---grounding helps in the
  well-specified meta/para regime and hurts in the ortho regime---transferring the diagnostic off
  solubility); all proofs are elementary
  (App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}
For a solute~$s$ in solvent~$v$ at temperature~$T$, the target is $y=\lnx$, the
log saturated mole fraction. Dissolving a solid trades two costs against each
other---breaking the crystal lattice ($\Phicry$) and mixing the freed molecules
against non-ideal solute--solvent interactions ($\lng$)---and solid--liquid
equilibrium (SLE) sets the log-solubility to their negative sum:
\begin{equation}
  \lnx = -\underbrace{\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\Big(\tfrac1T-\tfrac1\Tm\Big)}_{\Phicry(T)\ \text{(crystal, depends on $s$)}}
         \;-\;\underbrace{\lng(x_2,T)}_{\text{activity, depends on }(s,v)} ,
  \label{eq:sle}
\end{equation}
with the constant-$\Delta C_p$ (heat-capacity-of-fusion) correction omitted for
clarity. The activity term is produced by a \emph{differentiable closure} $g$:
a graph encoder predicts a $\sigma$-profile---the distribution of screening
(surface) charge density over a molecule, the histogram a COSMO model
consumes---for each molecule, and COSMO-SAC (the COnductor-like Screening
Model--Segment Activity Coefficient \cite{lin2002}) maps the pair of profiles to
$\lng$. Write $\zstar$ for the \emph{reference} physical latent, the externally referenced
$\sigma$-profile from the VT-2005 database (the Virginia Tech 2005 compilation of
quantum-chemically computed screening-charge profiles for ${\sim}1{,}400$
compounds \cite{mullins2006}), and $g(\zstar)$ for the closure output when fed the
reference profile. The model's own head instead predicts a \emph{learned} profile
$\hat z$ and evaluates $g(\hat z)$; a matched \emph{DirectGNN} control shares the
encoder but emits $\lnx$ with no closure, and is the reference for ``what the
physics bottleneck costs'' (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\begin{figure}[t]

\caption{The model and the oracle substitution. Solute and solvent graphs pass
through a shared encoder to a crystal head (the ideal term $\Phi(T)$) and a
$\sigma$-profile head; the predicted profile $\hat z$ feeds the fixed COSMO-SAC closure
$g$, which returns $\ln\gamma_2$, and an SLE solver combines the two into $\lnx$. Input

\end{figure}

\paragraph{The oracle substitution.} Feeding $\zstar$ in place of $\hat z$
(a $\sigma$-oracle) isolates the closure from encoder error: if the closure were
well specified, $g(\zstar)$ should be the best available activity estimate. The
paradox is that it is not.

\paragraph{Data.} Solubility measurements are a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split of
BigSolDB \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb} (grouping molecules by ring-system-plus-linker
scaffold, side chains stripped), so test solutes share no scaffold with training;
the activity labels $m$ are experimental infinite-dilution activity coefficients
($\lngi$, IDAC) compiled from ThermoML (the NIST/IUPAC archive of experimental
thermophysical data \cite{frenkel2011thermoml}), and the reference $\sigma$-profiles
$\zstar$ are the VT-2005 database.

\section{Theory: locating a physics prior's ceiling in a composed predictor}
\label{sec:framework}

The results below are stated for a general \emph{composed predictor} $\hat y=g(h(x))$
in which a fixed, non-fitted map $g$---the \emph{closure}---consumes a learned physical
intermediate $z=h(x)$, and are compared against a matched free predictor that shares the
encoder but drops $g$. Solubility, with $g$ the COSMO-SAC closure and $z$ the
$\sigma$-profile, is one instance; \S\ref{sec:pka} gives a second (pKa through a fixed
Hammett closure), and \S\ref{sec:dial} a controlled synthetic family. Nothing in
Lemmas~\ref{prop:ident}--\ref{prop:decomp}, Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, or
Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} is specific to COSMO-SAC. Two quantities organize the section:
the closure/insufficiency split $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$, which is the assumption-light
\emph{instrument}, and the observable grounding gap $\Gamma$, which is a \emph{symptom} of
closure misspecification whose validity we characterize (Fig.~\ref{fig:composed}).

\subsection{Why a free head can compensate: structural non-identifiability}
The crystal/activity split is not identifiable from solubility data alone, which
is precisely why an unconstrained head can silently absorb crystal error into the
activity term (Fig.~\ref{fig:ident}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.94\linewidth]{figs/fig_ident.pdf}
\caption{Why the crystal/activity split is non-identifiable from solubility.
\emph{(a)}~As functions of $1/T$ the crystal direction $\Phi$ and the activity direction
$\ln\gamma^\infty$ are nearly collinear, so a whole band of crystal/activity splits fits the
data equally well (the profiled Fisher information for $\dHfus$ is zero, Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}).
\emph{(b)}~An external single-component label points off that axis and collapses the band to a
point, pinning the split---the mechanism behind the aux-stream grounding, with the measured
Cram\'er--Rao drop on $\dHfus$.}
\label{fig:ident}
\end{figure}
\begin{lemma}[Structural non-identifiability of the SLE split]\label{prop:ident}
Consider the infinite-dilution SLE observable $\mu(T)=-\Phicry(T)-\lngi(T)$ with
$\Delta C_p=0$, so that $\Phicry(T)=(\dHfus/\Rgas)(1/T-1/\Tm)$ is affine in $u=1/T$,
and a symmetric two-suffix activity class ($\lng=\lngi\,(1-x_2)^2$) in which
$\lngi(T)$ is affine in $u$. Then, for any design with at least two distinct
temperatures, $\mu$ depends on the parameters only through the two-dimensional
statistic (its slope and intercept in $u$), and the activity intercept and slope
already span that plane;
hence the crystal parameters $(\dHfus,\Tm)$ are unidentified, the profiled Fisher
information for $\dHfus$ is exactly zero, and the indeterminacy set is
two-dimensional. The deficiency is closed only by a rank-completing external
constraint (a direct crystal label, or a fixed activity slope). At finite dilution
the composition factor $(1-x_2)^2$ breaks the exact affinity, leaving a non-zero but
near-degenerate information of order $x_2^2$ (numerically, condition number
$2.3\times10^{5}$; \S\ref{sec:ident}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent This is the standard structural-identifiability picture for a
collinear design \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}; we prove it in the SLE form
(App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}) and audit it numerically in \S\ref{sec:ident}. It is
\emph{enabling background},
not our headline: it explains why grounding an under-determined factor on
external single-component data is necessary, and why compensation between the
factors is expected. By itself it says nothing about whether grounding helps.
That depends on whether the closure that consumes the grounded inputs is
correctly specified, which is the question we make measurable next.

The non-identifiability is not binary but graded by the activity class: the efficient
information for $\dHfus$ is zero when the activity nuisance spans the crystal score's directions
$\{1,1/T\}$ and strictly positive (a finite Cram\'er--Rao bound) when the slope is constrained
(Lemma~\ref{prop:semiparam}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). The numerical audit (\S\ref{sec:ident})
shows the honest corollary: merely restricting the activity slope leaves
$\mathrm{sd}(\dHfus)\approx8{,}251$\,J/mol---finite but unusable---whereas external crystal labels,
which contribute a score direction outside the activity tangent space, close it to
$\approx1{,}689$\,J/mol.

\subsection{The closure--insufficiency decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}
Let $m$ be the activity target (experimental $\lngi$) and $g(\zstar)$ the closure
evaluated on the true latent. The excess risk of the reference-input path over the
best-possible predictor $\Ex[m\mid\cdot]$ (the Bayes predictor) decomposes
exactly---into a part the intermediate cannot resolve and a part the fixed map gets
wrong (Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp-concept}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{figs/fig_decomp_concept.pdf}
\caption{What the closure/insufficiency split means. The activity target $m$ is scattered
against a one-dimensional summary of the physical intermediate $\zstar$. The vertical spread
of the conditional cloud is the input insufficiency $\Binsuf=\Ex\,\Var(m\mid\zstar)$---what no
map of $\zstar$ can resolve---while the gap between the conditional mean $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ (the
best any map reaches) and the fixed closure $g(\zstar)$ is the closure misspecification $\Bclos$.
Feeding the true $\zstar$ through $g$ lands on the lower curve, off the conditional mean; that
displacement is the paradox. Schematic; the measured bounds are Fig.~\ref{fig:decomp}.}
\label{fig:decomp-concept}
\end{figure}

\begin{lemma}[Exact closure--insufficiency decomposition and its one-sided bounds]\label{prop:decomp}
For a \emph{fixed} (non-fitted) closure $g$,
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(m-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\text{MSE of the reference-input path}}
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[\Var(m\mid \zstar)\big]}_{\Binsuf\ \text{(inputs cannot resolve)}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\Bclos\ \text{(decoder mis-map)}} .
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The cross term vanishes identically because $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar)$ is
$\zstar$-measurable and $\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid\zstar])\mid\zstar]=0$. The split is exact with no fitting of the closure $g$---the cross term vanishes because $g$ is
fixed and $\zstar$ is externally observed---so the total and $\Bclos$'s Jensen lower bound are
fit-free; $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$, however, must be estimated with a conditional-variance
smoother whose choice affects the verdict (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is a bias--variance split of model
discrepancy in the sense of Kennedy--O'Hagan \cite{kennedy2001} and
Brynjarsd\'ottir--O'Hagan \cite{brynjarsdottir2014}: $\Bclos$ is the systematic
discrepancy of a structurally fixed physical map, and its dominance over
$\Binsuf$ is the empirical content of ``the ceiling is the closure, not the
inputs.'' We do not claim \eqref{eq:decomp} as new; the affordance is that the
external groundedness of $\zstar$ lets us \emph{measure} the split rather than
assume it. This is the move's distinction from model-discrepancy calibration and auxiliary-data
uncertainty quantification, which use held-out physical observations to \emph{fit} a correction to a
discrepancy: here the closure is fixed and the latent itself is externally observed, so the map's
error is \emph{attributed} with no correction fitted---at the cost that the affordance runs only
where such an external latent oracle exists (fixed-closure predictors with a tabulated or measured
latent: LFERs, COSMO / group-contribution closures, thermodynamic cycles), not in gray-box or PINN
hybrids whose internal fields have no external ground truth. \textbf{The exactness is contingent on
$g$ being fixed}: for a
\emph{fitted} decoder the cross term does not vanish and a plug-in point
decomposition is invalid (we observe a spurious $-0.36$ cross term when
$\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ is replaced by a fitted estimate). We therefore report
\emph{one-sided bounds}, not a point split (each proved in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Bclos \ge (\Ex[m]-\Ex[g])^2$ by Jensen: a constant offset between the
  decoder's mean output and the target is misspecification, not input insufficiency, and
  this bound needs \emph{no smoothness assumption}. Its one requirement is that $m$ and $g$
  share the $\lngi$ reference state, which we check directly (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item $\Binsuf \le \Ex[\Var(m\mid \mathrm{bin}(g(\zstar)))]$, a valid
  \emph{upper} bound (law of total variance, since a binning of $g(\zstar)$
  coarsens $\zstar$).
\end{itemize}
Because $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ depends only on $(m,\zstar)$, it is
\emph{independent of the closure convention}: any disagreement between closure
variants is decoder disagreement and lands in $\Bclos$.

\subsection{The grounding gap: a symptom of closure misspecification}
\label{sec:gap}
The paradox---true inputs hurting---is a measurable quantity, not a curiosity. Let
$\mathcal E$ be the encoder class ($h:x\mapsto z$) and define three population risks of
the activity target $m$: the oracle risk $R_{\mathrm{orc}}=\Ex[(m-g(\zstar))^2]$, the best
end-to-end risk $R_{\mathrm{e2e}}=\inf_{h\in\mathcal E}\Ex[(m-g(h(x)))^2]$, and the
free-predictor (black-box) risk $R_{\mathrm{free}}=\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid x])^2]$. The
\emph{grounding gap} is $\Gamma:=R_{\mathrm{orc}}-R_{\mathrm{e2e}}$; the paradox is
$\Gamma>0$ (a free learned head beats the true input pushed through the closure). Under
(A1) a representable encoder ($\phi^\star\in\mathcal E$ with $\zstar=\phi^\star(x)$) and
(A2) a lossless bottleneck ($\Ex[m\mid x]=\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$), the gap is sandwiched,
$0\le\Gamma\le\Bclos$ with $\Gamma=\Bclos-(R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{free}})$, so a
well-specified closure ($\Bclos=0$) forces $\Gamma=0$ and, under A1--A2, an observed
$\Gamma>0$ certifies $\Bclos>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). Then
$\Gamma$ is exactly the closure bias the encoder can \emph{absorb} by feeding $g$ a
wrong-but-useful intermediate---the formal content of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}'s ``a free
head silently compensates.''

\paragraph{$\Gamma$ is a symptom, not a certificate.} Both assumptions are load-bearing and
neither holds cleanly here. A1 is \emph{empirically doubtful}: the encoder is transfer-limited (a linear probe onto
descriptors drops from train $R^2\approx0.76$ to test $R^2\approx0.45$ out of distribution, a proxy
for---not a direct measure of---representability of $\zstar$), making $\phi^\star\in\mathcal E$
unlikely across scaffolds, so effectively $\phi^\star\notin\mathcal E$ and even $\Gamma\ge0$
is not guaranteed. A2 is unverifiable without the oracle and physically dubious (the
$\sigma$-profile is a lossy summary of the graph). When A2 fails, $\Gamma>0$ \emph{conflates}
two causes: closure misspecification ($\Bclos>0$) and input insufficiency (a correct closure
driven off-support to recover information the intermediate discarded). A controlled construction
with a \emph{well-specified} closure and a lossy intermediate gives $\Bclos=0$ yet $\Gamma>0$
(\S\ref{sec:dial}, and the pKa ortho sweep of \S\ref{sec:pka}). Certification of
closure-dominance therefore rests on the convention-independent \emph{decomposition}
(Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}), which needs neither A1 nor A2: in solubility the Jensen bound and
the insufficiency estimates place $\Bclos$ above $\Binsuf$ \emph{directly}
(\S\ref{sec:measure}), and the grounding gap only corroborates it. This is the paper's method:
\emph{the decomposition is the instrument, $\Gamma$ is the cheap symptom that motivates it, and
separating its two causes is what an externally referenced latent buys.}

\subsection{The physics tax and the critical data budget}
\label{sec:tax}
The gap is a population statement; the deployed question is whether the \emph{trained} physics
model beats the \emph{trained} black box at sample size $n$. Writing each trained risk as an
asymptote plus estimation variance, the \emph{physics tax}
$\mathcal{T}(n)\approx\Delta_\infty+[V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]$ (with
$\Delta_\infty:=R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}$) is negative---physics helps---only
when the estimation variance a constrained prior saves exceeds its asymptotic bias; if
$\Delta_\infty>0$ and the variance gap vanishes there is a critical budget $n^\star$ beyond which
physics always loses, and as the control nears the aleatoric floor the tax tends to $\Delta_\infty$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:tax}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). When the control is asymptotically Bayes and
A2 holds, $\Delta_\infty=\Bclos-\Gamma$, the \emph{un-compensatable} part of the closure bias. The
novelty inversion---the tax heaviest on the \emph{best-covered} solutes (\S\ref{sec:map},
exploratory)---points in a direction the proposition allows, but is not a controlled test.

\subsection{Supporting structural facts}
Three further consequences of the same collinear geometry are used informally and
are not stated as theorems. \emph{(i)~Temperature extrapolation.} Within the
affine-in-$1/T$ activity family, same-pair prediction at a temperature $T^\star$
outside the training range is an affine extrapolation whose error is controlled by
the fitted-slope variance times $|1/T^\star-\overline{1/T}|$, whereas an
unstructured temperature encoder is unconstrained beyond its support. This is the
one place the physical \emph{structure} is expected to help; \S\ref{sec:supporting}
reports the class-level margin, which is a property of the hypothesis class and not
an accuracy claim for the trained model (indeed the trained models trail the
van't~Hoff baseline). \emph{(ii)~Geometry of compensation.} As the training window
$\Delta T\to0$, $1/T$ becomes nearly constant, the crystal and activity design
columns become collinear, and the smallest Fisher eigenvalue tends to zero (the
$\Delta T$ sweep of \S\ref{sec:ident} reaches conditioning $10^{16}$ at
$\Delta T=10$\,K), so an optimizer prefers to split error between the factors rather
than fit either correctly. \emph{(iii)~Grounding design.} Each single-component
stream rank-completes a different deficient direction of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident};
this is the design rule behind the auxiliary streams, though (as \S\ref{sec:e2}
shows for a dose-limited crystal run) it does not by itself promise an end-task gain.

\subsection{What we prove, and what we do not claim}
Two lemmas carry the measurement---the split's non-identifiability
(Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}) and the exact closure/insufficiency decomposition with its one-sided
bounds (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}); its class-dependence (Lemma~\ref{prop:semiparam}), the
grounding-gap sandwich (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}), and the sign rule for the physics tax
(Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) are stated and proved in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}, all elementary. We are
deliberate about their reach: Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} certifies closure misspecification \emph{only}
under A1--A2, and both fail or are unverifiable here (A1 empirically, A2 in principle), so we rest
closure-dominance on the assumption-light decomposition and use $\Gamma$ as a corroborating
symptom, not a certificate; Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} predicts the \emph{sign} of the tax, not an
optimum. There is no ``optimal grounding'' theorem and no sufficient condition for the
decomposition to improve out-of-distribution accuracy---the honest empirical answer
(\S\ref{sec:results}) is that it does not. These results are read
against, not in competition with, known results on model discrepancy
\cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}, parameter sloppiness
\cite{transtrum2015,gutenkunst2007}, structural and practical identifiability
\cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}, estimation under misspecification \cite{white1982},
and underspecification \cite{damour2022}; the calibration-versus-accuracy separation
of \S\ref{sec:gateb} is the classical refinement decomposition of a probability
forecast \cite{murphy1973,degroot1983}.

\subsection{The grounding paradox}
\label{sec:paradox}

Feeding reference $\sigma$-profiles into the closure degrades it rather than improving it,
in both the activity and the solubility target, and it fails the way a \emph{misspecified
map} does---not the way missing information would. In the solubility target the effect
erases any positive $R^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox}): the model predicts solubility better
\emph{from its own learned $\sigma$-profile} than when \emph{given the external reference one}
(MAE $1.85$ vs $2.25$, in $\lnx$ units). Two facts pin the cause on the map. First,
restoring the physically \emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term
(the entropic contribution from solute--solvent size and shape mismatch) \emph{increases}
error---what a wrong map does, not what a missing input would. Second, on the activity
target ($\lngi$, $n{=}60$ matched pairs) the fixed reference-input closure sits about $4\times$
above the irreducible-variance floor a flexible regressor reaches (\S\ref{sec:measure}),
under-predicting $\lngi$ with a systematic bias (Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}). The paradox survives three controls---the evaluation-time
oracle, injection of the reference profile \emph{at training} (MAE $1.98$), and a channel-swap
($2.08$), all worse than the learned-$\sigma$ arm---ruling out a wiring artifact.

We grade the evidence in two tiers: \emph{Tier~1} claims hold for \emph{any} COSMO-SAC
configuration and are the load-bearing ones, while \emph{Tier~2} claims are
configuration-robust corroboration that is not strictly independent of the model fit.

\paragraph{The load-bearing separation (leakage-immune, Tier~1).} We subtract the leakage-immune
law-of-total-variance $\Binsuf$ upper bound---the most conservative of our estimators, since the
$z^\ast$-neighbour estimators ($k$-NN, RF) are deflated by near-duplicate leakage and so \emph{inflate}
$\Bclos$---from the total error. On the model's \emph{deployed} residual-only convention this gives
$\Bclos\gtrsim0.85$, well above $\Binsuf\lesssim0.62$ (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). The bound conditions on
$g(\zstar)$, so we report it per convention; it is the claim we lean on, and it needs no smoothness
assumption beyond the validity of the $\Binsuf$ upper bound. We stress-test it directly
(App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}). The crossed $41$-solute$\times17$-solvent design leaks: any two pairs
sharing a molecule share half their $\zstar$ coordinates, so the $z^\ast$-neighbour estimators are
anti-conservative---the $k$-NN bound reads $0.40$ but jumps to $1.83$ once shared-molecule
neighbours are forbidden, so they only \emph{bracket} $\Binsuf$ across $\mathrm{MSE}/2$. The LOTV
bound instead conditions $m$ on the coarsened scalar $g(\zstar)$ and is immune to that leakage. It
is stable---it survives
leave-one-solvent-out ($17/17$ folds), leave-one-solute-out ($40/41$), leave-one-pair-out
($60/60$), and adversarial deletion of the eight highest-error pairs. For confidence, a single-axis
solvent-clustered bootstrap gives $P(\Bclos>\Binsuf)\approx0.89$; a two-way (solute$\times$solvent)
cluster bootstrap that also charges for repeated solutes lowers this to $P\approx0.78$ with a $90\%$
margin spanning zero. With only $17$ solvent and $41$ solute clusters this is the small-$G$ regime,
where cluster-bootstrap coverage is itself optimistic, so we read these figures as a coarse band (mid
$0.7$s to high $0.8$s) rather than to two decimals---and present the separation as a \emph{supporting}
measurement, likely but not certified at conventional confidence, not a stand-alone certificate. Two further checks live in
App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}: stratifying by $\lngi$, the closure bound rises with $\gamma$
($0.95\to2.69$, though thinly and estimator-dependently); and under the full COSMO-SAC convention the
assumption-free constant-offset (Jensen) bound also separates ($\Bclos\ge0.72>0.62$), whereas it
inverts under the deployed residual-only default ($0.43<0.62$)---the reason we headline the
leakage-immune bound rather than the constant offset.
Label noise is unestimable in this replicate-free set (the matched IDAC pairs carry no repeat
measurements at $298$\,K, so an inter-source floor cannot be formed as it was for solubility) and
assumed zero; the bounds we rely on are invariant to zero-mean noise. A \emph{systematic} between-method
offset in the compiled IDAC labels (gas--liquid chromatography, ebulliometry, and dilutor techniques
carry known method-dependent bias, not zero-mean scatter) is \emph{not} covered by that invariance.
We bound the exposure rather than leave it open. The load-bearing separation is a lower bound
$\Bclos\ge0.85$ against an upper bound $\Binsuf\le0.62$, a margin of ${\approx}0.23$ in
$(\ln\gamma)^2$. Method-heterogeneous bias uncorrelated with $\zstar$ inflates the \emph{insufficiency}
estimate (it adds conditional variance to $\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$), so it erases the certified
$\Bclos>\Binsuf$ only once it contributes ${\gtrsim}0.23\,(\ln\gamma)^2$ of between-method label
variance---a between-method standard deviation ${\gtrsim}0.5\,\ln\gamma$. A method-stratified
re-estimate on multi-source ThermoML entries is the direct test; absent it we state this overturning
threshold explicitly rather than assume the bias is zero. Because a replicate floor is
unavailable, we test exposure to the highest-scatter data directly: the separation \emph{sign} survives
deleting any single pair ($60/60$) and trimming the eight highest-error pairs---which include the
physically suspect associating outliers (e.g.\ $1$-hexanamine in methanol, $\ln\gamma^\infty{=}{+}2.62$
against its reverse $-0.97$)---though under that trim the estimated $\Bclos$ lower bound falls
${\sim}58\%$ ($0.91\to0.38$). The closure-dominance \emph{sign}, not its magnitude, is leverage-robust.

\paragraph{The closure defect is low-rank and correctable.} That $\Bclos$ dominates locates the
ceiling in the map; it does not say the map is \emph{fixably} wrong. It is. A small
structure-preserving residual on the fixed COSMO-SAC exchange-energy kernel
($\Delta=B\,\mathrm{diag}(a)\,B^{\!\top}$ over the $\sigma$-grid, symmetric, zero-initialised), fit on
the \emph{reference} VT-2005 profiles, removes $56\%$ of the total reference-input error out-of-sample (five-fold
cross-validated, activity MSE $1.47\!\to\!0.64$---nearly all of the reducible closure component above
the $\Binsuf$ floor) at \emph{rank one} ($52$ parameters; rank~2 adds
nothing, rank~3 over-fits). At $52$ parameters against $n{=}60$ this is provisional, and we have not
separated the kernel correction from a per-solvent offset. So $\Bclos$ is not diffuse model error but
a low-dimensional, correctable defect of the map---addressable with a handful of parameters on the
closure, whereas the same error is only made worse by feeding the inputs truthfully
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}) and untouched by an output residual (\S\ref{sec:gateb}); whether an end-to-end
model realises the gain is a compute-gated question we leave open.

\paragraph{Convention-robust corroboration (Tier~2).} The genuinely convention-robust fact is that
adding the physically \emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term \emph{increases}
error (activity MSE $1.47\to1.80$)---what a misspecified map does, not what missing information would.
This survives recomputing the size factor on the reference COSMO cavity volumes (Jensen $0.714\to0.717$,
essentially unchanged, since the term depends on size \emph{ratios}) and is concentrated on
size-asymmetric pairs---the documented miscalibration of the unmodified combinatorial term, the reason
modified-SG and free-volume corrections exist. The residual error is not an implementation artifact:
our differentiable layer reproduces the NIST benchmark COSMO-SAC-2002~\cite{bell2020cosmosac} to RMSE
$0.003\,\ln\gamma$ on identical VT-2005 profiles, and self-solvation $\ln\gamma^\infty(i,i)$ is exactly
zero; yet $90\%$ of the squared error falls on strong hydrogen-bond-donor solvents (acceptor-only
near-exact), placing $\Bclos$ in COSMO-SAC's documented single-parameter H-bond kernel---a known
limitation, not a layer idiosyncrasy. (A flexible regressor of $m$ on $\zstar$ fits $\sim$4$\times$
better than the fixed closure, but that regressor \emph{is} our $\Binsuf$ estimator ($\approx0.4$),
so ``$4\times$'' only restates that $\Bclos$ is large---not an independent corroboration.)

\paragraph{The ceiling moves with closure fidelity: a standard-closure control.} The 2002
closure-dominance above is not a general fact about physical inputs; it is specific to the deployed
map's single-parameter hydrogen-bonding kernel, and the field already has better-parameterized
closures built for exactly that defect. We run the NIST reference implementation of COSMO-SAC-2010
(donor/acceptor-typed HB) and -dsp on the same matched pairs, now under the reference Delaware (UD)
$3$-profile database---on which the reference 2002 map scores $\mathrm{MSE}=1.26$, against $1.47$ for
our deployed residual-only layer on the VT-2005 profiles used elsewhere. The better-parameterized
closures cut the error to $\mathrm{MSE}=0.77$ (2010) / $0.79$ (dsp, $n{=}57$). The cleanest read is the
\emph{same-database} contrast, which holds the latent $\zstar$ fixed and is therefore
insufficiency-free by construction: on the identical UD $3$-profiles, swapping the 2002 kernel for
2010 cuts the error from $\mathrm{MSE}=1.26$ to $0.77$, a ${\sim}70\%$ drop attributable to the closure
alone. Charging that against the $\Binsuf$ floor, this \emph{dissolves the closure-dominance
verdict}---$P\approx0.79$ under two-way solute$\times$solvent clustering that $\Bclos$ no longer exceeds
$\Binsuf$, the near-mirror of the $P\approx0.78$ that it \emph{does} bind under 2002 (same clustering,
opposite verdicts). This last step charges a UD-based error against a $\Binsuf$ estimated on the
VT-2005 latent used elsewhere, so the $P\approx0.79$ figure is a \emph{cross-database} quantity: we
report it as indicative, not exact, and rest the conclusion on the database-consistent
\emph{same-database} contrast above ($1.26\to0.77$ on identical UD profiles, insufficiency-free by
construction). A $\Binsuf$ re-estimate on the UD latent is the exact test; absent it the mismatch can
move $P\approx0.79$ but not its sign, since a richer $3$-profile latent would if anything \emph{lower}
$\Binsuf$, reinforcing rather than weakening the closure attribution. We do \emph{not} claim a flip to the inputs: we hold only an \emph{upper} bound
on $\Binsuf$, so ``the inputs bind'' would require a lower bound we do not have---what the modern
closure establishes is only that our own 2002 attribution no longer holds. The decision-relevant
reading is thus not ``the closure always dominates'' but ``on the low-fidelity 2002 closure it does, and the
fix is a better closure, not truer inputs''---closure fidelity is the second axis of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\paragraph{Scope.} The measurement is confined to the low-$\gamma$, $298$\,K, VT-2005-matchable
corner, so the numeric ceiling is not claimed to transfer to the finite-composition regime where the
paradox itself lives; the full adversarial audit and its four hedges are in App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}.

\subsection{The ceiling is not fixable downstream}
\label{sec:gateb}
% Artifacts: results/e5_sigma_grounding/seed_42/grounded_a_truetrain_residual{,_v2}_predictions.summary.json (Gate B v1 gated-shut / v2 forced-open); model.py correction_force_open_gate.
If the closure binding were merely a bad point estimate, a learned output-space
correction on top of the physics prediction should recover accuracy. It does not.
An additive bounded residual with a learned confidence gate
($\hat y=\hat y_{\text{phys}}+(1-c)\,r$, with $y=\lnx$) leaves the valve
effectively shut (applied
residual $\approx0$). Forcing the gate open removes the systematic
\emph{bias} ($+0.45\to+0.06$) but recovers little \emph{accuracy} ($R^2$
$0.236\to0.274$ on the same rows, a marginal rise that leaves the fit far short of usable). This is the classical calibration-versus-accuracy
separation: when the upstream map is misspecified, a monotone or affine post-hoc
map can fix reliability but not resolution. Recalibration buys calibration
without buying accuracy, which is what we expect if the closure, rather than the
estimate, is the binding constraint.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The result is a cautionary measurement for physics-informed modeling: grounding a
model on truer physical inputs is only as good as the closure that consumes them,
and when that closure is a fixed, mildly misspecified map, better inputs surface
its bias rather than help. The external latent does not make the physics path win. What it buys is
\emph{attribution}: on the infinite-dilution, low-$\gamma$ subset we can measure, the
ceiling sits in the decoder ($\Bclos$), with the inputs ($\Binsuf$) the less likely
source and label noise unestimable but assumed zero (and unable to manufacture the
constant offset the bounds rest on). We do not claim this numeric attribution transfers
unchanged to the finite-composition, high-$\gamma$ regime the solubility paradox itself
spans; that the solubility paradox shares the same closure-misspecification cause is a
conjecture supported by in-regime signs---the more-physics-worse effect on the full
$n{=}5608$ set and the chemistry map---not a measured fact. For practice this relocates the lever. Within this closure family, effort
spent on richer inputs or a better encoder is mis-targeted; the payoff is in the
closure itself, for example a corrected or learned residual on the map rather
than on its output (which \S\ref{sec:gateb} shows is inert).

\paragraph{The accuracy case is broad, with an explained boundary.} That the physics prior does
not improve accuracy is a pattern with no confirmed exception \emph{on accuracy}: the grounded
model trails the matched black box on the full corpus (and the external state-of-the-art (SOTA),
FastSolv and SolProp retrained on our corpus, does no better---though on a by-solute split that
bounds task difficulty rather than ranking head-to-head; App.~\ref{sec:si-baselines}), a
(dose-limited) attempt to ground the crystal factor on external labels did not move the ceiling
(\S\ref{sec:e2}, inconclusive). The one axis on which the physical \emph{structure} does
help is temperature extrapolation---but as a hypothesis-class property that the trained models do
not exploit (\S\ref{sec:supporting}), a partial counterexample we flag rather than hide. Read
together these point to a \emph{structural} ceiling: the crystal/activity split is
non-identifiable from solubility alone (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}), and the activity closure is
misspecified where we can measure it (\S\ref{sec:measure})---a measurement that feeds reference inputs
through the fixed closure and so cannot be a training artifact---so within this family better
inputs, more data, and auxiliary grounding do not recover an accuracy the factorization cannot
express. The contribution is accordingly not
an accuracy result but a \emph{map} of when grounding a learned latent in reference physics helps or hurts:
the externally grounded decomposition \emph{locates} the ceiling (measured, not proved), a standard
higher-fidelity closure moves it and dissolves the closure-dominance verdict, and---where the closure binds and the
latent is end-to-end---the ``physical'' intermediate behaves as a compensating surrogate; the two
axes are closure fidelity and latent supervision (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}). What a misspecified
closure costs is thus not only accuracy but the interpretability the bottleneck appeared to buy.

\paragraph{When a physics prior must lose.} The tax rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) turns these
negatives into a phase picture rather than a single verdict (Fig.~\ref{fig:phase}), and says
\emph{when} to expect them. A fixed-closure prior helps only when the estimation variance it saves exceeds its
asymptotic bias $\Delta_\infty$ (equal to $\Bclos-\Gamma$ only under A2 with an
asymptotically-Bayes control, otherwise read off the large-$n$ asymptote); both are measurable. The variance benefit is
largest at small $n$ and vanishes as data accumulate or as the black box nears the aleatoric
floor, whereas $\Delta_\infty$ is set by the closure and does not. A prior is therefore
predicted to hurt precisely in the mature-data, low-noise, well-covered regime, and this is what we
observe---the \emph{novelty inversion}, our most unexpected secondary finding. Resolving the
physics tax by solute novelty runs opposite to the usual coverage intuition: the closure is neutral
on the most \emph{novel} solutes (nearest-train Tanimoto $\le0.3$:
$\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=-0.04\pm0.18$) and heaviest on the most \emph{familiar}, best-covered ones
(Tanimoto $>0.8$: $+0.51\pm0.09$), because a structural closure bias surfaces as the dominant
residual only where the direct control is already accurate. The tax likewise concentrates in
strongly hydrogen-bonding solvents (water $+0.52\pm0.35$) and reverses sign in the
acceptor-dominated amides ($-0.22\pm0.11$) the closure was built for (\S\ref{sec:map}). Both
patterns are exploratory---no multiplicity control across strata families---but they point where
the tax rule predicts, and where much of modern machine learning operates. The synthetic dial (\S\ref{sec:dial})
and the pKa closure (\S\ref{sec:pka}) trace the two axes---closure fidelity and
intermediate sufficiency---directly. We claim no universal boundary; we \emph{propose}, but do not here cleanly validate, a diagnostic for the sign of the physics tax:
measure $\Bclos$ through the decomposition, read $\Delta_\infty$ off the large-$n$ asymptote, and
the sign should follow, though we do not cleanly validate it here. In this sense the failure is not idiosyncratic to
solubility; it recurs whenever a misspecified closure is coupled to an expressive encoder on
abundant, low-noise data.

\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~$\Binsuf$ is bounded, never point-identified: there
are no true replicate measurements at fixed $\zstar$, so the insufficiency estimates
lean on a smoothness assumption. The constant-offset (Jensen) bound is smoothness-free
but assumes $m$ and $g$ share the $\lngi$ (mole-fraction, Lewis--Randall) reference
state; we check this two ways---the closure is nearly exact within the largest
single-solvent stratum (pyridine, MSE $0.07$), which a uniform reference-state offset
could not produce, and an independent recomputation reproduces the closure output---so
the mean offset is genuine decoder bias, not a units artifact. We headline the
convention-independent bound (\S\ref{sec:measure}) as the load-bearing claim.
(ii)~The assumption-free constant-offset margin is convention-specific (full COSMO-SAC)
and \emph{inverts} under the deployed residual-only default; there the separation rests
on the convention-independent bound $\Bclos\ge\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ and
the more-physics-worse effect (which we verified is robust to the molecular-volume
basis). (iii)~The measurement is at infinite
dilution and 298\,K on the VT-2005-matchable, low-$\gamma$ subset; the non-ideal
amide-solvent regime is untested. (iv)~Absolute scaffold $\lnx$ accuracy is
encoder-bound; it sits well \emph{above} the inter-lab replicate-reproducibility floor
($0.15$--$0.31$ in $\lnx$; \S\ref{sec:supporting}), so label noise does not explain the ceiling,
but it is near the practical difficulty of new-solute extrapolation set by the current
state of the art. (v)~The compensating-surrogate result rests on a single converged one-model isolation
control ($n{=}44$ matched molecules; \S\ref{sec:surrogate}): the reported $53\%$ departure, its
low-rank structure (top-two modes $73\%$), and its $3.3\times$ out-of-sample transfer supersede an
earlier short pilot that reached only ${\sim}16\%$/${\sim}1.7\times$ before convergence. The
remaining hedges are the sample size and that the clean isolation is a single GPU run. This paper
is not an accuracy claim.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Feeding a physics-informed solubility model its reference physical inputs made it
worse. What the paper contributes is a way to measure that failure: with an
externally grounded latent, the reference-input excess risk splits into a
decoder-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term, and on the
infinite-dilution, low-$\gamma$ subset we can measure, the ceiling is most likely
the closure rather than the inputs ($P\approx0.78$ under two-way solute$\times$solvent clustering,
$0.89$ single-axis; not certified at conventional confidence;
label noise unestimable and assumed zero). The
practical implication is to fix the map itself, since within this closure truer
inputs surface its bias and an output-space correction is inert---though this attribution is itself
the sub-threshold, $2002$-specific corner measurement above, which a modern closure
(COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) dissolves. Neither failure is universal: raising closure fidelity or supervising
the latent toward truth moves the system out of the hurts-corner, so whether grounding helps is
\emph{shaped by} two axes---closure fidelity and how the latent is trained (Fig.~\ref{fig:map}). The same grounding also indicates,
where the closure binds, that the learned $\sigma$-profile is a closure-compensating surrogate rather
than the physical quantity---an interpretability cost of physics-informed bottlenecks that the
external latent makes measurable: a $53\%$, low-rank, transferable departure from truth on a
converged one-model control.
