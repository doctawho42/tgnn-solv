How much of a solid dissolves in a given liquid decides whether a drug is formulated as a tablet or
an injection, which solvent a crystallisation runs in, and how a product is purified. Each value is
measured one solute, one solvent and one temperature at a time, so most industrially relevant
combinations remain unmeasured, and independent determinations of the same system disagree by enough
to floor what any predictor can achieve. Dissolving a solid also trades the cost of breaking a
crystal against the cost of mixing the freed molecules into the solvent, so a predictor must be
right about two unrelated kinds of chemistry at once, on molecules unlike any it was shown.

Predictors divide by how much physical structure they impose. Direct models regress solubility from
descriptors or from a representation learned off the molecular graph; FastSolv \cite{osanloo2024}
does so with a deep neural network and leads on extrapolation to new solutes. They are the more
accurate there and they are opaque, the prediction arriving as a single number, silent about which
part of the chemistry it captured. Physics-informed models instead predict the quantities the
physical picture names---an activity coefficient for the solute--solvent interaction, a melting
temperature and heat of fusion for the crystal---and combine them through a fixed equation, as
SolProp \cite{vermeire2022solprop} does with a thermodynamic cycle of separately learned components.
That buys decomposability: each part is a quantity chemists tabulate on its own, so it can be
supervised against reference data a direct regressor cannot use. On accuracy the black box leads and
the physics-informed cycle trails it. Learned surrogates for the activity coefficient split the same
way: Winter et al.\ \cite{winter2022} predict \lngi\ from SMILES with a language model, and
Sanchez-Medina et al.\ \cite{sanchezmedina2023} and Wang et al.\ \cite{wang2026ghgeat} embed a
Gibbs--Helmholtz constraint in a graph network for its temperature dependence, all three making the
activity coefficient the regression target. A second line keeps a mechanistic model and learns its
\emph{input}. COSMO-SAC \cite{lin2002} is a segment-activity reformulation of Klamt's COSMO-RS, and
the $\sigma$-profile it consumes---the screening-charge density over a molecule's cavity---is that
framework's construct, tabulated from quantum chemistry \cite{mullins2006}; predicting it from
structure removes that calculation from the pipeline. TeNNet-SAC \cite{yang2025tennet} does so while
also learning the segment-activity kernel and the cavity geometry, and reports accuracy better than
COSMO-SAC's after end-to-end fine-tuning---the opposite headline from this one, on the same broad
construction, and we set the two side by side in \S\ref{sec:surrogate}.

A third design removes the intermediate: HANNA \cite{specht2024hanna} parameterises the excess Gibbs
energy directly from SMILES with no quantum chemistry, hard-coding the pure-component limit, the
Gibbs--Duhem relation and permutation symmetry in the architecture rather than the loss, and reports
higher accuracy than UNIFAC over $317{,}421$ mixture points. The two published designs bracket what
this work measures: admissibility can be bought with quantum labels or built into the functional
form, and this model does neither.

When a fixed physical structure improves or degrades a learned model is the broader question of
physics-informed machine learning \cite{karniadakis2021}, whose dominant forms impose structure as a
differentiable object: a governing equation enforced through the loss \cite{raissi2019}, gray-box
hybrids composing learned components with fixed mechanistic maps \cite{willard2022}, and operator
learning with a trainable correction to a misspecified equation \cite{ma2026}. More physics is
expected to improve generalisation, yet the same structure has documented failure modes in which it
makes optimisation or accuracy worse \cite{krishnapriyan2021}. In neural PDE solvers a learnable
term added to a fixed discretised operator absorbs truncation artifacts instead of physics, so the
apparent interpretability is illusory \cite{mohan2024}---though that error source is numerical,
which puts it beyond external reference. The same concern appears in \emph{concept-bottleneck
models}, networks routed through a layer of named, human-interpretable concepts that are the whole
of what the prediction is made from. That is the shape of a learned intermediate feeding a fixed
physical map, and there the failure is \emph{concept leakage}: a learned concept silently encodes
information beyond its nominal meaning \cite{mahinpei2021,margeloiu2021,havasi2022}. One route to it
runs through the downstream model, a misspecified head forcing the encoder to deform the concept
into carrying a dependence beyond that head's reach \cite{parisini2025}, after which overwriting a
frozen head's leaked concepts with ground truth can degrade accuracy \cite{espinosa2025leakage}. The
gap between a fixed physical map and reality is the model-discrepancy problem of Kennedy and
O'Hagan \cite{kennedy2001} and its calibration critique \cite{brynjarsdottir2014}. Four further
literatures---forecast decomposition, quasi-maximum likelihood, identifiability, and the validity of
composed pipelines---supply pieces we use below.

The design rests on an assumption rarely stated because it is taken for self-evident: a model
supplied with physical inputs closer to their true values predicts better. It needs testing, and for
one class of intermediate it can be tested outright, because a reference value is tabulated and can
be handed to the model in place of the one it computed. When a predictor of this shape is
inaccurate, either the fixed physical model is wrong or the learned quantities feeding it are
uninformative, and the two call for opposite remedies. What this literature lacks is a way to
\emph{measure}, rather than diagnose, which of the two limits accuracy, and whether the learned
intermediate has stopped reporting the quantity it is named after; in the concept-bottleneck case
the external reference that would settle either question is missing. We supply one---a reference
$\sigma$-profile for solubility, tabulated substituent constants for a second chemical domain---and
hold the mechanistic model fixed, so the tabulated value stays available as an external reference
for the learned one.

Here a graph neural network predicts the pair of charge-density distributions COSMO-SAC consumes, in
place of the quantum-chemical calculation that ordinarily supplies them. The tabulated counterpart
enters at either of two points: as a target the network is trained toward, or as a replacement for
what the network computed at the moment of prediction (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). Those are the two
operations the word \emph{grounding} names, and only one of them carries a sign. Training toward the
reference moves the error four ways out of five and the fifth the other way; supplying it at the
moment of prediction raises the error at every seed. Tabulated profiles serve a third purpose on
activity-coefficient data, where they divide the error of a fixed model between the model itself and
the inputs handed to it. Solubility withholds that division, a solubility measurement constraining
the crystal and the mixing contributions jointly and leaving the blame between them unassigned. What
the division returns is a map and not a verdict, one answer per stratum, a stratum being a
chemically defined set of rows: whether a fixed model's own error stands above what its inputs fail
to carry is a property of the chemistry the model is asked about, and where it does stand above, a
more accurate input exposes the model's bias instead of removing it. The learned distribution
meanwhile drifts from its tabulated counterpart along a direction shared across molecules, and stops
reporting the quantity it is named after. Tabulated substituent constants behave the same way inside
a fixed Hammett relation, the linear equation predicting how readily an acid gives up its proton
from the constants tabulated for its substituents.

A cluster-bootstrap frequency $P$ is the frequency, over two-way (solute\,$\times$\,solvent) cluster
resamples, with which the margin it is attached to stays positive. It is a resampling frequency rather

$>0.95$ and $<0.2$ closing the ends), because with $17$ solvent and $41$ solute clusters on the

\subsection{Supervising the intermediate toward the reference, and substituting it at prediction time}
\label{sec:paradox}
The word ``grounding'' names two opposite operations on the same VT-2005 database. Every solubility
figure in this subsection and the next is an \lnx\ MAE over one row set, the $5608$ of the $8103$
scaffold-split test rows that carry a solubility measurement (Table~\ref{tab:notation}). The
single-seed control arms below carry that identical set, so every comparison here stays inside one row
set. Supervising the
$\sigma$ head against the database \emph{during training} moves solubility MAE from
$2.037\pm0.064$ (ungrounded) to $1.927\pm0.094$ (grounded) over five leak-free seeds, a mean of
$+0.11$ that is not a sign: the per-seed gains are $+0.149$, $+0.122$, $+0.175$, $-0.017$ and
$+0.122$, and the two arms' per-seed values interleave, the grounded arm the worse of the two at
seed $45$.

% without it, while \S\ref{sec:si-tables} carried the asymmetry.  The null-target arm that would
% separate the reference values' ACCURACY from the extra pretraining is a training run and was not

The two arms differ in training budget: the grounded arm precedes the shared curriculum with a
$\sigma$ warm-up on the grounding pool of up to $40$ epochs, early-stopped on a held-out tenth,
while the ungrounded arm sets that budget to zero (\S\ref{sec:si-lock}). The warm-up against
permuted $\sigma$ targets that would separate the two was left unrun, so what the gain prices is the
intervention entire, extra pretraining and accurate targets together, rather than the accuracy of the
reference values by itself.
Those spreads are over initialisation and batch ordering, so they price the training run rather than
the draw of test solutes. Pooling the five seeds and resampling the $147$ test solute clusters, we
put the gain at $+0.110$, $95\%$ percentile interval $[+0.001,+0.222]$. The two prices hold
different things fixed and they do not agree: over the draw of solutes the interval clears zero at
its endpoint, and over training runs the gain has no sign at all.
Since the ungrounded arm carries no $\sigma$ stream, this is the one contrast in the paper a
$\sigma$-stream leak could inflate, and it is what the re-run was pre-committed to settle. Its
stream is certified inside each training run and reaches no test molecule: $0$ by canonical SMILES
against $5101$ held-out SMILES, and $0$ by Bemis--Murcko scaffold against $2702$ held-out scaffolds

family, whose stream build is uncertified, put the same gain at $+0.198$ $[+0.06,+0.33]$, and at

That pair bounds where a leak can act in \emph{that} family and is what limitation~(xii) refers to;
it is superseded here by a build in which a leak cannot act at all.
Substituting the reference profile for the learned one \emph{at inference} degrades it, and that
substitution alone is the effect this paper is named for. It erases any positive $R^2$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox}): the model predicts solubility better \emph{from its own learned
$\sigma$-profile} than when \emph{given the external reference one}, at MAE $1.93$ against $2.34$ in \lnx\

$95\%$ percentile interval $[+0.29,+0.54]$. The median per-row gap is $+0.30$ against the mean's

If the ceiling is the closure, the direct remedy is a better closure. The standard $2002\to2010$
step delivers one on the VT-2005-matched set, more than halving the error under the full convention
($P>0.95$), and withholds it on the broad IDAC set, where it scores $-0.99$ at $P\approx0.2$ under
the residual-only convention and leaves both an improvement and an equality unestablished in each of
its three cells (Table~\ref{tab:kernels}). A block split places that reversal in the
\emph{chemistry} and not in the profile database or the temperature range: the \emph{2010,
dispersion off} arm wins on the $57$ pairs shared with the smaller set ($1.78\to0.77$, $P>0.95$,
full convention) and loses that win on the complementary $420$ and again on the $80$ of those at
$298$\,K, the block the temperature half of the claim rests on and the weakest of the three. Those
$57$ pairs are broad-set rows too, so they are an overlapping sample and not an independent one, and
that split is the whole of the evidence for the reversal being chemical. We claim only that
\textbf{the fidelity lever we can demonstrate is confined to the smaller set's chemistry}, and that
null is additionally a small-molecule and glycol-heavy one, so on drug-like solids the lever is
untested rather than absent. Under the 2010 kernel's own typed latent, $306$-dimensional and so
several times worse resolved, the floor checks return $+1.29$ on the broad set against $-0.77$ at
$P\approx0.3$ on the smaller one, of which the first stands: loss of resolution rather than a
demonstrated flip to the inputs, which would need a \emph{lower} bound on $\Binsuf$
(\S\ref{sec:si-fidelity}).

Where $\Bclos$ dominates, on the one row set the rule leaves standing, the ceiling is in the map
\emph{as it is deployed}: the fixed kernel together with the one computed conformer it reads, which
is the composite $\Bclos$ is the error of and the object the pipeline runs on. Which of the two
carries the error this section leaves open, and dominance leaves open too whether the composite is
\emph{fixably} wrong---a fitted kernel residual on the $60$ matched pairs does not transfer across
solvents under the solvent-grouped folds that are the one fold
design free of leakage, and that result is a null and not a bound, so $\Bclos$ is an error a handful
of kernel parameters can absorb \emph{within} a solvent and whose correctability across solvents we
leave unshown. Where it fails is chemically placed: on the VT-2005-matched set $90\%$ of the squared
error falls on strong hydrogen-bond-donor solvents and the closure is near-exact on acceptor-only
ones, the chemistry the documented single-parameter H-bond term governs and the term the
donor/acceptor-typed 2010 closure was built to replace \cite{hsieh2010,fingerhut2017}. That the
$2002$ kernel fails there is established and the localisation restates it; what is new is that on
one such chemistry the failure is bounded against what the closure's inputs leave unresolved. Half
of that reading carries to the published evaluation of Table~\ref{tab:closure-anchor} and half stops
there, and on the broad set neither it nor its replacement reproduces, so we state neither. It
places the error in a \emph{chemistry} and not in one component of the composite, those solvents'
reference profiles being single gas-phase conformers too; of that, the conformational part is
bounded rather than open, since replacing the four glycol-ether references with Boltzmann ensembles
moves the stratum's margin from $+2.04$ to $+2.27$, and every substitution reported here stays
scoped to the VT-2005 tabulation's level of theory (\S\ref{sec:si-correctable}).

That COSMO-SAC-2002 is a low-fidelity closure is the contribution rather than a qualification of the
result: it is what a practitioner adopts by default, a closure's fidelity is \emph{unknowable in
advance} on a new system, and the field routinely composes an expressive encoder with a fixed
mechanistic map and assumes the map is good enough. Where that assumption fails the learned latent
silently departs from the quantity it is named after (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}), and an external oracle
is what makes the departure visible. Outside that one row set the map reports cells it is unable to
resolve, which is weaker than cells in which the closure is sound. How large an exposure the choice
of profile database is, and why that arithmetic sizes it without bounding its share, is in
\S\ref{sec:si-fidelity}.

\subsection{The same split on a second closure: pKa through a fixed Hammett relation}
\label{sec:pka-main}
% LENGTH PASS, 2026-08-18.  This subsection ran 1848 prose words and is cut to ~1010.  The
% construction, the estimator validation and the competence sweep are in \S\ref{sec:pka}, which
% carries every number this cut drops: the offsets 0.429/+0.303/+0.034/-0.198 and the 0.371, the
% filter's 5-of-163 arithmetic with the +0.55 it restores, the 8.3-against-5.1 pooled estimator, the
% 12% interpolation floor, the 2.6+-2.0 extrapolation break, and the p=1/16 sign test.
% TWO CLAUSES ARE VERBATIM-MANDATED and may not be paraphrased or moved into a caveat list:
% "The flip is an in-distribution result and the scaffold protocol does not certify it" and
% "We measure every solubility result in this paper on a scaffold split".  Both are bolded in the
% source and both print here.  The one MSE contrast the SI does not carry -- 3.33 to 0.30, the
% factor of eleven the filter makes on the clean pole -- is kept for that reason.
The instrument generalises beyond COSMO-SAC, and a second domain runs it where the sign of grounding
can be predicted before the measurement. The closure is a Hammett linear free-energy relationship
$g(\zstar)=\mathrm pK_{a,0}-\rho\,\hammett^\star$, with $\mathrm pK_{a,0}$ and $\rho$ the tabulated
Hansch--Leo--Taft constants of the reaction series, one literature pair per scaffold: $(4.20,1.00)$
benzoic acid, $(9.99,2.23)$ phenol, $(4.60,2.77)$ anilinium, $(5.25,5.90)$ pyridinium. Those pairs
are literature values, unfitted on these data, so $g$ is fixed in the sense
Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp} requires and the same $g$ runs inside every split. It reads two things, so
$\zstar$ is the pair $(s,\hammett^\star)$: the series $s$, which selects that literature pair, and
the substituent constant. The constant is the one learned quantity, the intermediate
$\hat\sigma_{\mathrm H}$ being it predicted from structure. The external reference is the
Hansch--Leo table \cite{hansch1991survey} and the data are the OPERA compilation
\cite{mansouri2019opera} under a uniform, closure-independent quality filter
(\S\ref{sec:si-pka}).

A rule that reads structure alone, never the residual, assigns the reference constant and thereby
defines two poles. $\hammett^\star$ is the sum over substituted ring positions of that substituent's
tabulated constant at that position, $\sigma_m$ meta and $\sigma_p$ para, from a fixed list of
nineteen; an ortho position, and a substituent outside the list, enter as zero. The \emph{clean}
pole is the $158$ records whose every substituted position is meta or para with a tabulated
substituent. The \emph{degraded} pole is the $846$ where at least one is neither ($571$ ortho, $275$
untabulated), and there $\hammett^\star$ is a partial sum over the positions the table can see. The
split cuts across benzenoid and heteroaromatic alike, all four series occurring on both sides, $32$
of the clean pole's records being pyridinium.

The relation's two classic failure modes fall onto the two channels of Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}:
\emph{resonance saturation}, a curvature beyond a linear closure, lands in $\Bclos$, and what the
zeroed positions carry (ortho field and steric effects, and whatever an untabulated substituent
contributes) is information the intermediate lacks and lands in $\Binsuf$. On the clean pole, the
chemistry the relation was built for, the closure predicts to RMSE $0.55$ and the two available
bounds on $\Bclos$ disagree. The binning-free floor $\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ is $-0.2$
($95\%$ CI $[-0.4,-0.0]$) and establishes nothing: the out-of-fold $\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ is
$0.500$ against an $\mathrm{MSE}$ of $0.302$, a difference negative whatever the closure, which says
that a flexible regressor on the closure's own argument predicts worse than the closure does. The
Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}(b) bound runs the other way and is informative: the per-scaffold systematic
offsets reach $0.429$ in $\mathrm pK_a$ on phenol, and the $n$-weighted mean of their squares bounds
$\Bclos$ below at $0.083$, $27\%$ of the pole's total. At least a quarter of the clean pole's error
is closure error, so its specification stays in doubt. On the degraded pole the closure reaches RMSE
$2.26$, of which the out-of-fold within-series regressor places at most $4.5$ of the $5.1$ total in
input insufficiency and hence at least $0.6$ in the closure, both one-sided and in the same
direction as the solubility bounds.

Two properties of that estimator carry into how the poles may be read. It differs from the eight-bin
cell of \S\ref{sec:estimator}, and the functional differs too: these are \emph{floors}
$\mathrm{MSE}-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ on $\Bclos$, establishing $\Bclos>0$ when positive, and not the
separation margin $\mathrm{MSE}-2\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ of Table~\ref{tab:notation}, which would
establish the stronger $\Bclos>\Binsuf$. And conditioning on $s$ is forced, not a conservative
choice: pool the four series and the same estimator returns $8.3$ against the degraded pole's
$\mathrm{MSE}$ of $5.1$, under which $g$ is unmeasurable, Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp} withholds its
licence, and the floor it leaves is vacuous anyway. The folds are free of the leakage
\S\ref{sec:broad} refuses a fitted estimator for on solubility: the regressor reads
$\hammett^\star$ within a series and nothing besides, so a fold hands it only what the closure's own
argument already carries. The quality filter is closure-independent (net formal charge $\neq0$,
applied to both poles, blind to any residual) and drops five pre-ionized microstates, and it decides
the clean pole's level: it moves that pole by a factor of eleven in $\mathrm{MSE}$, $3.33$ to
$0.30$, and leaves the degraded pole where it was, so a large part of the poles' separation in level
is the filter's. On the $163$ rows before it the same floor is $+0.55$ rather than $-0.2$, positive,
establishing $\Bclos>0$ by the other route as well.

The decisive test is the same oracle swap, measured \emph{in-distribution} across $K{=}20$
within-scaffold splits: the learned $\hat\sigma_{\mathrm H}$ replaced by the tabulated
$\sigma_{\mathrm H}^\star$ in the same closure, the series label read from structure in both arms so
the swap moves one coordinate of $\zstar$. It flips. On the clean pole the reference \emph{improves}
accuracy, MAE $0.48$ learned against $0.315$ with the reference, a gap of $-0.165$ at a $90\%$ CI of
$[-0.28,-0.04]$, better in $19$ of $20$ splits and in three of the four series: an internal positive
control on real data, and one on the swap's \emph{sign} and not on the closure's specification,
since the bound above puts at least a quarter of that pole's error in the closure. On the degraded
pole the same swap \emph{degrades} it, $0.83$ against $1.62$, worse in $20$ of $20$ splits and in
all four series, a gap of $+0.79$ $[{+}0.57,{+}0.95]$, the freely learned constant absorbing what
the zeroed positions carry and the tabulated one lacks. The sign of grounding reverses inside one
closure, in the direction the poles' construction fixes in advance: which ring positions the
Hansch--Leo table can see, complete on one pole and a partial sum on the other.
Remark~\ref{prop:tax} supplies the vocabulary and not the prediction.

That the reversal runs through the insufficiency channel, and not through the closure-error
cancellation of \S\ref{sec:surrogate}, is unsupported on the clean side, where the bound above
leaves at least $27\%$ of the error in the closure. On the degraded side it rests on the flip itself
and on how the pole is built; the Jensen bound adds only compatibility, leaving at most $87\%$ of
the error in insufficiency, and an upper bound is as consistent with none of it as with all. The
out-of-fold regressor answers the same question on the same pole at $4.5$ of the $5.1$ and is an
upper bound too, so the two agree while leaving the attribution to rest elsewhere. A competence
sweep adds that the oracle side of the comparison holds fixed across the training budget while the
learned side moves: the learned arm stays above the clean pole's threshold ($\approx0.315$) at all
four budgets swept, and beats the degraded pole's ($1.62$) across the whole interpolation range,
down to $12\%$ of the data, grounding reverting to helping only under scaffold \emph{extrapolation}
where the learned constant breaks ($2.6{\pm}2.0$) back above the threshold, in the mean over six
splits, three of which still have grounding hurting (\S\ref{sec:pka}).

\textbf{The flip is an in-distribution result and the scaffold protocol does
not certify it.} Re-running the same $K{=}20$ certification on a scaffold split rather than a
stratified one, the reference helps on \emph{both} poles: the clean pole's gap is $-0.61$ and the
degraded pole's $-1.10$, with a series-cluster interval of $[-1.86,+0.37]$ and $P=0.28$ there, so
the degraded pole's ``grounding hurts'' half fails to certify, and so does the reversal.
We measure every solubility result in this paper on a scaffold split, and this is the one place the
protocol changes. What the scaffold run confirms is the mechanism and not the flip: the learned
constant breaks under extrapolation and rises above the oracle threshold on both poles. The
clustering unit is the Hammett reaction series, of which only $G{=}4$ exist, so the bootstrap
resolves series-level sign agreement and nothing finer, read as a sign test on four units:
$p{=}1/16$ on the degraded pole, where all four series agree that grounding hurts, and
$p\approx0.31$ on the clean one, where three do. That test carries the degraded pole and not the
positive control, which rests on its splits and its gap instead.

paradox's own regime is a conjecture and the principal open question here. The $n{=}60$ set is all

source-clustered re-estimate lies beyond it, and its margin inverts under a broader ThermoML pull
where the broad set holds steady. Both sets sit outside the deployment regime

\item What the convention rule costs the aggregate (\S\ref{sec:estimator}).
\item That other cells of the map keep their sign as well (\S\ref{sec:broad}).

alone, and the admissibility rule is a stability filter rather than a correction for the search.
Neither test of the search establishes a chemical effect: under the chemistry-blind relabelling null
the shape of the finding actually claimed sits at chance (\S\ref{sec:broad}), and the pre-declared
out-of-sample test returned \emph{not testable}. We therefore state the finding at the level the
instrument licenses on its own rows and \emph{not} as a multiplicity-controlled chemical effect, and

\item The accuracy ceiling standing a factor of five above the label-noise floor (\S\ref{sec:data}).
\item What the three-seed latent-departure result establishes and where it stops
(\S\ref{sec:surrogate}).
\item What bounds the black-box probe (the probe paragraph above).
\item What the closure-fidelity contrast leaves unmeasured, and what would settle it

\item What the deposit leaves uncheckable (the data-availability statement).
\item Two terms the thermodynamics asks for are set to zero in the deployed model, and both are
absorbed at training time by the same learned $\sigma$ head: $\Delta C_p$ in the ideal term,
one-signed and worth a median $0.32$ in \lnx\ where a melting point is measured
(\S\ref{sec:framework}), and the Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term, off in every arm the
paradox is reported on. Both cancel in the evaluation-time substitution and both survive the
training that produced the drift of \S\ref{sec:surrogate}, so the mechanism there reaches beyond the
activity kernel.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% OPENING SENTENCE REWRITTEN 2026-08-12 by the second branch of the Sec. 2.2 pre-commitment: the

Grounding a learned physical intermediate in reference values is two operations rather than one, and here
only one of them carried a sign. The operation
that failed is the one a practitioner reaches for, because it is the one that runs without retraining.
The intermediate paid for the arrangement.
Trained through a model beyond its power to correct, and measured on runs of its own, it stopped reporting the
physical quantity it is named after wherever a reference existed to hold it against. That is the
price of reading a grounded latent as a measurement.

The general statement is a design rule with a test attached. Wherever a predictor composes a fixed
physical model over a learned intermediate for which an external reference exists, that reference
measures whether the fixed model itself limits accuracy, and where it answers, the answer decides the
remedy: a more faithful physical model, the calculation that supplies its reference values included.

The answer comes chemistry by chemistry rather than once for a field, and it comes
one-sided, so what the measurement returns is the chemistry on which better inputs are not the
remedy. What this work establishes about the instrument is the map itself: which cells
it can answer in, and at what size. Whether a latent trained through an imperfect model becomes, in
general, a surrogate for that model's error is the prediction this work leaves testable.
