\begin{abstract}
Grounding a learned physical latent in reference values a black box cannot exploit is a standard recipe
for out-of-distribution prediction, on the expectation that truer inputs help. Routed through a fixed,
misspecified COSMO-SAC closure they do not: a graph network predicts solid--liquid solubility better from
its own learned $\sigma$-profiles (MAE $1.85$) than from the external quantum-chemical reference
(VT-2005, MAE $2.25$), collapsing the arm's $R^2$ to ${\approx}0$. This reference-degradation is
\emph{certified} over three seeds and three injection controls; it is the paper's motivating
\emph{symptom}, and its cause is the contribution, which we rest on the axis where the evidence is clean. Because the learned
latent has an external oracle, the reference-input activity risk splits exactly, with no model fit, into a
closure-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term; on a matched infinite-dilution set the
closure term is the larger on the bounds we can compute (likely; $P{\approx}0.78$, $n{=}60$). On the same
footing ($n{=}44$), holding the learned latent against its reference gives the \emph{best-supported
explanation}: trained end-to-end through the misspecified closure, the latent departs from the physical
quantity into a low-rank, out-of-sample-transferable surrogate that cancels the closure's error rather
than reporting the charge distribution. We grade every claim by strength of evidence
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}): the symptom, and a second certified sign-flip in the pKa domain, anchor the
account; the closure attribution and the surrogate mechanism are its likely, not certified, core. On
solubility the reference substitution falls almost entirely on the solvent (few
drug-like solutes have reference profiles), so we read the solubility paradox as a symptom, not the
carrier of the attribution. We looked for the obvious remedy and did not find it: a fair, validated
comparison of the 2002 and modern (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) kernels does not establish a fidelity gain on
representative data. We make no accuracy claim; the contribution is the compensating-surrogate mechanism
and a reusable instrument for locating a fixed closure's ceiling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% Frame: the field's default is "add physics / ground on real inputs -> better OOD".
% We show a clean counterexample and a measurement that says why.
Coupling a graph encoder to an explicit thermodynamic bottleneck is a common recipe for
out-of-distribution solubility prediction: the model routes its prediction through interpretable
physical factors, each groundable on abundant single-component data a black box cannot exploit. The
expectation is that the closer the physical inputs are to the truth, the better the model predicts. This
expectation fails: feeding our model the external quantum-chemical reference $\sigma$-profile makes its
\emph{solubility} prediction \emph{worse} than its own learned profile does. We treat this
reference-degradation as the motivating \emph{symptom} and rest the causal claims on the axis where the
evidence is clean. On solubility the substitution falls almost entirely on the solvent---few drug-like
solutes have a reference profile---so the clean, per-molecule attribution lives on the activity axis,
where the latent has an external oracle and matched pairs. There the reference-input activity risk splits,
with no model fit, into a closure-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term, and on a matched
infinite-dilution set the closure term is the larger on the bounds we can compute (\S\ref{sec:measure});
and holding the learned latent against its reference gives the mechanism---trained end-to-end through a
misspecified closure, it is driven off the physical quantity into a systematic, low-rank, transferable
compensating surrogate (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}). A fair, validated comparison of
the 2002 and modern (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) kernels, however, does \emph{not} establish that a
higher-fidelity closure removes the penalty on representative data (\S\ref{sec:measure}): we locate
the ceiling but cannot raise it by the standard route. Whether grounding helps is thus tied to
\emph{closure fidelity}---the fidelity of the map that
consumes the grounded inputs---which we probe directly with three real closures, a synthetic sweep,
and a second closure in the pKa domain (\S\ref{sec:pka}); the composed-predictor framing
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) and penalty sign rule give the formal account. Supervising the latent toward
truth points the opposite way, so how the latent is trained is a second lever that interacts with
fidelity---measuring it directly is left to future work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our thesis is that whether grounding a learned latent in reference physics helps or hurts is determined
by the \emph{fidelity of the closure} that consumes the grounded inputs. The causal weight rests on the
activity axis, where the latent has an external oracle and matched pairs: the \emph{structure} of the
compensating-surrogate mechanism (its sign and low-rank, transferable form robust; its magnitude a
three-seed estimate, mean$\pm$sd) and a reusable instrument that attributes the ceiling to the closure.
The reference-degradation itself---the \emph{paradox}---is the motivating symptom; on solubility it is
real and certified as an effect, but it falls almost entirely on the solvent channel, so we do not rest
the attribution on it. A second lever, how the latent is trained, interacts with fidelity---our
warmup-manifold contrast and the supervised, higher-fidelity TeNNet-SAC \cite{yang2025tennet} point
across it---but measuring both levers together is future work.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The mechanism where grounding hurts.} Held against its own reference on matched pairs
  ($n{=}44$), the end-to-end ``physical'' latent is a low-rank, out-of-sample-transferable
  closure-\emph{compensating} surrogate, not the physical quantity---why grounding hurts here, and why a
  supervised-$\sigma$ model finds the opposite (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}).
  \item \textbf{A measurement instrument.} Because the latent $\zstar$ (the reference $\sigma$-profile)
  has external ground truth, the reference-input activity risk splits \emph{exactly}, with no model fit,
  as $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$ (Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}); on the matched regime ($n{=}60$) the closure term is
  the larger on the bounds we can compute (likely; $P_{\mathrm{boot}}\approx0.78$, \S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{The grounding paradox, as motivating symptom.} Substituting reference VT-2005
  $\sigma$-profiles into the fixed COSMO-SAC closure degrades $\lnx$ prediction relative to a free learned
  head---the opposite of the intended benefit ($3$ seeds, $n{=}5608$, three injection controls). The
  effect is certified, but because only a handful of drug-like test solutes have a reference profile the
  substitution lands almost entirely on the solvent, so we read it as the symptom that motivates the
  attribution above, not its carrier (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).
  \item \textbf{No established fidelity lever.} A fair, validated comparison of the 2002 and modern
  (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) kernels does \emph{not} establish a fidelity gain on representative
  infinite-dilution data ($+56\%$ on the curated $n{=}60$ corner with a margin grazing zero, $+3\%$ on
  the representative $n{=}477$, not certified). We locate a ceiling and offer no fix inside this
  closure family (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item \textbf{Not fixable downstream.} A post-hoc output residual recovers calibration but not
  accuracy, so the closure binding cannot be undone downstream---a \emph{structural} ceiling within
  this closure family (\S\ref{sec:gateb},~\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item \textbf{A general account.} The method holds for any composed predictor $\hat y=g(h(x))$ with a
  fixed closure over a learned intermediate; two structural lemmas are in the main text, the
  grounding-gap identity and penalty sign rule in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}. It transfers to a
  synthetic closure sweep (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and a second real closure---pKa through a Hammett
  relationship, where the same oracle swap flips sign across strata (\S\ref{sec:pka}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Problem setup and notation}
\label{sec:setup}
For a solute~$s$ in solvent~$v$ at temperature~$T$, the target is $y=\lnx$, the
log saturated mole fraction. Dissolving a solid trades two costs against each
other---breaking the crystal lattice ($\Phicry$) and mixing the freed molecules
against non-ideal solute--solvent interactions ($\lng$)---and solid--liquid
equilibrium (SLE) sets the log-solubility to their negative sum:
\begin{equation}
  \lnx = -\underbrace{\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\Big(\tfrac1T-\tfrac1\Tm\Big)}_{\Phicry(T)\ \text{(crystal, depends on $s$)}}
         \;-\;\underbrace{\lng(x_2,T)}_{\text{activity, depends on }(s,v)} ,
  \label{eq:sle}
\end{equation}
with the constant-$\Delta C_p$ (heat-capacity-of-fusion) correction omitted for
clarity. The activity term is produced by a \emph{differentiable closure} $g$:
a graph encoder predicts a $\sigma$-profile---the distribution of screening
(surface) charge density over a molecule, the histogram a COSMO model
consumes---for each molecule, and COSMO-SAC (the COnductor-like Screening
Model--Segment Activity Coefficient \cite{lin2002}) maps the pair of profiles to
$\lng$. Write $\zstar$ for the \emph{reference} physical latent, the externally referenced
$\sigma$-profile from the VT-2005 database (the Virginia Tech 2005 compilation of
quantum-chemically computed screening-charge profiles for ${\sim}1{,}400$
compounds \cite{mullins2006}), and $g(\zstar)$ for the closure output when fed the
reference profile. The model's own head instead predicts a \emph{learned} profile
$\hat z$ and evaluates $g(\hat z)$; a matched \emph{DirectGNN} control shares the
encoder but emits $\lnx$ with no closure, and is the reference for ``what the
physics bottleneck costs'' (schematic in SI, Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}).

\paragraph{The oracle substitution.} Feeding $\zstar$ in place of $\hat z$
(a $\sigma$-oracle) isolates the closure from encoder error: if the closure were
well specified, $g(\zstar)$ should be the best available activity estimate. The
paradox is that it is not.

\paragraph{Data.} Solubility measurements are a Bemis--Murcko scaffold split of
BigSolDB \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb} (grouping molecules by ring-system-plus-linker
scaffold, side chains stripped), so test solutes share no scaffold with training;
the activity labels $m$ are experimental infinite-dilution activity coefficients
($\lngi$, IDAC) compiled from ThermoML (the NIST/IUPAC archive of experimental
thermophysical data \cite{frenkel2011thermoml}), and the reference $\sigma$-profiles
$\zstar$ are the VT-2005 database.

\section{Theory: locating a physics prior's ceiling in a composed predictor}
\label{sec:framework}

The results below are stated for a general \emph{composed predictor} $\hat y=g(h(x))$
in which a fixed, non-fitted map $g$---the \emph{closure}---consumes a learned physical
intermediate $z=h(x)$, and are compared against a matched free predictor that shares the
encoder but drops $g$. Solubility, with $g$ the COSMO-SAC closure and $z$ the
$\sigma$-profile, is one instance; \S\ref{sec:pka} gives a second (pKa through a fixed
Hammett closure), and \S\ref{sec:dial} a controlled synthetic family. Nothing in
Lemmas~\ref{prop:ident}--\ref{prop:decomp}, Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, or
Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} is specific to COSMO-SAC. Two quantities organize the section:
the closure/insufficiency split $B=\Bclos+\Binsuf$, which is the assumption-light
\emph{instrument}, and the observable grounding gap $\Gamma$, which is a \emph{symptom} of
closure misspecification whose validity we characterize.

\subsection{Why a free head can compensate: structural non-identifiability}
The crystal/activity split is not identifiable from solubility data alone, which
is precisely why an unconstrained head can silently absorb crystal error into the
activity term.
\begin{lemma}[Structural non-identifiability of the SLE split]\label{prop:ident}
Consider the infinite-dilution SLE observable $\mu(T)=-\Phicry(T)-\lngi(T)$ with
$\Delta C_p=0$, so that $\Phicry(T)=(\dHfus/\Rgas)(1/T-1/\Tm)$ is affine in $u=1/T$,
and a symmetric two-suffix activity class ($\lng=\lngi\,(1-x_2)^2$) in which
$\lngi(T)$ is affine in $u$. Then, for any design with at least two distinct
temperatures, $\mu$ depends on the parameters only through the two-dimensional
statistic (its slope and intercept in $u$), and the activity intercept and slope
already span that plane;
hence the crystal parameters $(\dHfus,\Tm)$ are unidentified, the profiled Fisher
information for $\dHfus$ is exactly zero, and the indeterminacy set is
two-dimensional. The deficiency is closed only by a rank-completing external
constraint (a direct crystal label, or a fixed activity slope). At finite dilution
the composition factor $(1-x_2)^2$ breaks the exact affinity, leaving a non-zero but
near-degenerate information of order $x_2^2$ (numerically, condition number
$2.3\times10^{5}$; \S\ref{sec:ident}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent This is the standard structural-identifiability picture for a
collinear design \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}---equivalently the null-space picture of
regularization theory, in which the component of an inverse fit the data leaves unconstrained
lives in the null space of the forward map and is fixed only by the prior \cite{engl1996,hansen1998},
restated for learned reconstruction as null-space networks \cite{schwab2019}. What is specific to our
setting is that this indeterminate component is not projected out by construction but is left free
under \emph{end-to-end} training of the SLE-coupled latent. We prove it in the SLE form
(App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}) and audit it numerically in \S\ref{sec:ident}. It is
\emph{enabling background},
not our headline: it explains why grounding an under-determined factor on
external single-component data is necessary, and why compensation between the
factors is expected. By itself it says nothing about whether grounding helps.
That depends on whether the closure that consumes the grounded inputs is
correctly specified, which is the question we make measurable next.

The non-identifiability is not binary but graded by the activity class: the efficient
information for $\dHfus$ is zero when the activity nuisance spans the crystal score's directions
$\{1,1/T\}$ and strictly positive (a finite Cram\'er--Rao bound) when the slope is constrained
(Lemma~\ref{prop:semiparam}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). The numerical audit (\S\ref{sec:ident})
shows the honest corollary: merely restricting the activity slope leaves
$\mathrm{sd}(\dHfus)\approx8{,}251$\,J/mol---finite but unusable---whereas external crystal labels,
which contribute a score direction outside the activity tangent space, close it to
$\approx1{,}689$\,J/mol.

\subsection{The closure--insufficiency decomposition: the instrument}
\label{sec:decomp}
Let $m$ be the activity target (experimental $\lngi$) and $g(\zstar)$ the closure
evaluated on the true latent. The risk of the reference-input path---its mean squared error
$\Ex[(m-g(\zstar))^2]$---decomposes exactly into a part the intermediate cannot resolve (the
irreducible Bayes term) and a part the fixed map gets wrong; the excess over the Bayes predictor
$\Ex[m\mid\cdot]$ is the second term alone.

\begin{lemma}[Exact closure--insufficiency decomposition and its one-sided bounds]\label{prop:decomp}
For a \emph{fixed} (non-fitted) closure $g$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(m-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\text{ref.-input MSE}}
  &= \underbrace{\Ex\big[\Var(m\mid \zstar)\big]}_{\Binsuf\ (\text{irreducible})} \\
  &\quad + \underbrace{\Ex\big[(\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\Bclos\ (\text{decoder bias})} .
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The cross term vanishes identically because $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar)$ is
$\zstar$-measurable and $\Ex[(m-\Ex[m\mid\zstar])\mid\zstar]=0$. The split is exact with no fitting of the closure $g$---the cross term vanishes because $g$ is
fixed and $\zstar$ is externally observed---so the total and $\Bclos$'s Jensen lower bound are
fit-free; $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$, however, must be estimated with a conditional-variance
smoother whose choice affects the verdict (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
\end{lemma}
\noindent Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is a bias--variance split of model
discrepancy in the sense of Kennedy--O'Hagan \cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}:
$\Bclos$ is the systematic discrepancy of a fixed physical map, and its dominance over
$\Binsuf$ is the empirical content of ``the ceiling is the closure, not the inputs.'' We
do not claim \eqref{eq:decomp} as new. The affordance is that an externally grounded
$\zstar$ lets us \emph{measure} the split rather than assume it: unlike
model-discrepancy calibration, which \emph{fits} a correction from held-out
observations, here $g$ is fixed and $\zstar$ externally observed, so the map's error is
\emph{attributed}, not corrected---available only where an external latent oracle exists
(LFERs, COSMO / group-contribution closures, thermodynamic cycles), not in gray-box or
PINN hybrids. \textbf{Exactness is contingent on $g$ being fixed}: for a \emph{fitted}
decoder the cross term does not vanish and a plug-in point split is invalid (a spurious
$-0.36$ cross term appears when $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ is replaced by a fitted estimate). We
therefore report \emph{one-sided bounds}, not a point split (each proved in
App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Bclos \ge (\Ex[m]-\Ex[g])^2$ by Jensen: a constant offset between the
  decoder's mean output and the target is misspecification, not input insufficiency, and
  this bound needs \emph{no smoothness assumption}. Its one requirement is that $m$ and $g$
  share the $\lngi$ reference state, which we check directly (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
  \item $\Binsuf \le \Ex[\Var(m\mid \mathrm{bin}(g(\zstar)))]$, a valid
  \emph{upper} bound (law of total variance, since a binning of $g(\zstar)$
  coarsens $\zstar$).
\end{itemize}
The Jensen constant-offset bound is convention-specific---it separates under the full combinatorial
convention but inverts under the deployed residual-only default---so the headline separation in
\S\ref{sec:measure} rests on the leakage-immune law-of-total-variance bound, and the Jensen numbers
are reported in App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}.
Because $\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ depends only on $(m,\zstar)$, it is
\emph{independent of the combinatorial convention} (full vs.\ residual) at fixed $\zstar$:
disagreement between closures that share one reference profile is decoder disagreement and lands
in $\Bclos$. A kernel that carries its own $\sigma$-averaging, by contrast, changes $\zstar$ itself,
so the 2002-vs-2010 contrast (\S\ref{sec:measure}) is not a fixed-input decoder difference.

\paragraph{The instrument's resolution is $\dim(\zstar)/n$.} Whether the split can be
\emph{point}-identified or only bounded is set by the latent dimension relative to sample size.
$\Binsuf=\Ex[\Var(m\mid\zstar)]$ is a conditional variance in $\dim(\zstar)$ dimensions, and
estimating it from $n$ observations is a nonparametric problem governed by $\dim(\zstar)/n$. On the
solubility regime $\zstar$ is a $102$-bin $\sigma$-profile with $n{=}60$ matched pairs
($\dim(\zstar)/n\approx1.7$): the conditional variance is unestimable, so every $\Binsuf$ entry of
Table~\ref{tab:decomp} is a one-sided \emph{bound} and $\Bclos$ is only lower-bounded---the
one-sidedness above is a consequence of this ratio, not a modelling choice. Where the latent is
low-dimensional the same instrument point-identifies: the pKa Hammett constant is a \emph{scalar}
with $n{=}1004$ ($\dim(\zstar)/n\approx10^{-3}$), so $\Binsuf$ is a point estimate (\S\ref{sec:pka}),
not a bound. The decomposition is identifiable exactly where $\dim(\zstar)/n$ is small---which is why
the pKa domain, not the solubility case, is where the split is pinned rather than bounded.

\subsection{What the instrument resolves, and what it does not}
\label{sec:gap}
The decomposition (\S\ref{sec:decomp}) is the load-bearing measurement---assumption-light, needing
neither a representable encoder nor a lossless bottleneck. Alongside it we report a cheaper, purely
observable quantity: the \emph{reference-input penalty} $\Gamma:=R_{\mathrm{orc}}-R_{\mathrm{e2e}}$, the
oracle risk minus the best end-to-end risk; the paradox is $\Gamma>0$. Under a representable encoder
(A1) and a lossless bottleneck (A2) it is sandwiched $0\le\Gamma\le\Bclos$, so an observed $\Gamma>0$
would certify $\Bclos>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). But A1 is empirically
doubtful (the encoder is transfer-limited: a linear probe drops from train $R^2\approx0.76$ to test
$R^2\approx0.45$) and A2 is unverifiable and physically dubious; when A2 fails, $\Gamma>0$ conflates
closure misspecification with input insufficiency---a well-specified closure with a lossy latent gives
$\Bclos=0$ yet $\Gamma>0$ (\S\ref{sec:dial}, and the pKa ortho sweep of \S\ref{sec:pka}). We therefore
read $\Gamma$ as a corroborating \emph{symptom} and rest the closure-dominance verdict on the
decomposition, which needs neither assumption: \emph{the decomposition is the instrument; $\Gamma$ is
the cheap symptom that motivates it}.

\paragraph{When a fixed prior loses; and scope.}\label{sec:tax} The deployed question---does the
\emph{trained} physics model beat the \emph{trained} black box at sample size $n$?---follows a sign
rule. Writing each trained risk as an asymptote plus estimation variance,
$\Ex[\hat R_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)]\approx R_{\mathrm{e2e}}+V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)$ and
$\Ex[\hat R_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]\approx R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}+V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)$ with
$V(n)\downarrow0$, the \emph{asymptotic physics penalty} is
$\mathcal{T}(n)\approx\Delta_\infty+[V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]$ with
$\Delta_\infty:=R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}$.
\begin{proposition}[Sign of the asymptotic physics penalty]\label{prop:tax}
(i)~$\mathcal{T}(n)<0$ (physics helps) iff $V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)>\Delta_\infty$:
the variance a constrained prior saves must exceed its asymptotic bias. (ii)~If
$\Delta_\infty>0$ and the variance gap vanishes ($V_{\mathrm{direct}}-V_{\mathrm{phys}}\to0$),
there is a critical budget $n^\star$ beyond which $\mathcal{T}(n)>0$ for all $n>n^\star$. (iii)~Since
$V_{\mathrm{direct}}-V_{\mathrm{phys}}\le V_{\mathrm{direct}}$, as the black box nears the
aleatoric floor ($V_{\mathrm{direct}}\to0$) the penalty tends to $\Delta_\infty$; this limit is
$\ge0$ when the control is asymptotically Bayes ($R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}=R_{\mathrm{free}}$),
not merely from $V_{\mathrm{direct}}\to0$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent The consequence for grounding is~(ii): a fixed-closure prior with strictly positive asymptotic
bias always loses once the budget passes $n^\star$ (proof in App.~\ref{sec:si-proofs}). There is no
``optimal grounding'' theorem, and the decomposition does not improve out-of-distribution accuracy
(\S\ref{sec:results}). Everything here is measured at infinite
dilution, $298$\,K, on the VT-2005-matchable low-$\gamma$ regime; transfer to the finite-composition
regime the paradox lives in is a conjecture, not a result (\S\ref{sec:scope}). We read these results
against known work on model discrepancy \cite{kennedy2001,brynjarsdottir2014}, parameter sloppiness
\cite{transtrum2015,gutenkunst2007}, identifiability \cite{raue2009,villaverde2016}, misspecification
\cite{white1982}, and underspecification \cite{damour2022}.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The mechanism (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}) and the instrument that measures it (\S\ref{sec:measure}) carry the
paper. Held against its reference on matched pairs, the end-to-end latent is a compensating surrogate that
cancels the closure's error rather than reporting the physical quantity, and the decomposition locates the
ceiling in the low-fidelity closure rather than its inputs. The grounding paradox (\S\ref{sec:paradox}) is
the motivating \emph{symptom} that makes the failure visible, not the carrier of the attribution; whether a
standard higher-fidelity closure (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) moves the ceiling we could not establish on
representative data (\S\ref{sec:measure}). The remaining sections---the synthetic
sweep (\S\ref{sec:dial}), the output-residual control (\S\ref{sec:gateb}), and the pKa closure
(\S\ref{sec:pka})---stress-test and generalize the account, with further diagnostics in the ESI.

Every claim in this paper is graded by strength of evidence---certified (holds under any closure
configuration, exactly proved, or bootstrap-certified with $P\!\to\!1$ across resamples), likely
(measured but sub-threshold or one-sidedly bounded on the matched $n{=}60$/$n{=}44$ sets), or open
(unestablished, directional, or conjectural)---in the ledger at the foot of Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}. We
make no accuracy claim.

\subsection{The grounding paradox}
\label{sec:paradox}

% Triple confirmation: eval-oracle / true-at-train / channel-swap.
Substituting the external reference $\sigma$-profile for the model's learned one degrades its
\emph{solubility} prediction, and it fails the way a \emph{misspecified map} does---not the way missing
information would. The effect erases any positive $R^2$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox}): the model predicts
solubility better \emph{from its own learned $\sigma$-profile} than when \emph{given the external
reference one} (MAE $1.85$ vs $2.25$, in $\lnx$ units), and it survives three controls---the
evaluation-time oracle, injection of the reference profile \emph{at training} (MAE $1.98$), and a
channel-swap ($2.08$), all worse than the learned-$\sigma$ arm---ruling out a wiring
artifact.\footnote{The three oracle-application controls (evaluation-only, training-time injection,
channel-swap; \S\ref{sec:methods}) are computed on the same three-seed cross-arm lock as
Table~\ref{tab:si-arms}; their per-arm predictions are released with the code.} That the
fault is a wrong \emph{map} rather than a missing input is established on the activity axis, where the
reference input is clean: restoring the physically \emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim
combinatorial term (the entropic contribution from solute--solvent size and shape mismatch)
\emph{increases} error, and on $n{=}60$ matched pairs the fixed reference-input closure sits about
$2.4\times$ above the leakage-immune irreducible-variance floor (the load-bearing LOTV bound
$\Binsuf\!\le\!0.62$; a more flexible regressor reaches a smaller, least-conservative floor near $0.40$,
\S\ref{sec:measure}), under-predicting $\lngi$ with a systematic bias (SI, Fig.~\ref{fig:parity}).

\paragraph{Why the solubility paradox is a symptom, not the carrier.} The solubility effect is real and
certified (three seeds, three controls), but it cannot carry the closure-versus-input attribution, for a
data reason. Drug-like solutes are essentially absent from the VT-2005 reference set, so only a handful of
test solutes have a reference $\sigma$-profile at all; on solubility the reference substitution therefore
falls almost entirely on the \emph{solvent} channel, and the both-channel substitution the attribution
would need is available for too few solutes to support a per-molecule conclusion (a fresh $2\times2$
channel-substitution test lands on ${\approx}7$ solutes, too few to separate the cause either way).
Computing reference profiles for drug-like solutes is possible in principle but costs hundreds of
quantum-chemistry runs on large, multi-conformer molecules, for an effect already certified as a
phenomenon---not worth it. We therefore carry the causal claim on the activity axis, where the reference
input is clean and matched: the decomposition ($n{=}60$, \S\ref{sec:measure}) and the compensating
surrogate ($n{=}44$, \S\ref{sec:surrogate}).

We separate the load-bearing evidence---a bound that holds under \emph{any} COSMO-SAC
configuration---from corroborating checks that are robust across the conventions we tried but not
strictly independent of the model fit.

\paragraph{The load-bearing separation (leakage-immune).} Subtracting the leakage-immune
law-of-total-variance $\Binsuf$ upper bound (the most conservative estimator; the $z^\ast$-neighbour
$k$-NN/RF estimators are deflated by near-duplicate leakage on the crossed $41$-solute$\times17$-solvent
design and only \emph{bracket} $\Binsuf$) from the total error gives, on the deployed residual-only
convention, $\Bclos\gtrsim0.85$ against $\Binsuf\lesssim0.62$ (Table~\ref{tab:decomp}). This is the
claim we lean on. Because that bound subtracts a \emph{population upper} estimate of $\Binsuf$, it
understates $\Bclos$ in expectation, so a surviving margin survives a fortiori. The finite-sample biases
run the other way and are controlled: the deflated $k$-NN difference is kept out of the bound, and the
LOTV binning's per-bin sparsity bias (which can under-estimate $\Binsuf$, and so inflate $\Bclos$) is
cross-checked against the sparsity-immune RF and ridge out-of-fold estimators (\S\ref{sec:si-tables}). It is stable---surviving leave-one-solvent, -solute, and -pair folds and adversarial
deletion of the eight highest-error pairs---but not certified: in a two-way cluster
bootstrap $\Bclos$ exceeds $\Binsuf$ in $78\%$ of resamples (a resampling frequency $P_{\mathrm{boot}}$,
not a posterior probability of the inequality), with the $90\%$ margin spanning zero; under one
deliberately aggressive out-of-fold ridge smoother the ordering even inverts ($\Binsuf{=}0.88>\Bclos{=}0.59$,
a sensitivity edge, App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}), so ``the larger'' is estimator-conditional. With only
$17$/$41$ clusters this small-$G$ fraction is a coarse band (mid-$0.7$s to high-$0.8$s), so we present
the separation as a
\emph{supporting} measurement---likely but not certified---not a stand-alone certificate. The
convention, leakage, $\gamma$-stratification, and leverage stress-tests are in
App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}. Label noise is unestimable here (replicate-free) and assumed zero; the bounds
are invariant to zero-mean noise, and between-method bias does not arise because the matched IDAC is
essentially single-method ($158/165$ gas chromatography), leaving only a within-method systematic
exposure that against the ${\approx}0.23\,(\ln\gamma)^2$ margin would have to be implausibly large to
close the separation.

\paragraph{The closure defect is low-rank and correctable.} That $\Bclos$ dominates locates the
ceiling in the map; it does not say the map is \emph{fixably} wrong. It is. A small
structure-preserving residual on the fixed COSMO-SAC exchange-energy kernel
($\Delta=B\,\mathrm{diag}(a)\,B^{\!\top}$ over the $\sigma$-grid, symmetric, zero-initialised), fit on
the \emph{reference} VT-2005 profiles, removes $49\%$ of the total reference-input error out-of-sample (five-fold
cross-validated, activity MSE $1.47\!\to\!0.75$---most of the reducible closure component above
the $\Binsuf$ floor) at \emph{rank one} ($52$ parameters; rank~2 adds
nothing, rank~3 over-fits). At $52$ parameters against $n{=}60$ this is provisional, and we have not
separated the kernel correction from a per-solvent offset. So $\Bclos$ is not diffuse model error but
a low-dimensional, correctable defect of the map---addressable with a handful of parameters on the
closure, whereas the same error is only made worse by feeding the inputs truthfully
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}) and untouched by an output residual (\S\ref{sec:gateb}); whether an end-to-end
model realises the gain is a compute-gated question we leave open.

\paragraph{Where the closure fails, chemically.} The residual error is not an implementation
artifact: our differentiable layer reproduces the NIST benchmark COSMO-SAC-2002~\cite{bell2020cosmosac}
to RMSE $0.003\,\ln\gamma$ on identical VT-2005 profiles, with self-solvation $\ln\gamma^\infty(i,i)$
exactly zero. Yet $90\%$ of the squared error falls on strong hydrogen-bond-donor solvents
(acceptor-only near-exact), placing $\Bclos$ squarely in COSMO-SAC's documented single-parameter
H-bond kernel---a known limitation, not a layer idiosyncrasy, and the specific defect the
donor/acceptor-typed 2010 closure was built to fix. Convention-robustly, adding the physically
\emph{more} complete Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term \emph{increases} error ($1.47\to1.80$)
on size-asymmetric pairs---what a misspecified map does, not what missing information would (details in
App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}).

\paragraph{Looking for the lever, and not finding it.} If the ceiling is the closure, the obvious
remedy is a better closure. We tested this directly and report a negative result.

Two corrections to our earlier reading come first. \emph{(i)}~The NIST reference implementation of
COSMO-SAC-2002, when handed a typed three-profile database, reads the nonpolar channel only and
silently discards the donor and acceptor profiles while retaining the total area as prefactor. The
figure we previously reported for the 2002 kernel on the UD (University of Delaware) profiles ($\mathrm{MSE}\ 1.26$) is that
crippled arm, not the reference closure; it is also not comparable to the $1.47$ we report for our own
layer on the single-profile VT-2005 database. The fair full 2002 on the same UD data scores
$\mathrm{MSE}\ 1.757$, which we validate two independent ways: our differentiable layer, and the NIST
reference itself fed the same profile reformatted into the three-profile layout ($1.758$).

\emph{(ii)}~There is consequently \textbf{no input-fixed design for this comparison, even in
principle}, and we withdraw the claim that it is insufficiency-free by construction. The $\sigma$-profile
averaging is part of each model's \emph{definition}---Mullins averaging for 2002, Hsieh averaging for
2010, as specified by the reference implementation and the models' own
authors~\cite{bell2020cosmosac}---so a single profile cannot be held fixed across the two kernels
without violating one of them. Forcing the profile literally identical denies the 2002 kernel two of
three channels, an input-insufficiency confound in precisely the term the decomposition of
\S\ref{sec:decomp} exists to isolate; ``insufficiency-free by construction'' is therefore incoherent,
not merely unverified. Each closure evaluated on its prescribed native profile is the specified use of
the model, not a convention compromise, so the fair values $1.757$ and $1.911$ are the correct
comparison rather than caveated ones.

With that settled, the swap is measurable. On the matched corner the fair 2002 $\to$ 2010/dsp swap
cuts the activity error from $1.757$ to $0.765$ ($n{=}60$; the two-way cluster bootstrap gives
$P=0.98$ with a $90\%$ margin of $[+0.05,+2.16]$, i.e.\ grazing zero). On a representative
IDAC$\,\cap\,$UD set---all temperatures, dispersion enabled, every arm validated against the NIST
reference---the same swap gives $1.911\to1.855$ ($n{=}477$; $P=0.62$, margin $[-2.25,+1.31]$).

\textbf{The two measurements are statistically compatible with a single small effect:} each interval
contains the other's point estimate. The two headline claims do not treat this corner inconsistently.
Contribution~(b), that the closure is the ceiling, is a property of the \emph{deployed} 2002 kernel,
whose fair error $1.757$ sits above the insufficiency floor here; it does not depend on any upgrade.
Contribution~(c), that no fidelity lever is established, is the separate question of whether
\emph{upgrading} the kernel removes that ceiling---and the corner's large $2002\to2010$/dsp gain
($1.757\to0.765$, $P=0.98$) does not carry to the representative set ($n{=}477$, $P=0.62$). So the corner
is authoritative for what the deployed closure does, not for what an upgrade buys. We therefore do not
read this as a lever that exists on our corner and fails to generalize, but as one small, unestablished
effect of which the corner supplies a low-power estimate.

This is consistent with the published behaviour of the closure family rather than a challenge to it,
and the comparison itself appears to be new: no large-scale infinite-dilution benchmark of the
$2002\to2010$ step exists. The comprehensive assessment of this family~\cite{fingerhut2017}
($29{,}173$ $\gamma^\infty$ points) evaluates COSMO-SAC-2010 and COSMO-SAC-dsp only, and its largest
documented infinite-dilution step---adding the entire dispersion term, $2010\to$\,dsp---buys a
${\sim}10$-point mean-absolute-deviation gain ($95\%\to86\%$), with a \emph{reversal} on aqueous
systems where dsp is worse than 2010 ($203\%$ vs.\ $194\%$). Our null for the smaller $2002\to2010$
step is in family with those magnitudes, and non-monotone progress across strata is documented
behaviour, not an anomaly. Two things follow. COSMO-SAC-2010's demonstrated gains are concentrated in
liquid--liquid equilibria and in the temperature dependence of the electrostatic term, where an
infinite-dilution corner has no leverage. And the family's \emph{best} infinite-dilution accuracy---%
$86\%$ MAD on $29{,}173$ points in the hands of its own developers---is itself external evidence that
the closure is the low-fidelity component: the ceiling we locate is where the field's own benchmark
places it. We claim only that \textbf{we cannot establish a closure-fidelity lever on the data we can
compute}, not that the modern closure is not an improvement.

The consequence for the paper is that we have no fix to offer. The ceiling we locate is not disposed
of by the standard upgrade path, and the search for a remedy inside this closure family came back
empty.

\paragraph{The reference is itself imperfect, but conservatively so.} The external ground truth used
to charge $\Bclos$ against $\Binsuf$ is quantum chemistry, not experiment: the VT-2005 profiles are
computed for a single reference conformer and protonation state (\S\ref{sec:si-repro}); that this
matters is documented rather than hypothetical---the successor Delaware database revises the VT-2005
molecular conformations for about a third of its compounds~\cite{bell2020cosmosac}. Any mismatch
between that fixed state and the conformer/tautomer population under the measurement conditions enters
as noise in the reference $\zstar$, and such noise raises $\Binsuf$---the input-sufficiency term---not
$\Bclos$. It therefore works against the closure-dominance reading rather than for it: a truer,
condition-matched reference would lower $\Binsuf$ and widen the margin we already find, so the verdict
is conservative to this defect.

\paragraph{Scope.} This measurement is a low-$\gamma$/$298$\,K/VT-2005-matchable regime and does not
transfer to the finite-composition regime the paradox lives in (\S\ref{sec:scope}); the full audit is
in App.~\ref{sec:si-tables}.

\subsection{Isn't this just a bad closure?}
\label{sec:badclosure}
Yes---and that is the contribution, not the objection. COSMO-SAC-2002 has a documented
single-parameter hydrogen-bond kernel, and the 2010/dsp closure was built to fix exactly that defect;
we do not dispute this---it is a well-documented defect, though on our data upgrading the closure did
not establish a fidelity gain (\S\ref{sec:measure}). But the 2002 kernel is not an exotic choice---it is the closure a
practitioner reaches for, and its fidelity is \emph{not knowable in advance} on a new system. The field
routinely composes an expressive learned encoder with a fixed mechanistic map and assumes the map is
good enough. What we show is what happens when it is not: the encoder silently compensates, the
resulting latent is a surrogate rather than the physical quantity it is named after
(\S\ref{sec:surrogate}), and the interpretability the bottleneck appeared to buy is absent---none of it
visible without an external oracle. Our decomposition is how you make it visible on \emph{your} closure,
before its misspecification has quietly eaten your result. The practical consequence inverts the usual
instinct: before investing in richer inputs, measure the split---within a closure family the payoff is
in the closure.

\subsection{The ceiling is not fixable downstream}

\subsection{What we do not measure, and why}
\label{sec:scope}
The paper's strongest claim and its causal attribution live in \emph{different regimes}, and we state
the gap plainly rather than distribute it across scope footnotes. The grounding paradox is measured
where the model operates---finite composition, all $\gamma$, $n{=}5608$. Its \emph{cause}---the
closure/insufficiency attribution (\S\ref{sec:measure}) and the compensating-surrogate mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:surrogate})---is measured only on the matched external-reference regime: infinite
dilution, $298$\,K, low $\gamma$, $n{=}60$ (attribution) and $n{=}44$ (mechanism). So the cause of the
headline result is \emph{not} measured in the headline's own regime; we transfer it by in-regime
signs---the more-physics-worse effect on the full $n{=}5608$ set and the chemistry-resolved structure
(\S\ref{sec:map})---which is a conjecture, not a measured fact, and is the first place a referee
should press. Two structural limits keep us from closing it. The external-reference regime cannot be
enlarged: only $74$ VT-2005-matchable IDAC pairs exist ($60$ at $298$\,K plus $14$ off-temperature),
so the $n{=}60$ attribution cannot be pushed to significance on this data; and the matched IDAC is
essentially single-method (gas chromatography), so a method-stratified re-estimate is unavailable.
We therefore present the attribution as a bounded demonstration on the matched corner and mark both
its regime transfer and the absence of an established closure-fidelity lever (\S\ref{sec:measure}) as
the open questions.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The result is a cautionary measurement for physics-informed modeling: grounding a
model on truer physical inputs is only as good as the closure that consumes them,
and when that closure is a fixed map of unknown fidelity---one we found we could not raise by the
standard route---better inputs surface its bias rather than help. The external latent does not make
the physics path win. Used as an instrument on the regime we can measure, the decomposition places
the ceiling in the closure rather than its inputs; but we looked for the payoff in the closure and did
not find it: a fair, fully validated comparison of the 2002 and 2010/dsp kernels on representative
infinite-dilution data does not establish a fidelity gain. We locate a ceiling and offer no lever. This attribution is a demonstration on a low-$\gamma$,
infinite-dilution regime, not a certificate: we do not claim it transfers unchanged to the
finite-composition regime the paradox spans, where its shared closure-misspecification cause is a
conjecture supported by in-regime signs (the more-physics-worse effect on the full $n{=}5608$ set and
the chemistry-resolved structure), not a measured fact.

\paragraph{The accuracy case, and its boundary.} That the physics prior does not improve accuracy has
no confirmed exception: the grounded model trails the matched black box on the full corpus, the
external SOTA (FastSolv, SolProp) does no better on a difficulty-bounding by-solute split
(App.~\ref{sec:si-baselines}), and grounding the crystal factor on external labels did not move the
ceiling (\S\ref{sec:e2}). The one place the physical \emph{structure} helps is temperature
extrapolation---but as a hypothesis-class property the trained models do not exploit
(\S\ref{sec:supporting}). These point to a \emph{structural} ceiling: the crystal/activity split is
non-identifiable from solubility alone (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}), and the activity closure is
misspecified where we can measure it (\S\ref{sec:measure}), so within this family better inputs, more
data, and auxiliary grounding do not recover an accuracy the factorization cannot express. What a
misspecified closure costs is thus not only accuracy but the interpretability the bottleneck appeared
to buy.

\paragraph{When a fixed-closure prior helps versus hurts.} The penalty sign rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}) predicts
\emph{when} to expect these negatives: a fixed-closure prior helps only when the estimation variance it
saves exceeds its asymptotic bias $\Delta_\infty$ (both measurable). The ``grounding helps iff the
closure is well specified'' reading is thus a statement about the \emph{bias} channel ($\Delta_\infty$);
even a correctly specified or true-valued input need not win on total error, because a fitted nuisance
can be the more \emph{efficient} estimator---the semiparametric phenomenon by which an estimated
propensity beats the known-true one \cite{hirano2003}, sharpest where plug-in efficiency is delicate
\cite{robins1997}---and Prop.~\ref{prop:tax} already carries this, its variance term overturning the
sign the bias term alone would predict. The variance benefit is largest at
small $n$ and vanishes as data accumulate or as the black box nears the aleatoric floor, whereas
$\Delta_\infty$ is set by the closure; a prior is therefore predicted to hurt precisely in the
mature-data, low-noise, well-covered regime. The synthetic sweep (\S\ref{sec:dial}) and the pKa closure
(\S\ref{sec:pka}) exercise the fidelity and sufficiency behaviour directly. Whether grounding helps is
shaped by \emph{two} axes---closure fidelity and how the latent is trained (supervised vs.\
end-to-end); characterising that plane in full, and sweeping each axis directly on real systems, is deferred
to future work.

\paragraph{The novelty inversion (exploratory).} The sharpest---and most unexpected---confirmation of
the penalty sign rule runs opposite to the usual coverage intuition. Resolving the asymptotic physics penalty by solute
novelty, the closure is neutral on the most \emph{novel} solutes (nearest-train Tanimoto $\le0.3$:
$\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=-0.04\pm0.18$) and heaviest on the most \emph{familiar}, best-covered ones
(Tanimoto $>0.8$: $+0.51\pm0.09$)---exactly as Proposition~\ref{prop:tax} predicts, since the variance
a prior saves vanishes precisely where the black box is already accurate, leaving a structural closure
bias as the dominant residual. The penalty likewise concentrates in strongly hydrogen-bonding solvents
(water $+0.52\pm0.35$) and reverses sign in the acceptor-dominated amides ($-0.22\pm0.11$) the closure
was built for (\S\ref{sec:map}). Both patterns are exploratory---no multiplicity control across strata
families---but they land where the penalty sign rule predicts, and where much of modern machine learning
operates. We claim no universal boundary; we \emph{propose}, but do not here cleanly validate, a diagnostic for the sign of the asymptotic physics penalty:
measure $\Bclos$ through the decomposition, read $\Delta_\infty$ off the large-$n$ asymptote, and
the sign should follow. In this sense the failure is not idiosyncratic to
solubility; it recurs whenever a misspecified closure is coupled to an expressive encoder on
abundant, low-noise data.


\paragraph{Limitations.} (i)~The phenomenon and its causal attribution live in \emph{different
regimes}: the paradox is a finite-composition solubility effect, but the closure--insufficiency
measurement is made on the infinite-dilution/$298$\,K/low-$\gamma$ VT-2005-matchable regime, so the
transfer of the attribution back to the paradox's own regime is argued from in-regime signs, not
measured there (\S\ref{sec:scope}); moreover, because few drug-like solutes have a VT-2005 reference
profile, the solubility substitution falls almost entirely on the solvent channel, so the clean
both-channel evidence is on the activity axis rather than the solubility axis. (ii)~$\Binsuf$ is upper-bounded, not point-identified (no
replicates at fixed $\zstar$); the smoothness-free Jensen bound assumes $m$ and $g$ share the $\lngi$
reference state, which we verify (\S\ref{sec:measure}), so the mean offset is genuine decoder bias, not
a units artifact. (iii)~The constant-offset margin is convention-specific and inverts under the
deployed residual-only default; there the separation rests on the convention-independent bound and the
more-physics-worse effect. (iv)~Absolute scaffold
accuracy is encoder-bound but well \emph{above} the inter-lab noise floor ($0.15$--$0.31$ in $\lnx$;
\S\ref{sec:supporting}), so label noise does not explain the ceiling. (v)~The compensating-surrogate
result is three-seed mean$\pm$sd ($n{=}44$; \S\ref{sec:surrogate}); its load-bearing content is the
\emph{sign} and low-rank, transferable \emph{structure}, with the magnitudes
($51\pm2\%$/$72\pm1\%$/$4.1\pm0.5\times$) reported but varying across pipeline versions.
(vi)~The closure-fidelity contrast is measured on the activity axis; we have \emph{not} tested whether
the \emph{solubility} paradox itself survives a modern $2010$/dsp closure. That test needs a typed
(donor/acceptor) $\sigma$-profile head and a UD$\,\cap\,$BigSolDB matched set, and is the natural next
experiment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Feeding a physics-informed solubility model its reference physical inputs made it
worse. What the paper contributes is a way to measure that failure: with an
externally grounded latent, the reference-input risk splits into a
decoder-misspecification term and an input-insufficiency term, and on the
subset we can measure, the ceiling is most likely
the closure rather than the inputs ($P_{\mathrm{boot}}\approx0.78$ under two-way clustering, not certified at
conventional confidence). The
practical implication is a caution, not a fix: within this closure, truer
inputs surface its bias and an output-space correction is inert, and a fair, validated comparison of
the 2002 and modern (COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp) kernels does not establish that upgrading the closure removes
the penalty on representative data. We locate a ceiling and offer no lever; supervising the latent
toward truth may be a second route, which we leave to future work. The mechanism behind the ceiling is
the paper's central finding: on matched pairs the learned $\sigma$-profile is a closure-compensating
surrogate rather than the physical quantity---an interpretability cost of physics-informed bottlenecks
that the external latent makes measurable, a $51\pm2\%$, low-rank, transferable departure from truth
across three seeds.
