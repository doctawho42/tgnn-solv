\subsection{Whether the unconstrained control represents the thermodynamics}
\label{sec:blackbox}
% Artifacts: results/blackbox/{RESULT_study1a_2026-07-26,RESULT_study2,RESULT_study4}.md and the
% JSONs beside them, via scripts/experiments/blackbox_study{1a,2,4}.py on the same three
% current-split DirectGNN checkpoints used for the controlled comparison (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).
The physics arm's intermediate is a compensating surrogate rather than the quantity it is named
after (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}). The unconstrained control is free to represent the same
thermodynamics implicitly, and whether it does is a separate, testable question. We tested it on
the three DirectGNN checkpoints of the controlled comparison, on the same scaffold split and
against the same external references. It does not.

\paragraph{The crystal term is not recoverable from the representation.} The probe target is the
measured ideal-solubility term $\Phi^{*}(\text{mol},T)$; the input is the $788$-dimensional pair
representation entering the prediction head, located by a forward pre-hook and measured rather
than inferred from configuration. Ridge regression is fitted on $152$ solutes and scored on $13$
held out with no solute in common, with the penalty chosen by solute-grouped cross-validation on
the fitting split alone and read against a $1000$-draw null that permutes molecular identity while
preserving each solute's temperatures. Held-out $R^{2}$ is $-0.51$, $+0.11$ and $+0.03$ across the
three seeds, none significant, with the jackknife over held-out solutes changing sign in two seeds.
The same probe applied to quantities the representation necessarily carries recovers the model's
own prediction at $R^{2}=0.98$ and temperature at $R^{2}=0.9997$, which establishes that the
extraction and the estimator are sound and the null is a property of the representation. The
permutation null itself reaches $R^{2}\approx0.32$--$0.38$ at this sample size, so the result is an
upper bound: an effect of $R^{2}\gtrsim0.30$ would have been detected.

\paragraph{The temperature response does not expose it either, and neither do the data.} The
sharper form of the question is whether $\partial\lnx/\partial(1/T)$ recovers
$-\Delta H_{\text{fus}}/R$. The SLE identity places the activity term in the same slope, so this is
a property of the corpus before it is a property of any model. Over $1353$ solute--solvent pairs
spanning $151$ solutes, the empirical slope correlates with $-\Delta H_{\text{fus}}/R$ at
$r=+0.24$. The slopes are sharply determined (median $|\text{slope}|/\mathrm{SE}=49$, one pair of
$1353$ wrong-signed), so the weak correlation is structural and not a resolution limit: a model
reproducing the data exactly would score $+0.24$. The model tracks the observed slope at
$r=0.20$--$0.41$ with a median error near $30\%$, and its response is about three times more curved
in $1/T$ than the measurements, so it does not represent solubility in a van't Hoff form.

\paragraph{The representation is organised by scaffold.} This question needs no external
reference and is therefore measured at full test size: $5608$ rows, $147$ solutes, $124$
Bemis--Murcko scaffolds, $70$ solvents. Each factor's share of representation variance is reported
as an excess over a permutation null matched to the level at which that factor varies
(App.~\ref{sec:si-blackbox}). Solute identity accounts for $0.59$--$0.61$ and scaffold for
$0.59$--$0.61$: scaffold captures $99.8$--$100.3\%$ of what full molecular identity captures, in
every seed. The representation does not resolve molecules that share a scaffold, and the effect is
specific to scaffold rather than to any partition of the solutes ($+0.082$ against a solute-level
null, the same value in all three seeds). Solvent identity accounts for $0.27$--$0.30$, temperature
for $0.03$--$0.04$, and crude physical character for less: $\log P$, topological polar surface area
and molecular weight each take under $14\%$ of the solute-level structure.

This is the mechanism of the transfer limitation reported in \S\ref{sec:gap}. A test molecule on an
unseen scaffold is not a small extrapolation but a point off the axis along which the
representation is organised. The same measurement separates two properties that are easily
conflated: temperature is decodable from this vector at $R^{2}=0.9997$ while occupying $3$--$4\%$
of its variance. Decodability of a quantity is therefore not evidence that a representation is
organised around it.
