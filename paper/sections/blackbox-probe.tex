\subsection{Does the black-box control carry the thermodynamics instead?}
\label{sec:blackbox}
% Artifacts: results/blackbox/{RESULT_study1a_2026-07-26,RESULT_study2,RESULT_study4}.md and the
% JSONs beside them, via scripts/experiments/blackbox_study{1a,2,4}.py on the same three
% current-split DirectGNN checkpoints used for the controlled comparison (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).
The physics arm's intermediate is a compensating surrogate (\S\ref{sec:surrogate}). A natural
reply is that the thermodynamics has not disappeared but moved: the unconstrained control might
represent the two SLE terms implicitly, having been given the freedom to do so. We tested that on
the same three DirectGNN checkpoints, the same scaffold split, using the same external oracles,
and the answer is no---with the bound stated, because the oracle is small.

\paragraph{The crystal term is not recoverable from the representation.} We probe the $788$-bin
pair representation entering the prediction head\footnote{Located by a forward pre-hook; the model
exposes no pooled ``pair'' module, and its dimension is measured rather than inferred from
configuration.} for the measured ideal-solubility term $\Phi^{*}(\text{mol},T)$, fitting on $152$
solutes and scoring on $13$ held out with zero solute overlap. Ridge, $\alpha$ chosen by
solute-grouped cross-validation on the fitting split alone, read against a $1000$-draw null that
permutes molecular \emph{identity} while preserving each solute's temperatures. Held-out
$R^{2}=-0.51/+0.11/+0.03$ across seeds; no seed is significant, and the jackknife over held-out
solutes changes sign in two of the three. What licenses reading this as a negative rather than as
a broken pipeline is a positive control on the same rows: the same probe recovers the model's own
prediction at $R^{2}=0.98$ and temperature at $R^{2}=0.9997$. The null itself reaches
$R^{2}\approx0.32$--$0.38$ at this sample size, so the claim is a bound, not a point---an effect of
$R^{2}\gtrsim0.30$ would have been seen and was not, and a weaker one is beyond what $13$ solutes
can resolve.

\paragraph{Neither is it visible in the temperature response, and the data barely expose it.} The
sharper form of the question---does $\partial\lnx/\partial(1/T)$ recover $-\Delta H_{\text{fus}}/R$
---turns out to be a question about the corpus before it is one about the model, since the SLE
identity puts the activity term in the same slope. Measured on the data alone, over $1353$
solute--solvent pairs spanning $151$ solutes, the empirical slope correlates with
$-\Delta H_{\text{fus}}/R$ at only $r=+0.24$; the slopes themselves are sharply determined (median
$|\text{slope}|/\mathrm{SE}=49$, one pair of $1353$ wrong-signed), so this is structure and not
noise. A model reproducing the data exactly would therefore score $+0.24$, not $1$. On the $13$
held-out solutes that ceiling is not distinguishable from zero, so the model-side test is
unanswerable there; what can be said is that the model tracks the observed slope loosely
($r=0.20$--$0.41$, $\approx30\%$ median error) and that its response is about three times more
curved in $1/T$ than the measurements, so it is not representing solubility in a van't Hoff-like
form.

\paragraph{What the representation \emph{is} organised by: scaffold.} Freed from the oracle, the
same question runs on the whole test set ($5608$ rows, $147$ solutes, $124$ Bemis--Murcko
scaffolds, $70$ solvents). Reporting each factor's share of representation variance as an excess
over a permutation null matched to the level at which that factor varies,\footnote{The level
matters: $\eta^{2}$ is invariant to relabelling groups, so permuting an identity factor at its own
level is a no-op, while a row-level null for a factor that is constant within a solute destroys
the block structure and inflates it. Identity factors take the row-level null, factors derived
from identity the solute-level one.} solute identity accounts for $0.59$--$0.61$ and scaffold for
$0.59$--$0.61$: scaffold captures $99.8$--$100.3\%$ of what full molecular identity captures, in
every seed. The representation does not resolve molecules sharing a scaffold, and the effect is
specifically scaffold rather than any solute partition ($+0.082$ against a solute-level null, the
same value in all three seeds). Solvent identity accounts for $0.27$--$0.30$, and crude physical
character for very little---$\log P$, TPSA and molecular weight each take under $14\%$ of the
solute-level structure.

This supplies the mechanism behind a limitation we asserted but did not explain
(\S\ref{sec:gap}): the encoder is transfer-limited because a test molecule on an unseen scaffold
is not a mild extrapolation but a point off the axis along which the representation is organised
at all. It also separates two things that probing work often conflates. Temperature is decodable
from this vector at $R^{2}=0.9997$ and occupies $3$--$4\%$ of its variance; both are true, and
only the second describes how the model is arranged. A probe recovering a quantity is not evidence
that the representation is built around it.

\paragraph{Scope.} The oracle-based half of this rests on $13$ held-out solutes and cannot be
enlarged: the molecules carrying both a measured crystal and a measured activity reference are
$25$ common solvents supplying $66\%$ of the training rows, so they cannot be held out of a
solubility model at all. The scaffold result does not depend on the oracle and is measured at full
test size. Full tables, nulls and controls are in App.~\ref{sec:si-blackbox}.
