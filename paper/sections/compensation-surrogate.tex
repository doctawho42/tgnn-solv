\subsection{What the learned intermediate is: a closure-compensating surrogate}
\label{sec:surrogate}
% Artifact: results/compensation/{surrogate_vs_true,isolation}.json via
% scripts/analysis/run_compensation_surrogate.py on the n=44 matched VT-2005 set.
If the closure is the binding term (\S\ref{sec:measure}), the learned intermediate is under
pressure not to be physically faithful but to \emph{cancel} the closure's error. We can see this
directly, because the same matched set that carries external ground truth for the decomposition
also lets us compare the model's predicted $\sigma$-profile $\hat\sigma$ against the true VT-2005
profile $\sigma$ molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$ with both available).

\paragraph{The learned profile is not the physical one, and not by noise.}
The deviation is large and structured. Averaged over the matched molecules, $\hat\sigma$ differs
from $\sigma$ by $\lVert\hat\sigma-\sigma\rVert/\lVert\sigma\rVert\approx0.82$---the learned
``physical'' profile is off by the order of its own magnitude. Crucially the deviations are
\emph{low-rank}: their first two principal components carry $66\%$ of the variance (isotropic noise
over $51$ bins would give ${\approx}4\%$), so $\hat\sigma-\sigma$ is a systematic distortion, not
per-molecule error. Its dominant mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) is a consistent transfer of
area \emph{out of} the nonpolar bins ($\sigma\!\approx\!0$) and \emph{into} the polar wings
($|\sigma|$ large): the network systematically makes molecules look more polar than they are.

\paragraph{It is a transferable closure-inverse, not a per-molecule fit.}
That the deviation compensates the closure is nearly tautological in-distribution (a model that fits
$m$ through $g(\hat\sigma)$ makes $\hat\sigma$ whatever cancels $g$'s error); the non-trivial content
is that the compensation is a single \emph{transferable} transform. Fitting one affine distortion
$D$ on half the molecules ($\hat\sigma\!\approx\!D\sigma$) and testing on the held-out half, $D\sigma$
predicts $\hat\sigma$ \emph{$16\times$} better than the true $\sigma$ does out-of-sample. So
$\hat\sigma$ is not $\sigma$ plus noise; it is a consistent, generalizable closure-inverse of it---one
systematic way the intermediate ``lies'' to the closure, shared across chemistry.

\paragraph{The distortion is driven by the objective, not the lack of grounding.}
One could object that $\hat\sigma$ deviates only because nothing asked it to be physical. It is not
that: a $\sigma$-grounded model (a sigma-profile warm-up on external single-component labels) brings
$\hat\sigma$ to within $26\%$ of the true profile, so the intermediate \emph{can} be physical. The
$82\%$ divergence belongs to the solubility-trained model, and the SLE-induced distortion
(the grounded-to-trained difference) is itself systematic---top-two-mode variance $77\%$, an affine
transfer $26\times$ better than identity out-of-sample---and carries the same nonpolar$\to$polar
signature. The learned profile is pulled off physical truth by the fitting objective, in a direction
that inflates the segment interactions the misspecified exchange kernel under-weights, cancelling part
of $\Bclos$: the input compensating the map, exactly as the non-identifiability of the split predicts
(Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}).

\paragraph{Consequence: the physical readout is illusory where it matters.}
This reframes what a COSMO-SAC-over-$\hat\sigma$ model is doing. Its ``physical'' intermediate is not
the molecule's charge distribution; it is a closure-inverse surrogate, and the surrogate departs from
truth precisely in the polar / hydrogen-bonding region that carries the chemistry a $\sigma$-profile
is read for. Reading $\hat\sigma$ as an interpretable physical quantity is therefore systematically
misleading in the misspecified regime---the interpretability a physics-informed bottleneck appears to
offer is not guaranteed by the architecture. It also re-explains the grounding paradox
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}): substituting the \emph{true} $\sigma$ hurts because the rest of the pipeline is
calibrated to the compensated $\hat\sigma$, not to physics. We flag two limits: the matched set is the
low-$\gamma$, $n{=}44$ subset, and the grounded-vs-trained contrast is across two models; a single
model checkpointed at warm-up and at convergence would isolate the objective's effect from
architecture outright, which we leave as a small confirmatory run.
