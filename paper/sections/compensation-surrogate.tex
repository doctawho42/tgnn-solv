\subsection{Why grounding hurts here: the end-to-end latent is a compensating surrogate}
\label{sec:surrogate}
% Artifact: results/sur/surrogate_seeds/surrogate_seeds.json (3-seed GCP L4) +
% surrogate_vs_true.json via scripts/analysis/run_compensation_surrogate.py, n=44 matched VT-2005.
This is the \emph{mechanism} behind the low-fidelity, end-to-end regime of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}): when a $\sigma$-profile head is trained \emph{end-to-end} through a fixed,
misspecified closure---never supervised toward the reference profile---it is under pressure not to be
physically faithful but to \emph{cancel} the closure's error. The external ground truth that lets us
measure the ceiling (\S\ref{sec:measure}) also lets us test this directly, comparing the predicted
profile $\hat\sigma$ against the reference VT-2005 profile $\sigma$ molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$).

\paragraph{The learned profile is a systematic, low-rank, transferable surrogate---confound-free.}
We use the most controlled test available: a grounded model checkpointed before and after
solubility fine-tuning (the $\sigma$ head unfrozen), so architecture and initialisation are held fixed
and only the objective differs; we repeat it over \emph{three seeds} and report mean$\pm$sd. The
grounded base recovers the reference profile to within $36\pm1\%$
($\lVert\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{grounded}}-\sigma\rVert/\lVert\sigma\rVert$), so the intermediate
\emph{can} be physical; solubility fine-tuning then drives it $41\pm3\%$ off the grounded profile, for a
total departure from truth of $51\pm2\%$. The drift is not per-molecule noise, and its structure is not a
constant offset: the first two principal components of the \emph{mean-centred} deviation carry
$72\pm1\%$ of the variance, $4.8\times$ the finite-sample isotropic null of ${\approx}15\%$ (the top-two
sample eigenvalue share of Gaussian noise at $n{=}44$, $p{=}51$, from eigenvalue spread, not the naive
$4\%$ population value). To test whether that
structure transfers across molecules we fit one distortion operator $D$, a dense affine map on the $51$-bin
grid (a $51\times51$ linear part plus a $51$-bin offset, $2652$ parameters). The fit is \emph{regularised},
not free: each output bin is a ridge regression of $52$ coefficients ($\ell_2$ penalty $10^{-3}$) on $22$ of
the $44$ molecules, underdetermined without the penalty, so the held-out $22$ carry the claim rather than
the fit. On that held-out half $D$ predicts the drift $4.1\pm0.5\times$ better than the zero-drift (identity)
null out-of-sample. That null is deliberately weak: the affine offset lets $D$ absorb the systematic mean
shift as well, so the transfer ratio alone also credits it for that shift; the evidence that the drift has
structure \emph{beyond} an offset is the mean-centred low-rank spectrum above. So $\hat\sigma$ is a systematic, low-rank,
\emph{transferable} closure-compensating surrogate---one generalizable way the intermediate departs from
the physical profile to compensate the closure, shared across chemistry, driven by the fitting objective
rather than by architecture or a
lack of grounding.\footnote{Three-seed mean$\pm$sd (seeds $0$--$2$). The central claim is the
\emph{sign} and the low-rank, transferable \emph{structure}, both seed-stable; the \emph{magnitude} has
moved across pipeline versions over the project---a short run reached only ${\sim}16\%$, and earlier
confounded/two-model analyses over-stated the departure (${\sim}82\%$) and transfer ($16$--$26\times$)---so
we report it but do not draw strong conclusions from it (with $n{=}3$ the sd is itself only indicative, not a precision).}

\paragraph{How far is $51\%$? A reference ladder.} A relative deviation is hard to read on its own, so we
calibrate it against predictors of known molecular content on the same $44$ molecules and the same metric
(\texttt{scripts/experiments/rho\_control\_ladder.py}). A leave-one-out corpus-mean profile---one fixed
shape reused for every molecule, carrying \emph{no} molecular information---sits at $0.493$. Assigning each
molecule the true reference profile of a different, randomly chosen molecule sits at $0.651$--$0.684$ over
five derangements, and a uniform profile at $0.889$. Against that ladder the grounded base ($0.364$) is
clearly molecule-specific, comfortably inside the constant baseline; the fine-tuned latent ($0.507$) is
not. After solubility fine-tuning the ``physical'' intermediate is no closer to the reference profile than
a shape that ignores which molecule it describes. The ladder is a yardstick, not a competitor---its rungs
are built from the reference profiles themselves and are unavailable to any predictor---but it fixes the
scale on which the drift should be read, and it says the drift consumes essentially all of the molecular
specificity that grounding had bought.

\paragraph{Dominant mode and consequence.} The dominant drift mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) transfers
area \emph{out of} the nonpolar bins ($\sigma\!\approx\!0$) and \emph{into} the polar wings: the network
makes molecules look more polar than they are, inflating the segment interactions the misspecified
exchange kernel under-weights and thereby cancelling part of $\Bclos$---the input compensating the map,
exactly as the non-identifiability of the split predicts (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}). This re-explains why
grounding hurts \emph{here} (\S\ref{sec:paradox}): the pipeline is calibrated to the compensated
$\hat\sigma$, not to physics, so substituting the reference $\sigma$ breaks the compensation. It also means
the model's ``physical'' intermediate is not the molecule's charge distribution but a surrogate,
consistent with known cautions that the interpretability of physics-grounded latents is not guaranteed
under misspecification \cite{brynjarsdottir2014,takeishi2021}, and that adapting a representation can
itself collapse an otherwise physically-faithful latent \cite{interno2026}. Two things are specific
here. The drift is the mechanism under study rather than an artifact of the evaluation: the adaptation
is the solubility fine-tuning that \emph{creates} the surrogate, and it is referenced throughout to an
external profile, not read from an unconstrained probe. And its \emph{structure}---low-rank and
transferable, not diffuse---distinguishes it from a random adaptation collapse.

\paragraph{Why $\sigma$ is under-determined by activity.} The freedom that makes the surrogate possible
is the closure's geometry. At infinite dilution the aggregate activity Fisher $J^{\top}J$
($J=\partial\lngi/\partial\sigma$) is rank-one (participation ratio $1.0$ by autograd; finite differences
decorrelate the redundant flat-profile coordinates and inflate it, \S\ref{sec:si-mech}, so we read
$\mathrm{PR}$ against a permuted control, not absolutely), and the per-molecule Fisher that governs the drift has participation ratio ${\approx}1.4$
of $51$ even at full solvent coverage: the activity map concentrates on the one or two hydrogen-bonding
directions where the closure is least faithful (\S\ref{sec:map}) and is weakly sensitive to the rest,
so the end-to-end head can move $\sigma$ along the low-sensitivity directions at little cost to the fit
(the concrete, $\sigma$-profile form of the non-identifiability of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}).
We state this narrowly---participation ratio is a concentration statistic, not a hard count, and it is
measured at infinite dilution, so its transfer to the finite-composition regime that generates the drift
is not established (the same scope gap as $\Bclos$, \S\ref{sec:scope}); the external reference here is
itself \emph{computed} (VT-2005 quantum chemistry), not experimental, so this does not establish that
recovery is impossible in principle.

Whether the drift is additionally the closure's \emph{signed} first-order inverse $J^{+}(m-g)$ is
under-resolved on this sparse, near-rank-one problem---a signed null we report but do not rely on
(App.~\ref{sec:si-failed-diagnostics}); the central claim is the under-determination above, not the
geometry of the drift.

\paragraph{The contrast that fixes the sign, and how the axes interact.} The sign of grounding is not
universal, and it turns on \emph{both} axes. On our own model, holding the $\sigma$ head at its
supervised warmup manifold instead of letting it drift end-to-end cuts the reference-input penalty by
$65\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ from $+0.40$ to $+0.14$ over $n{=}5571$ matched test rows): a
near-physical intermediate makes the pipeline far more robust to substituting the reference $\sigma$. But
supervision alone does not \emph{flip} the sign here---the penalty stays positive, because the closure
is still the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 and the warmup $\sigma$ is itself only partly physical. The
full flip needs supervision \emph{and} fidelity together: the recent TeNNet-SAC model
\cite{yang2025tennet}, which supervises its $\sigma$-profiles toward quantum-chemistry values
\emph{and} learns its segment-activity closure, outperforms COSMO-SAC and gains from truer profiles. So
the two axes interact---supervision moves the system toward the helps-regime, closure fidelity carries
it across (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
