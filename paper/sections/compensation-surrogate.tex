\subsection{The end-to-end latent departs from its reference along a shared direction}
\label{sec:surrogate}

A candidate \emph{mechanism} for the low-fidelity, end-to-end regime of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}) is this: when a $\sigma$-profile head is trained \emph{end-to-end} through a
fixed, misspecified closure---with no $\sigma$ supervision in that phase---it is under pressure not to
be physically faithful but to \emph{cancel} the closure's error. That hypothesis has two parts, and
this section establishes only the first. Its antecedent---that the latent leaves the reference, and
does so along a direction shared across molecules rather than as per-molecule noise---is measured. Its
consequent---that the departure cancels part of $\Bclos$---is not, and the one direct check we can run
on retained checkpoints argues against it, though on the wrong axis and with little power. We name the hypothesis a \emph{compensating surrogate} and
grade it open. The external reference that fixes the ceiling (\S\ref{sec:measure}) is what makes the
first part testable: we compare the predicted profile $\hat\sigma$ against the reference VT-2005
profile $\sigma$ molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$).

\paragraph{The drift under fine-tuning is systematic and low-rank, and generalises against a
zero-drift null only---confound-free.} The comparison is within a single grounded model, checkpointed before and after
solubility fine-tuning (the $\sigma$ head unfrozen), so architecture and initialisation are held fixed
and only the objective differs; we repeat it over \emph{three seeds} and report mean$\pm$sd. Under
$\sigma$ supervision the head reproduces the reference profile to within $36\pm1\%$
($\lVert\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{grounded}}-\sigma\rVert/\lVert\sigma\rVert$), so the intermediate \emph{can}
carry the physical shape---though as an in-sample fit: all $44$ probe
molecules lie in the $\sigma$-grounding pool and the warm-up early-stops on it, so no
out-of-sample recovery estimate exists here.
Solubility fine-tuning then drives the profile $41\pm3\%$ off the grounded one, for a
total departure from the reference of $51\pm2\%$. That figure, the $72\pm1\%$ top-two share below
and the $4.1\pm0.5\times$ transfer ratio below that are \emph{not stable across analysis
configurations}: a shortened run and uncontrolled two-model comparisons move them in both
directions. What this section carries is therefore the sign of the departure and the presence of a
shared direction, not these magnitudes (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}, \S\ref{sec:discussion}(v)). The drift is not
per-molecule noise, and its structure is not a constant offset: the first two principal components
of the \emph{mean-centred} deviation carry $72\pm1\%$ of the variance. That share must be read
against a null sharing the deviation's own smoothness rather than an isotropic one; we therefore
compare against a \emph{phase-randomised} null, which preserves each deviation's amplitude spectrum
exactly and destroys only the between-molecule alignment that a shared compensating direction
asserts. The observed share clears it ($0.399$ against $0.221$, $p<0.001$), at roughly
$1.8\times$; a wrong-molecule null built from real reference profiles does \emph{not} clear
($0.772$, $p=1.00$), and if the case for preferring the phase-randomised one is not accepted the
spectrum is uninformative rather than supporting. That case, the calibration behind it, and the
fact that the isolation drift has no matched null of its own are in \S\ref{sec:si-mech}. To test
whether the structure transfers across molecules we fit one ridge-regularised distortion operator
$D$ on half the $44$ molecules, so the held-out half carries the claim rather than the fit.
There $D$ predicts the drift $4.1\pm0.5\times$ better than the zero-drift (identity)
null out of sample. That null is deliberately weak, however, and nothing controls it on these three
seeds, whose checkpoints were not retained. A molecule-independent constant offset reaches
$2.1\times$ on the one companion run where that control can be computed, so the ratio stands against
the identity null alone. The $\pm0.5$ spans seeds at one fixed partition rather than split-to-split
variability, and the held-out half is homologue-saturated, so ``held out'' is largely within-cluster
interpolation (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). Structure
\emph{beyond} an offset therefore rests on the mean-centred spectrum, not the ratio---and
mean-centred structure is equally what a shrinkage of each profile toward the corpus-mean shape would
produce, which these statistics do not exclude (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). $\hat\sigma$ therefore
departs from its reference systematically and at low rank, and the departure generalises across
molecules---against a zero-drift null only, on a set whose halves are chemically close---driven by the
fitting objective rather than by architecture or a lack of grounding. Whether that departure is
compensation is the separate question below.

\paragraph{A reference ladder for the $51\%$ departure.}
Calibrated on the same $44$ molecules and metric, the fine-tuned latent ($0.507$) sits
\emph{outside} a leave-one-out corpus-mean profile ($0.493$) that carries no molecular information
at all, while the grounded base ($0.364$) sits well inside it: the drift consumes essentially all
the molecular specificity grounding had provided, as a collapse toward that shape would also look.

\paragraph{Dominant mode, and the direct cancellation test.} The dominant drift mode
(Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) transfers area \emph{out of} the nonpolar bins ($\sigma\!\approx\!0$) and
\emph{into} the polar wings: the network represents molecules as more polar than they are. Whether that
shift \emph{cancels} part of $\Bclos$---the input compensating the map, as the non-identifiability of
the split allows (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident})---is the comparison
$\mathrm{MSE}(m,g(\hat\sigma))$ against $\mathrm{MSE}(m,g(\zstar))$ on the same pairs. It does not
support the cancellation reading. Holding the cavity area at
its reference value so that only the profile shape varies, the sole retained end-to-end SLE-trained
COSMO-SAC checkpoint gives $18.75$ against $1.47$: on the activity axis its drifted profile is an
order of magnitude \emph{worse} through the same closure, not better. Three limits sit on that
number. The checkpoint is the
uncontrolled one, whose departure ($82\%$) is half again the three-seed $51\pm2\%$, so it may not be
representative of the three seeds. The axis is wrong: the drift is produced by the finite-composition solubility objective
and scored here at infinite dilution. The check also has almost no power in the direction that
matters, so it can separate ``no cancellation'' from ``large cancellation'' but not from ``some
cancellation'' (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}, which also explains why a grounded warm-up checkpoint's score
on the same comparison is a statement about profile roughness and an in-sample fit). It is the only
direct evidence available, the three seed checkpoints not having been retained, and it is evidence
against rather than a test the hypothesis passed or failed cleanly.

A second, independent check on the closure's own axis points the same way: a \emph{more} polar
profile moves $g$ further from $m$ (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). Both checks are on the
infinite-dilution activity axis, whereas the drift is produced by the finite-composition solubility
objective, so neither refutes cancellation in the regime that generates it. They remove the
one place where it could have been measured, leaving the cancellation reading inferred from the
paradox itself and graded open. What is established is weaker and still consequential: the model's ``physical''
intermediate is not the molecule's charge distribution, consistent with cautions that the
interpretability of physics-grounded latents is not guaranteed under misspecification
\cite{brynjarsdottir2014,takeishi2021}, and that adapting a representation can itself collapse an
otherwise physically-faithful latent \cite{interno2026}. What is specific here is that the drift is
referenced throughout to an external profile rather than to an unconstrained probe. Its
\emph{structure}---low-rank rather than diffuse---excludes a random collapse but not the structured
shrinkage above. Only the drift's \emph{sign} against the closure's own error pulled back through
its Jacobian would separate those, and that comparison is under-resolved on this near-rank-one
problem (App.~\ref{sec:si-failed-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Why $\sigma$ is under-determined by activity.} The freedom that would make a compensating
surrogate possible is the closure's geometry: at infinite dilution the activity map concentrates on
the one or two hydrogen-bonding directions where the closure is least faithful (\S\ref{sec:map}) and
is weakly sensitive to the rest. The end-to-end head can therefore move $\sigma$ along the
low-sensitivity directions at little cost to the fit---the $\sigma$-profile form of
Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}. The
Fisher measurement behind that statement, and the two respects in which it does not settle the
question---it is taken at infinite dilution rather than in the finite-composition regime that
generates the drift, and the external reference is itself computed rather than experimental, so
nothing here shows recovery to be impossible in principle---are in \S\ref{sec:si-mech}.

\paragraph{The contrast that fixes the sign, and how the axes interact.} The sign of grounding turns
on \emph{both} axes, and on which sense of grounding is meant. Freezing the $\sigma$ head at its
supervised warm-up state instead of letting it drift end to end cuts the reference-input penalty by
$65\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ from $+0.40$ to $+0.14$ over $n{=}5571$ matched test rows). Those are a
separate matched pair of runs, one base resumed twice with the freeze off and on, and not the $+0.41$
headline arms of \S\ref{sec:paradox} (\S\ref{sec:si-tables}). A
near-physical intermediate makes the pipeline far more robust to substituting the reference $\sigma$.
Supervision alone does not, however, \emph{flip} the sign here---the penalty stays positive, the closure
still being the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 and the warmup $\sigma$ itself only partly physical. The
full flip needs supervision \emph{and} fidelity together: TeNNet-SAC \cite{yang2025tennet}, which
supervises its $\sigma$-profiles toward quantum-chemistry values \emph{and} learns its
segment-activity closure, outperforms COSMO-SAC and gains from truer profiles. Supervision moves the
system toward the regime in which grounding helps, and closure fidelity carries it across
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
