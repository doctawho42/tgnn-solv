\subsection{Why grounding hurts on this anchor: the end-to-end latent is a compensating surrogate}
\label{sec:surrogate}
% Artifact: results/compensation/isolation_gpu.json (converged one-model control) +
% surrogate_vs_true.json via scripts/analysis/run_compensation_surrogate.py, n=44 matched VT-2005.
This is the \emph{mechanism} behind the low-fidelity, end-to-end corner of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}): when a $\sigma$-profile head is trained \emph{end-to-end} through a fixed,
misspecified closure---never supervised toward the true profile---it is under pressure not to be
physically faithful but to \emph{cancel} the closure's error. The external ground truth that lets us
measure the ceiling (\S\ref{sec:measure}) also lets us test this directly, comparing the predicted
profile $\hat\sigma$ against the true VT-2005 profile $\sigma$ molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$).

\paragraph{The learned profile is a systematic, low-rank, transferable surrogate---confound-free.}
We use the cleanest test available: a \emph{single} grounded model checkpointed before and after
solubility fine-tuning (the $\sigma$ head unfrozen), so architecture and initialisation are held fixed
and only the objective differs. The grounded base recovers the true profile to within $33\%$
($\lVert\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{grounded}}-\sigma\rVert/\lVert\sigma\rVert$), so the intermediate
\emph{can} be physical; solubility fine-tuning then drives it $45\%$ off the grounded profile, for a
total departure from truth of $53\%$. The drift is not per-molecule noise: its first two principal
components carry $73\%$ of the variance (isotropic noise over $51$ bins would give ${\approx}4\%$), and
a single affine distortion $D$ fit on half the molecules predicts the drift on the held-out half
$3.3\times$ better than identity out-of-sample. So $\hat\sigma$ is a systematic, low-rank,
\emph{transferable} closure-compensating surrogate---one generalizable way the intermediate ``lies'' to
the closure, shared across chemistry, driven by the fitting objective rather than by architecture or a
lack of grounding.\footnote{These are the confound-free, converged one-model numbers. The magnitude
develops over training---a short run reached only ${\sim}16\%$---and earlier confounded and two-model
analyses over-stated both the departure (${\sim}82\%$) and the transfer ($16$--$26\times$).}

\paragraph{Dominant mode and consequence.} The dominant drift mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) transfers
area \emph{out of} the nonpolar bins ($\sigma\!\approx\!0$) and \emph{into} the polar wings: the network
makes molecules look more polar than they are, inflating the segment interactions the misspecified
exchange kernel under-weights and thereby cancelling part of $\Bclos$---the input compensating the map,
exactly as the non-identifiability of the split predicts (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}). This re-explains why
grounding hurts \emph{here} (\S\ref{sec:paradox}): the pipeline is calibrated to the compensated
$\hat\sigma$, not to physics, so substituting the true $\sigma$ breaks the compensation. It also means
the model's ``physical'' intermediate is not the molecule's charge distribution but a surrogate,
consistent with known cautions that the interpretability of physics-grounded latents is not guaranteed
under misspecification \cite{brynjarsdottir2014,takeishi2021}; what is
specific here is the \emph{structure}---the surrogate is low-rank and transferable, not diffuse.

\paragraph{How much room, and why $\sigma$ is under-determined by activity.} The freedom that makes the
surrogate possible is the closure's geometry. At \emph{infinite dilution} the aggregate activity Fisher
$J^{\top}J$ ($J=\partial\lngi/\partial\sigma$, over $100$ VT-2005 solutes $\times\,48$ solvents $\times$ two
temperatures) is rank-one---participation ratio $1.0$, reproduced to $1.00$ by an independent
finite-difference computation.\footnote{$J^{\top}J$ is the Gram of $\partial\lngi/\partial\sigma_{\text{solute}}$
at infinite dilution over $100\times48\times2$ evaluations at real VT-2005 profiles. The solute-profile
autograd is stable at infinite dilution and agrees with central finite differences; at finite composition the
fixed-point autograd is numerically unstable (per-pair gradients inflate by ${\sim}10^{6}$), so the rank's
transfer to finite composition cannot be assessed this way.} That aggregate is the \emph{common} sensitivity
direction across chemistry; the object that governs per-molecule drift is the per-molecule Fisher, whose
participation ratio is ${\approx}1.4$ of $51$ even at full solvent coverage. Either way the activity map
concentrates on ${\approx}1$--$2$ profile directions---the hydrogen-bonding bins
($\sigma\!\approx\!\pm\sigma_{\mathrm{hb}}$), where the closure is least faithful (\S\ref{sec:map})---and is
weakly sensitive to the rest, so the solute profile is strongly \emph{under-determined} by activity: the
end-to-end head can move it along the low-sensitivity directions at little cost to the fit. We state this
narrowly. Participation ratio is a concentration statistic, not a hard count of free directions; and it is
measured at infinite dilution, where $\partial\Gamma_{S,\mathrm{mix}}/\partial\sigma_{\text{solute}}\!\to\!0$
by the dilution limit, so the low rank may be a property of that limit rather than of the map, and its
transfer to the finite-composition regime that actually generates the drift (the SLE solves $x_2$ at
saturation) is \emph{not} established---the same scope gap we flag for $\Bclos$ (\S\ref{sec:measure}). So
activity under-determines $\sigma$ at infinite dilution, measured where the latent has an external reference;
we do not claim recovery is impossible in principle.

\paragraph{The drift is not the closure's first-order inverse.} One might hope the drift is the closure's
linear-response inverse, $\hat\sigma-\sigma\approx J^{+}(m-g(\sigma))$, with $J=\partial g/\partial\sigma$ the
closure Jacobian and $m$ the observed \lngi---predictable a priori from $J$, before any solubility training.
It is not: on the matched set the measured per-molecule drift shows no signed alignment with $J^{+}(m-g)$
(median cosine $-0.00$; Fig.~\ref{fig:picard}).\footnote{The residual COSMO-SAC closure runs a segment-activity
fixed-point iteration, so its autograd Jacobian is numerically unstable
($\lVert\partial g/\partial\sigma_{\text{solvent}}\rVert\!\sim\!10^{15}$ at infinite dilution); $J$ is
computed by finite differences on the converged closure.} We report this as a negative on the \emph{signed}
prediction only, and do not build on it. The matched set is sparse---a median of one pair per molecule, so
each per-molecule Jacobian subspace is one-dimensional---which makes the dimension-matched row-space controls
uninformative here: with a resolved direction common across molecules, a molecule's ``own'' and ``other''
subspaces coincide, so their near-equality carries no signal. The load-bearing claim is the
under-determination above, not the geometry of the drift.

\paragraph{The contrast that fixes the sign, and how the axes interact.} The sign of grounding is not
universal, and it turns on \emph{both} axes. On our own model, holding the $\sigma$ head at its
supervised warmup manifold instead of letting it drift end-to-end cuts the true-input penalty by
$65\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ from $+0.40$ to $+0.14$ over $n{=}5571$ matched test rows): a
near-physical intermediate makes the pipeline far more robust to substituting the true $\sigma$. But
supervision alone does not \emph{flip} the sign here---the penalty stays positive, because the closure
is still the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 and the warmup $\sigma$ is itself only partly physical. The
full flip needs supervision \emph{and} fidelity together: the recent TeNNet-SAC model
\cite{yang2025tennet}, which supervises its $\sigma$-profiles toward quantum-chemistry values
\emph{and} learns its segment-activity closure, outperforms COSMO-SAC and gains from truer profiles. So
the two axes interact---supervision moves the system toward the helps-corner, closure fidelity carries
it across (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
