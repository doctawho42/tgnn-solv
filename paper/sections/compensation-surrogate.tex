A candidate \emph{mechanism} for the low-fidelity, end-to-end regime: a $\sigma$-profile head trained
\emph{end-to-end} through a fixed, misspecified closure, with no $\sigma$ supervision in that phase,
is under pressure to \emph{cancel} the closure's error instead of being physically faithful. This
section establishes the first of its two parts. The antecedent, that the latent leaves the reference
along a direction shared across molecules, is measured; the consequent, that the departure cancels
part of $\Bclos$, stays unmeasured, and the one direct check argues against it. We compare the
predicted profile $\hat\sigma$ against the reference VT-2005 profile molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$):
anatomy of the object the two operations of \S\ref{sec:paradox} act on, and not evidence for the sign
of either.

\paragraph{What is measured, and on what.} We compare within a single grounded model, checkpointed
before and after solubility fine-tuning, over the $n{=}44$ matched molecules, at three seeds. Those
runs are a \emph{separate} set from the arms that produce the paradox: their $\sigma$ head stays
unfrozen through fine-tuning, where the arms of \S\ref{sec:paradox} freeze it from P2 onward, whose
own drift the deposit cannot recover. What follows is therefore measured on runs of its own and stays
unestablished of the checkpoints whose paradox it is offered to explain. Under $\sigma$ supervision
the head reproduces the reference to within $36\pm1\%$, so the intermediate \emph{can} carry the
physical shape --- as an in-sample fit, all $44$ probe molecules lying inside the $\sigma$-grounding
pool. Fine-tuning drives it a further $41\pm3\%$ away, a total departure of $51\pm2\%$ whose
magnitude is unstable across analysis configurations, so what these runs fix is the sign and the
shared direction below and not the size. On the reference ladder (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}) it sits at
$0.507$, past the $0.493$ of one corpus-mean profile reused for every molecule: the drift consumes
essentially all the specificity grounding supplied.

\paragraph{What the departure is, and what it is not.} The drift is structured, and beyond a constant
offset. The first two principal components of the mean-centred deviation carry $72\pm1\%$ of the
variance. The top-two share of the learned-versus-reference deviation, $0.399$, clears a
phase-randomised null ($0.221\pm0.01$, the $\pm$ being the null's own spread over draws; $p<0.001$)
on one grounded checkpoint and not over the three seeds. Two nulls it fails to clear travel with it:
the mean-centred share has no matched null of its own, and a wrong-molecule null built from real
reference profiles does \emph{not} clear ($0.772$, $p=1.00$), so a reader who declines the case for
preferring the phase-randomised null should read the spectrum as uninformative. One distortion
operator fitted on half the $44$ molecules predicts the drift on the other half $4.1\pm0.5\times$
better than the zero-drift null --- a weak null, uncontrolled here, a constant offset alone reaching
$2.1\times$ where that control exists --- so the ratio stands against the identity null alone, its
$\pm$ spans the three seeds at one partition of the $44$ and not the partitions, and the held-out
half is homologue-saturated besides (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). Structure \emph{beyond} an offset
therefore rests on the mean-centred spectrum, which itself leaves open a shrinkage of every profile
toward the corpus-mean shape. The dominant mode (Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) moves area out of the
nonpolar bins into the polar wings: fine-tuning makes the network represent molecules as more polar
than its \emph{own grounded profile} does.

\paragraph{Whether the departure cancels closure error.} It remains unshown, and the one direct check
runs against it. Holding the cavity area at its reference value, the sole retained end-to-end
SLE-trained COSMO-SAC checkpoint scores $18.75$ against $1.47$ on the $n{=}60$ VT-2005-matched pairs:
on the activity axis its drifted profile is an order of magnitude \emph{worse} through the same
closure. Four limits sit on it, and \S\ref{sec:si-mech} carries them. The checkpoint is the
uncontrolled one, at $82\%$ departure against the three-seed $51\pm2\%$, and a retired run at that,
trained at $8$ segment iterations and scored at $30$, scoring MAE $2.61$ at $R^2=-0.31$ on the
scaffold split. The drift is produced at finite composition and scored at infinite dilution. And the
check has almost no power in the direction that matters: it separates ``no cancellation'' from
``large cancellation'' and leaves ``some cancellation'' open. It and the closure's own-axis coefficient
sweep beside it (\S\ref{par:own-axis}) both sit on the infinite-dilution axis, which is where
cancellation could have been measured, so the reading stays inferred from the paradox itself. A
second reading is live: the deployed ideal term sets $\Delta C_p$ to zero, one-signed and worth a
median $0.32$ in \lnx\ where a melting point is measured (\S\ref{sec:framework}), and the externally
supervised crystal head cannot absorb it, so a polar drift is also what compensating an over-large
$\Phicry$ would look like. What is established is weaker and still consequential: the intermediate
departs from the molecule's charge distribution, the risk misspecification poses to physics-grounded
latents \cite{brynjarsdottir2014,takeishi2021} and the collapse an adapted representation can suffer
\cite{interno2026}. Its low-rank \emph{structure} excludes a random collapse.

\paragraph{What leaves $\sigma$ under-determined, and what freezing it buys.} The freedom that makes
such a cancellation possible is the closure's geometry: at infinite dilution the activity map's
Fisher spectrum is effectively rank one, participation ratio $1.0$ in aggregate and ${\approx}1.4$ of
$51$ per molecule, the resolved direction lying on the hydrogen-bonding wings
(\S\ref{par:fisher-geometry}). The head can therefore move $\sigma$ along the remaining,
low-sensitivity coordinates at little cost to the fit, which is the $\sigma$-profile form of
Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}. Freezing it at its supervised warm-up state instead cuts the reference-input
penalty by $65\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ $+0.40$ to $+0.14$, $n{=}5571$ matched test rows) --- a
separate matched pair, one base resumed twice with the freeze off and on, so a difference between two
runs and not a mean over any, and distinct from the $+0.41$ arms of \S\ref{sec:paradox}. Supervision
by itself stops short of a \emph{flip} in the sign: the penalty stays positive, the closure is still
the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 and the warm-up $\sigma$ itself only partly physical.

\paragraph{The nearest published construction, which reports the opposite sign.} TeNNet-SAC
\cite{yang2025tennet}, a $\sigma$-profile predictor feeding a COSMO-SAC-shaped segment-activity
model, reports accuracy consistently better than COSMO-SAC across its benchmarks. The two works score
different operations. Its gain is a training-time gain from supervising and then fine-tuning a
learned profile, and over five leak-free seeds that operation has no established sign here
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}). Its profile predictor, supervised externally on quantum-chemical
$\sigma$-profiles for $39{,}745$ molecules, then stays frozen while only the segment-activity output
head is fine-tuned, so the closure's gradient never reaches the intermediate. What we find negative
is substituting a tabulated profile at inference, which TeNNet-SAC leaves unreported, on an
intermediate trained through the closure with neither safeguard. Its segment-activity kernel is
trainable besides, where this one is fixed at the $2002$ parameterisation. If the sign of the
substitution turns on whether the kernel can move, our finding is about a fixed $2002$ kernel and not
about routing a prediction through a physical intermediate, and \S\ref{sec:notdecided} already
records that no grounding gap has been measured under any modern closure. Retraining the two arms
against the $2010$/dispersion kernel end to end is limitation~(x)'s named run and remains to be made.

\paragraph{Whether a downstream correction repairs it.}
\label{sec:gateb}
If the closure binding were merely an inaccurate point estimate, a learned output-space correction on
top of the physics prediction should recover accuracy. It fails to. An additive bounded residual with
a learned confidence gate leaves the gate effectively shut, and forcing it open removes the
systematic \emph{bias} ($+0.45\to+0.06$) while recovering little \emph{accuracy} ($R^2$
$0.236\to0.274$ on the same rows), as expected if the closure and not the estimate is the binding
constraint. Both arms run at seed $42$ alone, and the statement is about the one correction tested: a
gated additive bounded residual on the output, on this closure and this corpus.

COSMO-SAC's hydrogen-bonding contribution acts on segments beyond $|\sigma|=\sigma_{\mathrm{hb}}$,
and the $2002$ kernel assigns a segment to the donor side by that threshold alone, with no atom
typing. Donor-window area is therefore a statement about surface charge density and not a
hydrogen-bond-donor count: $264$ of the $1003$ molecules in the reference tabulation that lack an
N--H, O--H or S--H have area there, acetonitrile's $6.71$\,\AA$^2$ standing above ethanol's $6.20$.
What carries the argument is the molecule-matched comparison. Cyclohexane,
acetone, toluene and tetrahydrofuran each carry $0.000$\,\AA$^2$ of donor-window area in the
reference tabulation, exactly zero and not merely small, against $15.75$ for water; the learned
profile carries $44.0$, $32.4$, $24.5$ and $50.7$\,\AA$^2$ in that same window, between $23\%$ and
$36\%$ of each molecule's total surface. Figure~\ref{fig:donor-window} draws that comparison over the
sixteen solvents carrying the most scored rows; the reference is exactly zero in twelve of them. We read those areas off the one trained COSMO-SAC head the
tree holds, the retired run of the two-MSE check above, scoring MAE $2.61$ at $R^2=-0.31$ on the
scaffold split. A null arm in the same deposit separates architecture from training: as a fraction of
profile mass in the same extreme bins, where the reference tabulation's median is $0.000$, an
\emph{untrained} network of this architecture reaches $0.212$ against the trained head's $0.189$. The
window is full before any training, and training emptied it slightly.

Two causes stay entangled. This head's $\sigma$-supervision stream ran for zero steps, and
\S\ref{sec:ident} is why that matters: solubility alone underdetermines the profile's shape, leaving
an unsupervised profile free in exactly the bins that carry no constraint, which accounts for a
quantity of this size. The parameterisation accounts for far less. The profile is emitted through a
softmax over the $51$ bins, strictly positive where $57\%$ of the reference table's bins are exactly
zero, so supervision at any strength leaves the window occupied. But a softmax reaches $10^{-6}$ at a
logit gap of ten, four orders below what is measured. What
the measurement establishes is that the window is occupied in a trained arm and the closure's
hydrogen-bond term consequently live on every pair of the scored set, a defect whichever cause
carries it. A supervised arm separates the two, and a head able to emit zeros removes the
architectural half.

An account of this degradation is available from outside chemistry, and it is not the one above.
Wu \latin{et al.}\cite{wu2019illconditioned} substitute near-exact Reynolds stresses into the RANS
equations and recover large velocity errors, which they trace to ill-conditioning: the fixed model
amplifies a small perturbation of its input. That account and the compensation account differ on one
measurable quantity, the size of the substitution step, and they differ in opposite directions.
Ill-conditioning requires the step to be small. On the $354$ test rows whose solute and solvent both
carry a VT-2005 profile ($64$ solutes, $18$ solvents, $4.4\%$ of the split), it is not: the reference
profile stands $1.53$ $[1.39,1.80]$ times the learned profile's own norm away from it, so the two are
different objects rather than an estimate and its target. Neither does the closure amplify
selectively along that direction. A random direction of the same norm produces a \emph{larger}
relative change in $\ln\gamma^\infty$ on $71\%$ $[62,76]$ of those rows at eight segment iterations
and on $81\%$ $[70,86]$ at thirty. The amplification ratio itself is not stable across that pair
(median $0.60$ $[0.37,1.14]$ against $1.29$ $[0.64,2.20]$), which is the segment count behaving as
the free hyperparameter \S\ref{sec:estimator} says it is; the step size is a property of the two
profiles and does not move with it. Both readings are of the retired head of this section and price
the quantity rather than the arms of Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox}.
