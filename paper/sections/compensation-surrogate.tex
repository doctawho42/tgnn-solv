\subsection{The end-to-end latent departs from its reference along a shared direction}
\label{sec:surrogate}

A candidate \emph{mechanism} for the low-fidelity, end-to-end regime of the map
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}) is this: when a $\sigma$-profile head is trained \emph{end-to-end} through a
fixed, misspecified closure---with no $\sigma$ supervision in that phase---it is under pressure not to
be physically faithful but to \emph{cancel} the closure's error. That hypothesis has two parts, and
this section establishes only the first. Its antecedent---that the latent leaves the reference, and
does so along a direction shared across molecules rather than as per-molecule noise---is measured. Its
consequent---that the departure cancels part of $\Bclos$---is not, and the one direct check we can run
on retained checkpoints argues against it, though on the wrong axis and with little power. We name the hypothesis a \emph{compensating surrogate} and
grade it open. The external reference that fixes the ceiling (\S\ref{sec:measure}) is what makes the
first part testable: we compare the predicted profile $\hat\sigma$ against the reference VT-2005
profile $\sigma$ molecule by molecule ($n{=}44$).

\paragraph{The drift under fine-tuning is systematic and low-rank, and generalises against a
zero-drift null only---confound-free.} The comparison is within a single grounded model, checkpointed before and after
solubility fine-tuning (the $\sigma$ head unfrozen), so architecture and initialisation are held fixed
and only the objective differs; we repeat it over \emph{three seeds} and report mean$\pm$sd. Under
$\sigma$ supervision the head reproduces the reference profile to within $36\pm1\%$
($\lVert\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{grounded}}-\sigma\rVert/\lVert\sigma\rVert$), so the intermediate \emph{can}
carry the physical shape---though as an in-sample fit, not a recovery estimate: all $44$ probe
molecules lie in the $\sigma$-grounding pool and the warmup early-stops on that pool, so no
out-of-sample recovery estimate exists here.
Solubility fine-tuning then drives the profile $41\pm3\%$ off the grounded one, for a
total departure from the reference of $51\pm2\%$. That figure, the $72\pm1\%$ below and the
$4.1\pm0.5\times$ transfer ratio below that are reported but are \emph{not stable across analysis
configurations}: a shortened run gives only ${\sim}16\%$ departure and a transfer of $1.7\times$,
below what a constant offset alone reaches on the one run where that control can be computed
(\S\ref{sec:si-mech}), while uncontrolled two-model comparisons over-state
both the departure (${\sim}82\%$) and the transfer ($16$--$26\times$); with $n{=}3$ the $\pm$ is
indicative and not a precision. What this section carries is therefore the sign of the departure and
the presence of a
shared direction, not these magnitudes (\S\ref{sec:discussion}(v)). The drift is not per-molecule noise, and its structure is not a
constant offset: the first two principal components of the \emph{mean-centred} deviation carry
$72\pm1\%$ of the variance. That share must be read against a null that shares the deviation's own
smoothness, not an isotropic one, since smoothing isotropic noise alone reaches a top-two share of
$0.25$ to $0.83$ as the correlation length grows from one bin to eight. We therefore compare against a
\emph{phase-randomised} null, which preserves each deviation's amplitude spectrum exactly and
destroys only the between-molecule alignment that a shared compensating direction
asserts. On a grounded checkpoint's deviation from the reference the observed share exceeds that
null ($0.399$ against $0.221\pm0.011$, $p<0.001$), so a shared direction is
present, at roughly $1.8\times$ a smoothness-matched null. A second structured reference does
\emph{not} clear: assigning each molecule another molecule's reference profile gives
$0.772\pm0.023$, above the observed value ($p=1.00$). We take the phase-randomised
null as the appropriate one, the wrong-molecule deviation being a difference between two
points on the reference manifold and so inheriting that manifold's own concentration, which would
demand more alignment than a shared direction implies. If that argument is not accepted, the
spectrum is uninformative rather than supporting. Note that the observed share exceeds that null on
the \emph{reference deviation}, a different quantity from the isolation drift, which has no matched
null of its own (\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). To test whether that
structure transfers across molecules we fit one ridge-regularised distortion operator $D$ on half
the $44$ molecules, so the held-out half carries the claim rather than the fit.
There $D$ predicts the drift $4.1\pm0.5\times$ better than the zero-drift (identity)
null out of sample---but that null is deliberately weak, and on these three seeds nothing controls
it. A molecule-independent constant offset, fitted on the same training half, is itself
$2.1\times$ better than that null on the one companion run whose checkpoints were retained; the
three seed checkpoints behind the $4.1\times$ were not, so no offset control can be computed on the
run the ratio comes from and the ratio stands against the identity null alone. The $\pm0.5$ spans
model seeds at one fixed
partition rather than split-to-split variability, and the held-out half is homologue-saturated, so
``held out'' is largely within-cluster interpolation. Construction, the offset control and the
saturation audit are in \S\ref{sec:si-mech}. Structure
\emph{beyond} an offset therefore rests on the mean-centred spectrum, not the ratio---and
mean-centred structure is equally what a shrinkage of each profile toward the corpus-mean shape would
produce, which these statistics do not exclude (the reference profiles themselves sit at $0.77$
mean-centred, so any drift linear in the profile inherits that concentration). So $\hat\sigma$
departs from its reference systematically and at low rank, and the departure generalises across
molecules---against a zero-drift null only, on a set whose halves are chemically close---driven by the
fitting objective rather than by architecture or a lack of grounding. Whether that departure is
compensation is the separate question below (three-seed mean$\pm$sd, seeds $0$--$2$).

\paragraph{A reference ladder for the $51\%$ departure.} A relative deviation carries no absolute
scale. Calibrated on the same $44$ molecules and metric, the fine-tuned latent ($0.507$) sits
\emph{outside} a leave-one-out corpus-mean profile ($0.493$) that carries no molecular information
at all, while the grounded base ($0.364$) sits well inside it: the drift consumes essentially all
the molecular specificity grounding had provided, as a collapse toward that shape would also look.

\paragraph{Dominant mode, and the direct cancellation test.} The dominant drift mode
(Fig.~\ref{fig:surrogate}) transfers area \emph{out of} the nonpolar bins ($\sigma\!\approx\!0$) and
\emph{into} the polar wings: the network represents molecules as more polar than they are. Whether that
shift \emph{cancels} part of $\Bclos$---the input compensating the map, as the non-identifiability of
the split allows (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident})---is the comparison
$\mathrm{MSE}(m,g(\hat\sigma))$ against $\mathrm{MSE}(m,g(\zstar))$ on the same pairs. It does not
support the cancellation reading. Holding the cavity area at
its reference value so that only the profile shape varies, the sole retained end-to-end SLE-trained
COSMO-SAC checkpoint gives $18.75$ against $1.47$: on the activity axis its drifted profile is an
order of magnitude \emph{worse} through the same closure, not better. Three limits sit on that
number. The checkpoint is the
uncontrolled one, whose departure ($82\%$) is half again the three-seed $51\pm2\%$, so it may not be
representative of the three seeds. The axis is wrong: the drift is produced by the finite-composition solubility objective
and scored here at infinite dilution. And the check has almost no power in the direction that
matters---$18.75$ against $\Var(m)=2.47$ is $7.6\times$ worse than predicting the mean, which is what
a broken profile looks like, not a partly compensating one, so it can separate ``no cancellation''
from ``large cancellation'' but not from ``some cancellation''. It is the only direct evidence
available, the three seed checkpoints not having been retained, and it is evidence against rather than
a test the hypothesis passed or failed cleanly. (A grounded warm-up checkpoint scores $0.73$ on the
same comparison; \S\ref{sec:si-mech} explains why that is a statement about profile roughness and an
in-sample fit, not a both-channel reproduction of the paradox.)

A second, independent check on the closure's own axis points the same way: there the closure
\emph{under}-predicts, and raising its hydrogen-bond coefficient makes the strong-donor pairs worse
while zeroing it makes them better, so a \emph{more} polar profile moves $g$ further from $m$
(\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). Both checks are on the
infinite-dilution activity axis, whereas the drift is produced by the finite-composition solubility
objective, so neither refutes cancellation in the regime that generates it; what they do is remove the
one place we could have measured it, leaving the cancellation reading inferred from the paradox itself
and graded open. What is established is weaker and still consequential: the model's ``physical''
intermediate is not the molecule's charge distribution, consistent with cautions that the
interpretability of physics-grounded latents is not guaranteed under misspecification
\cite{brynjarsdottir2014,takeishi2021}, and that adapting a representation can itself collapse an
otherwise physically-faithful latent \cite{interno2026}. What is specific here is that the drift is
referenced throughout to an external profile rather than to an unconstrained probe. Its
\emph{structure}---low-rank rather than diffuse---excludes a random collapse but not the structured
shrinkage above; only the drift's \emph{sign} relative to $J^{+}(m-g)$---the closure's own error
pulled back through the pseudoinverse of its Jacobian $J=\partial g/\partial\sigma$, the direction a
compensating drift would take (\S\ref{sec:si-mech})---would separate those, and that
comparison is under-resolved on this near-rank-one problem
(App.~\ref{sec:si-failed-diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Why $\sigma$ is under-determined by activity.} The freedom that would make a compensating
surrogate possible is the closure's geometry: at infinite dilution the activity map concentrates on
the one or two hydrogen-bonding directions where the closure is least faithful (\S\ref{sec:map}) and
is weakly sensitive to the rest, so the end-to-end head can move $\sigma$ along the low-sensitivity
directions at little cost to the fit---the concrete $\sigma$-profile form of the non-identifiability
of Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}. The Fisher measurement behind that statement, the concentration
statistic's own caveats, and the two respects in which it does not settle the question---it is taken
at infinite dilution rather than in the finite-composition regime that generates the drift, and the
external reference is itself computed rather than experimental, so nothing here shows recovery to be
impossible in principle---are in \S\ref{sec:si-mech} and App.~\ref{sec:si-failed-diagnostics}.

\paragraph{The contrast that fixes the sign, and how the axes interact.} The sign of grounding turns
on \emph{both} axes, and on which sense of grounding is meant. Freezing the $\sigma$ head at its
supervised warm-up state instead of letting it drift end to end cuts the reference-input penalty by
$65\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}$ from $+0.40$ to $+0.14$ over $n{=}5571$ matched test rows)---a separate
matched pair of runs, one base resumed twice with the freeze off and on, and not the $+0.41$
headline arms of \S\ref{sec:paradox} (\S\ref{sec:si-tables}): a
near-physical intermediate makes the pipeline far more robust to substituting the reference $\sigma$.
But supervision alone does not \emph{flip} the sign here---the penalty stays positive, the closure
still being the low-fidelity COSMO-SAC-2002 and the warmup $\sigma$ itself only partly physical. The
full flip needs supervision \emph{and} fidelity together: TeNNet-SAC \cite{yang2025tennet}, which
supervises its $\sigma$-profiles toward quantum-chemistry values \emph{and} learns its
segment-activity closure, outperforms COSMO-SAC and gains from truer profiles. Supervision moves the
system toward the regime in which grounding helps, and closure fidelity carries it across
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
