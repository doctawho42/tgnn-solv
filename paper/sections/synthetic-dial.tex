\subsection{Closure fidelity sets the reference-input penalty} \label{sec:dial}
% Artifact: results/synthetic_dial/summary.json via scripts/analysis/run_fidelity_dial.py (seed 0).
To isolate the mechanism we construct a controlled synthetic experiment in which only the closure's
fidelity varies (input quality, sample size, and estimator held fixed). A teacher draws a latent
$\zstar$ and a target through a known map $T$; a fixed candidate closure $g_F$ reconstructs it under
a tunable misspecification of scalar fidelity $F$ ($F=1$ recovers $T$ exactly, smaller $F$ pushes
$g_F$ away). The reference-input penalty $\Delta R^2 = R^2_{\text{oracle}}-R^2_{\text{head}}$ is a measure
of $\Bclos$ in this construction---the free head recovers $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ while $g_F$ is the misspecified map. As $F$
falls the penalty grows monotonically and without exception: averaged over six misspecification forms
(linear, quadratic, cubic, absolute-value, sinusoidal, cross-term) $F{=}1.00$ gives
$\Delta R^2\approx0$, $F{=}0.38$ gives $\approx-0.33$, and an over-rotation stress test ($F{=}{-}0.50$)
gives $\approx-2.0$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:dial}); the ordering is identical across four unrelated teacher
families (linear, monotone-nonlinear, kinetics-exponential, PDE-field), so the effect is a property of
fidelity magnitude, not of any one domain. Placing the real system on the sweep only illustratively
(the real-closure-to-$F$ map is a synthetic construction, not a calibrated measurement), the COSMO-SAC
closure over predicted $\sigma$-profiles falls at $F\lesssim0.4$: its solubility $\Delta R^2\approx-0.36$
is what a closure of this fidelity predicts, consistent with $\Bclos$ dominating $\Binsuf$.

The same construction shows why the grounding gap is an indicator, not a certificate (\S\ref{sec:gap}).
Closure fidelity and input sufficiency are varied separately: when $\zstar$ is sufficient, $\Gamma$
tracks $\Bclos$ as fidelity varies
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}'s proxy is exact); when $\zstar$ is lossy but the closure is \emph{well specified},
$\Bclos=0$ throughout yet $\Gamma>0$ grows with the discarded variance. A
positive grounding gap can thus be pure input insufficiency, not closure error---so closure-dominance
is certified by the convention-independent decomposition, and $\Gamma$ only corroborates it.
