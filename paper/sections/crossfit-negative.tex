\subsection{A pre-declared attempt to tighten the insufficiency bound, and its failure}
\label{sec:si-crossfit}

\emph{What was declared, and when.} In the solubility domain $\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ is the
binning bound throughout: the within-bin variance of $m$ over eight equal-count bins of the scalar
$g(\zstar)$, whose slack is the information the binning destroys and is not estimable from these
data (\S\ref{sec:estimator}). The other domain does not coarsen---in the $\mathrm pK_a$ closure the same
quantity is the out-of-fold residual mean square of a cross-fitted forest of $m$ on $\zstar$
(\S\ref{sec:si-pka})---and because every margin in the map is
$\mathrm{MSE}-2\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$, a regressor that predicts $m$ from $\zstar$ better than an
eight-bin histogram of a scalar summary of $\zstar$ does would raise all of them at once. The
experiment that asks whether it does was pre-declared, in the header of the estimator itself
(sha256 \texttt{d158989fe9cfdc79}\ldots, the full digest
deposited beside it), written and staged in version control before any cross-fitted margin
existed. It fixed in advance: the feature set ($\zstar$ and nothing else---the two
$\sigma$-profiles, their areas and $T$, $105$ dimensions---with $v_{\mathrm{cosmo}}$ excluded
because it is an argument of the full convention only, so conditioning on it would not bound the
residual-only convention's $\Binsuf$); the regressor, the $\mathrm pK_a$ domain's own, untuned, with a ridge
sensitivity reported beside it and never adopted; the folds, five-fold grouped on the molecule pair, with a
source-grouped variant reported beside every cell; the row set, $473$ of the $477$, the four rows
whose solutes carry no VT-2005 profile dropped; an outcome-blind validity gate; a ban on forming
the per-cell minimum of the two bounds; the rule fixing which estimator the paper would adopt; and
three verdicts. Nothing above the declaration was edited afterwards, and both
bounds are deposited for every one of the $1164$ cells.

\emph{It did not tighten.} On the same $473$ rows, broad set, row unit, primary folds, the
cross-fitted bound is $\Binsuf^{\mathrm{cf}}=0.856$ against the binning bound's $0.704$ (residual-only
convention) and $0.489$ (full)---looser by $22\%$ and by $75\%$. It is looser having
\emph{passed} its own gate: no pair straddles a fold, the global out-of-fold $R^2$ is $+0.823$, and
the value sits well above the deposited replicate floor $\Ex[\Var(m\mid\text{pair})]=0.046$. This
is the estimator working and losing, not failing. The arithmetic of the loss is one comparison:
with $\Var(m)=4.85$ on these rows, a fitted bound beats the binning bound only above
$R^2=1-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{bin}}/\Var(m)$, which is $0.855$ under the residual-only convention and $0.899$
under the full one. The forest reached $0.823$.

Every consequence runs against this work, and the numbers are printed here for that reason. The
glycol-ether margin---the paper's one surviving stratified finding---falls from $+2.036$ to
$+1.757$ at the fixed estimator cell, its $90\%$ two-way cluster interval from $[+1.25,+2.87]$ to
$[+1.02,+2.49]$ and its leave-one-publication-out envelope from $[+1.597,+2.866]$ to
$[+1.342,+2.339]$, on the same $182$ rows either side---no dropped row carries a glycol-ether
solvent. The envelopes are exact deletion extrema and print to three decimals. The two intervals
are not on the same footing: $[+1.25,+2.87]$ is the two-way bootstrap run to convergence at
$1.2\times10^{8}$ draws, while $[+1.02,+2.49]$ is one $3000$-draw replicate of this scoring pass,
whose bootstrap is seeded per stratum name rather than per row set. That
row set is drawn here under three names and prints $[+1.02,+2.49]$, $[+1.05,+2.47]$ and
$[+1.02,+2.49]$, so the second decimal of the cross-fitted endpoint is seed and not estimator.
The fall it is quoted for is larger than that spread by six times on the lower endpoint and
twenty-four on the upper, which is why the comparison survives its own precision.
The map is poorer in every count that reads a margin: admissible cells $35\to32$,
strata admissible in all four unit\,$\times$\,convention cells $6\to5$, and distinct admissible
positive row sets after the maximality reduction $2\to1$, the alkanes being the row set that
drops. The count of \emph{boundable} strata does not move, $15$ of $59$ under both estimators,
because boundability is the $n\ge40$ test and the declaration froze it. The retired whole-set
aggregate does not return: its margin falls from $+0.944$ to $+0.211$ (full) and from $+0.506$ to
$+0.203$ (residual-only), and it now fails the deletion test outright, two publications flipping its
sign. Nor does the chemistry-blind multiplicity null improve where it matters. \emph{Both} arms of
that null are re-run on the $473$ rows, so the comparison is like for like and its binning-bound
column is not the one the rest of the submission prints: $p(C)$ moves from $0.297$ to $0.255$, but
$p(D)$, the null $p$ of the largest
certified margin, moves the wrong way, $0.094\to0.137$; conditioned on drawing a map that certifies
anything at all, a chemistry-blind relabelling reaches the observed maximum in $54\%$ of draws
against $16\%$ under the binning bound. The declared verdict is therefore \textsc{loss} on branch L1,
with L2 firing independently. Restricting $477\to473$ rows leaves the map itself
untouched---the four dropped rows leave the $477$-row baseline's $35/6/2$ and its $+2.036$ exactly
where they were---but it does move the null: on all $477$ the same binning bound gives $p(C)=0.286$
and $p(D)=0.1005$, that is $17\%$ of certifying draws. Every statement of this null outside this
subsection is that $477$-row one.

\emph{A deviation from the declaration, named as one.} The declaration's Sec.~3 fixed an
unconditional rule---``\textsc{the rule}. The cross-fitted estimator headlines
\textsc{unconditionally} \ldots\ provided it passes the validity gate of Sec.~3.1, which never
reads a margin, an $\mathrm{MSE}$, a $g$ or a stratum label''---and spelled out what it committed
to: ``If the cross-fit is \textsc{looser} than the binning bound, the headline margins
\textsc{shrink}, cells that are certified today lose their certification, and the map gets poorer.
That outcome is reported as the result, in the paper, with the same prominence a gain would have
had.'' \textbf{We did not follow that rule.} The binning bound remains the estimator of record, and
every margin printed in this paper is the binning bound's. The reason is that the rule was written
against one hazard---choosing the smaller bound cell by cell after seeing the cells, which is
biased low and which the paper disowns elsewhere---and it did not anticipate the other, that one
valid bound would be uniformly \emph{looser} than the other. Where that happens, obeying the rule
prints a bound that is valid and strictly less informative: every claim weakens and none becomes
more correct. What we did instead is what the declaration's Sec.~2 already required
independently---both bounds, both margins and their difference are printed for every cell of the
deposited map, the cells that favour this paper and the cells that do not, so that the poorer map
is legible in the deposit and every number in the
paragraph above re-derivable from it. Two things about this deviation should be said rather than absorbed. It runs in
this paper's favour, which is exactly why it is named here instead of being folded into a methods
sentence. And it is a choice made \emph{after} seeing a number, which is what the declaration
existed to prevent; what limits it is that the number is one global comparison of two bounds on one
row set, not a per-cell selection, and that the alternative is on the page.

\emph{Why the binning bound wins here, and where it would not.} The mechanism is measurable, and it is
not the obvious one. It was established on synthetic data matched to the real
design---$477$ rows in $185$ pairs from $15$ sources, the real rows-per-pair multiplicities, real
$\sigma$-profiles drawn from the deposited $1432$-row artifact so the regressor faces the real
collinear geometry, and $\Binsuf$ known in closed form by construction. The binning bound is handed
$g$: a physics-built statistic that was designed to predict $m$ and is close to sufficient for
$\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$, so binning it starts from the answer. The forest is handed $\zstar$ and must
rediscover $g$ from about $145$ of the $181$ pair groups in each training fold. \emph{It is not a
dimensionality effect}: coarsening the regressor's features to the leading unsupervised principal
components of $\zstar$---a legitimate operation on the bound, since a smaller $\sigma$-field can
only raise $\Ex[\Var(m\mid\cdot)]$---never helps. On the synthetic design the raw $105$ dimensions
give the tightest cross-fitted bound of the six feature sets tried (median $3.39$, against $3.71$ at
eight components, $4.04$ at sixteen, $4.59$ at four, $5.00$ at two and $6.10$ at one), and all six
sit above the eight-bin binning bound's $2.3$--$2.6$ on the same draws. What separates the two
estimators is sample size alone: the ratio of the two bounds falls from $3.2$ at $n{=}40$ to $1.24$
at $n{=}960$, and a log--log extrapolation of the two curves crosses near $n\approx2\times10^3$
rows, three to four times the $473$ actually scored ($1.7\times10^3$ fitting all eight sizes,
$2.0\times10^3$ omitting the two smallest; it is an extrapolation and is quoted as one). The same
account explains why the cross-fitted estimator is the right one in the $\mathrm pK_a$ domain and is not an
inconsistency between the two: there $\zstar$ is four series labels and a scalar, $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$
is estimable directly within a scaffold at the sample sizes available, and the closure confers no
head start because there is no high-dimensional statistic left for it to summarise
(\S\ref{sec:si-pka}). One further asymmetry is worth recording because a maximum-over-strata
statistic inherits it: across the $15$ boundable strata the binning bound has mean $0.315$ and
standard deviation $0.213$, the cross-fitted one $0.658$ and $0.519$, and the same ordering holds
under a chemistry-blind relabelling ($0.241$ against $0.553$, median over draws), so the extra
spread is a property of the estimator and not of the chemistry---which is why $p(D)$ worsened while
the bound merely loosened.

\emph{The design rule this yields.} For anyone reusing this instrument on another closure, the
choice of $\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ is not a choice between an old estimator and a modern one. Both
are valid upper bounds; the choice is about power, and it turns on three things that are known
before any margin is formed. \emph{(i)}~If the closure's own output $g(\zstar)$ is available as a
feature and is near-sufficient for $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$---the normal case when $g$ is a physical model
built to predict $m$---coarsening $g$ begins from a statistic the regressor would have to learn, and
it wins at every sample size that does not let the regressor learn it. \emph{(ii)}~If $\dim(\zstar)$
is small enough that $\Ex[m\mid\zstar]$ is directly estimable---a handful of labels and a scalar, as
in the $\mathrm pK_a$ domain---condition directly and do not coarsen, since the coarsening would then
discard information for no return. \emph{(iii)}~Between those poles the crossing is a sample-size
question with a computable answer: fit both and prefer the regressor only if its out-of-fold $R^2$
exceeds $1-\Binsuf^{\mathrm{bin}}/\Var(m)$, the requirement being a function of $(m,\zstar,g)$ that
reads no $\mathrm{MSE}$ and no margin. Here that requirement was $0.855$ and the forest returned
$0.823$, which is the whole of the result. The rule must be declared before the margins are read;
choosing per cell between two valid bounds after seeing them is biased low, and choosing once and
globally after seeing them, as we did above, is a disclosed deviation and not a method.

\emph{The fold warning, which is the part that can invalidate a bound rather than merely loosen it.}
The cross-fitted quantity is an upper bound only if the regressor is independent of the held-out
row's label, and on clustered data that is a property of the fold scheme, not of the estimator. This
was measured before the real rows were touched, against known truth. With pair-grouped or
source-grouped folds the estimator is a genuine upper bound: $0$ violations in $636$ cells over the
declared evaluation scope---fit once globally, evaluate on label-blind sub-strata, both grouped
schemes, random and chemically coherent sub-strata alike---including all $100$ cells at $n{=}40$,
the map's own boundability threshold. With row-level folds it is not a bound at all: $9.4\%$ of
cells ($18$ of $192$) fall below truth in the realistic regime, $\Binsuf/\Var(m)\le0.30$, and
$71.9\%$ ($46$ of $64$) at $0.60$. In the null design, where $m$ is independent of $\zstar$ and the
truth is $\Var(m)=4.85$, row-level folds return $1.73$ under a pair shock and $1.42$ under a source
shock---$64\%$ and $71\%$ \emph{below} truth. The numerical gates do not catch this. The leaky
version returns out-of-fold $R^2=+0.61$ and $+0.64$ in exactly those two cells and clears the
replicate-floor check by a factor of thirty; the only gate that fires is the assertion that no
group straddles a fold, which is a statement about the folds and not a number. \textbf{One deposited
number is compromised by this and must not be cited as a bound}: the deposited
decomposition artifact records an out-of-fold forest upper bound of $0.5646$, fitted on row-level folds, with no
grouping. Nothing in this paper rests on it: Table~\ref{tab:bounds} carries the audit artifact's
$0.621$ instead, and the disagreement between the two forests is reported above. The deposit now
carries the warning in the file itself, beside the number. The scheme's exposure differs by set and
both readings reach the same verdict: on the VT-2005-matched set where that number lives no two
rows share a pair, but $60$ rows carry $41$ solutes and $17$ solvents, so every held-out row has a
near-twin in training; on the broad set the pair channel is open outright, $359$ of the $477$ rows
sharing a molecule pair with another row (\S\ref{sec:si-repro}), and blocking it moves the same
forest from $0.15$ to $0.88$. The cross-fitted map, its per-cell table, its multiplicity null, its
within-stratum sensitivity, its verdict and its synthetic validation are all deposited, together with
the declaration above, the scoring pass and the validation run that produced them.
