\subsection{Grounding the crystal factor: a dose-limited null} \label{sec:e2}

A natural test of whether the ceiling is inputs rather than closure is to ground a \emph{different} factor---the crystal term---on abundant external data. We trained the identical model without and with an external single-component crystal-property pool of roughly $15{,}000$ melting points, added through the auxiliary-stream mechanism after excluding any pool solute whose Bemis--Murcko scaffold appears in the validation or test split. The pool did not improve prediction of the crystal properties it supplies (melting-point MAE $45.0\to46.6$~K, effectively unchanged, if anything marginally worse), and downstream solubility did not improve either ($\lnx$ MAE $1.81\to1.88$; the two nominally favorable shifts---$\lnx$ $R^2$ $0.320\to0.355$ and crystal offset $|\delta\Phicry|$ $4.99\to4.49$---are slight and do not amount to a gain). We flag this as \emph{dose-limited and inconclusive rather than a clean null}. In this run the crystal auxiliary stream was homeopathically dosed---a small fraction of optimizer steps at low loss weight, so the model saw the external pool less than one full pass at the selected checkpoint---and a stream that did not even lower the MAE of the property it directly supervises cannot license a strong conclusion. What it shows is that \emph{this dilute intervention} does not move the ceiling, not that external crystal labels cannot. We therefore do \emph{not} read it through the activity-path $\Bclos/\Binsuf$ decomposition: the crystal ``closure'' is the near-exact ideal-solubility term $\Phi=(\dHfus/\Rgas)(1/T-1/\Tm)$, not a misspecified map, so the flat $\Tm$ recovery is better read as the crystal/activity split's structural non-identifiability (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}) and the encoder's transfer limitation (\S\ref{sec:supporting}) than as closure dominance. A properly dosed crystal-grounding test is left to future work.
