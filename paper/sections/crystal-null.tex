\subsection{Grounding the crystal factor does not help either} \label{sec:e2}

If the accuracy ceiling reflected a shortage of information about the physical inputs, then grounding a factor other than the activity coefficient on abundant external data should relax the constraint. We tested this on the crystal term by training the identical model without and with an external single-component crystal-property pool of roughly $15{,}000$ melting points, added through the auxiliary-stream mechanism after excluding any pool solute whose Bemis-Murcko scaffold appears in the validation or test split. The pool did not improve prediction of the crystal properties it supplies: the melting-point MAE was $45.0$~K without the stream and $46.6$~K with it, effectively unchanged and if anything marginally worse. Downstream solubility accuracy did not improve either, with $\lnx$ MAE moving from $1.81$ to $1.88$. The two quantities that shifted in the nominally favorable direction moved only slightly and do not amount to a gain: $\lnx$ $R^2$ went from $0.320$ to $0.355$, and the systematic crystal offset $|\delta\Phicry|$ from $4.99$ to $4.49$. We read the whole panel as a null. Supplying the crystal factor with the external labels that determine it neither sharpens $\Tm$/$\dHfus$ recovery nor improves solubility, which is what the grounding paradox predicts: the binding constraint is the specification of the closure ($\Bclos$), not the coverage of the inputs fed through it ($\Binsuf$), so pouring more single-component data into a well-covered factor cannot move the ceiling.
