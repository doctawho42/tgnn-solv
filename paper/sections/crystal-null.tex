\subsection{Grounding the crystal factor: a dose-limited null} \label{sec:e2}

Whether the ceiling lies in the inputs rather than the closure can be tested by grounding a \emph{different} factor---the crystal term---on abundant external data. We trained the identical model without and with an external single-component crystal-property pool of roughly $15{,}000$ melting points, added through the grounding-stream mechanism after excluding any pool solute whose Bemis--Murcko scaffold appears in the validation or test split. The pool did not improve prediction of the crystal properties it supplies (melting-point MAE $45.0\to46.6$~K, a negligible change in the unfavorable direction), and downstream solubility did not improve either ($\lnx$ MAE $1.81\to1.88$; two nominally favorable but slight shifts, in $\lnx$ $R^2$ and the crystal offset $|\delta\Phicry|$, do not amount to a gain). This result is \emph{dose-limited and inconclusive rather than an unambiguous null}. In this experiment the crystal grounding stream was applied at a low dose (a small fraction of optimizer steps at low loss weight), so at the selected checkpoint the model had completed less than one full pass over the external pool, and a stream that does not lower the MAE of the property it directly supervises cannot justify a strong conclusion. The result shows that \emph{this low-dose intervention} does not move the ceiling, not that external crystal labels cannot. The activity-path $\Bclos/\Binsuf$ decomposition is uninformative here: the crystal ``closure'' is the near-exact ideal-solubility term $\Phi=(\dHfus/\Rgas)(1/T-1/\Tm)$, not a misspecified map, so the flat $\Tm$ recovery is more consistent with the crystal/activity split's structural non-identifiability (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident}) and the encoder's transfer limitation (\S\ref{sec:supporting}) than with closure dominance. A properly dosed crystal-grounding test is left to future work.
