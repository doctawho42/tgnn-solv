\subsection{Grounding does not pay off in the low-data regime either}
\label{sec:data-efficiency}
% Artifact: results/data_efficiency/summary.json via run_data_efficiency_summary.py
% over the Modal run (volume tgnn-e5, seed 42); ΔMAE = physics − DirectGNN.
The strongest a-priori case for a physics prior is data scarcity: with few training
molecules, an inductive bias toward the correct physical factorization should beat a
data-hungry black box even when the two tie at full data. We test this directly.
Subsampling the training set \emph{by solute} at fractions $0.05$--$1.0$ (the regime
that governs scaffold transfer), we train the physics-grounded model and DirectGNN on
the identical subsample and evaluate both on the fixed scaffold test
(Table~\ref{tab:data-eff}; seed 42, full-corpus run). The physics arm is \emph{worse at
every fraction}, and---against the hypothesis---the gap is \emph{smallest} at the least
data ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=+0.08$ at $5\%$) and \emph{grows} with more
($+1.0$ at full); its $R^2$ is negative throughout. In the language of
Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}, the critical budget $n^\star$ appears to sit below our smallest fraction.
We do not over-read this: the sweep is single-seed (seed 42), the low-data gap is small
($+0.08$ at $5\%$), and the physics-arm MAEs are non-monotone across fractions, so we take it as
\emph{consistent with}---not a sharp confirmation of---the sign rule, and leave multi-seed error
bars at each fraction to future work. Even in the regime built to favor it, grounding does not
buy accuracy; if anything, more data lets the black box pull
further ahead. The one metric that inverts is top-1 solvent ranking, higher for the
physics arm at $5\%$ ($0.42$ vs $0.23$)---consistent with the ranking-versus-accuracy
split of \S\ref{sec:supporting}---but that decision-quality signal does not translate
into lower error. This is the last regime in which a physics prior should plausibly
have helped; that it does not closes the accuracy case and leaves the structural
account of \S\ref{sec:framework} and \S\ref{sec:measure}---a non-identifiable split fed
through a misspecified closure---as the operative explanation.
