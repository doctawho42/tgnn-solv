\subsection{Grounding does not pay off in the low-data regime either}
\label{sec:data-efficiency}
% Artifact: results/data_efficiency/summary.json via run_data_efficiency_summary.py
% over the Modal run (volume tgnn-e5, seed 42); ΔMAE = physics − DirectGNN.
The strongest a-priori case for a physics prior is data scarcity: with few training molecules, an
inductive bias toward the correct factorization should beat a data-hungry black box. We test it
directly, subsampling the training set \emph{by solute} at fractions $0.05$--$1.0$ and training both
the physics-grounded model and DirectGNN on the identical subsample, evaluated on the fixed scaffold
test (Table~\ref{tab:data-eff}, seed 42). The physics arm is \emph{worse at every fraction}, and
against the hypothesis the gap is \emph{smallest} at the least data ($\Delta\mathrm{MAE}=+0.08$ at
$5\%$) and \emph{grows} with more ($+1.0$ at full); its $R^2$ is negative throughout. We do not
draw strong conclusions from a single-seed sweep with non-monotone physics-arm MAEs, and the light per-fraction budget
leaves the physics arm more under-converged than the control, so we take this as \emph{consistent
with}, not a sharp confirmation of, the sign rule (Prop.~\ref{prop:tax}). The one inverting metric is
top-1 solvent ranking ($0.42$ vs $0.23$ at $5\%$; the ranking-versus-accuracy split of
\S\ref{sec:supporting}), which does not translate into lower error. This is the last regime a physics
prior should plausibly help; that it does not is consistent with the broad accuracy null, alongside
the structural account---a non-identifiable split through a misspecified closure---as the operative
explanation.
