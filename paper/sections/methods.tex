\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
The model is a controlled comparison: a shared graph backbone feeds either an
explicit thermodynamic bottleneck (\emph{TGNNSolv}) or a direct regressor
(\emph{DirectGNN}) that emits \lnx\ with no physics. The gap between them is the cost or benefit of
the bottleneck confounded with the arms' differing inputs and tuning, stated at the control below.
Architecture, encoder variants, COSMO-SAC constants, solver settings, optimisation settings and the
full loss taxonomy are in \S\ref{sec:si-methods}.

\subsection{Data, splits, and labels}
Solubility measurements come from BigSolDB~2.0 \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb},
partitioned by Bemis--Murcko scaffold so every test solute is unseen at
training; row membership is set by a
melting-point-independent miscibility/structure filter, and crystal $\Tm$,
$\dHfus$, Hansen parameters, and infinite-dilution activity coefficients \lngi\
are merged on as per-solute labels where available. Two data hazards are handled
explicitly: melting-point tables arrive in either $^\circ$C or Kelvin and are unit-detected by
column median (an undetected double conversion inflates every $\Tm$ by $273$\,K), and the seeded
scaffold split is not stable across pipeline versions, so every regeneration is treated as
a new split. Crystal supervision is scarce---the test and validation splits contain essentially no
measured $\dHfus$, the quantitative basis of the identifiability argument
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). External single-component data used for grounding are an
open crystal-property pool ($\sim$15{,}000 melting points), the VT-2005
$\sigma$-profile database, and ThermoML infinite-dilution activity coefficients.
The released stream builder excludes any pool molecule whose scaffold appears in validation
or test and aborts on a missing split file --- but that is a property of the builder, not a
certified property of the runs reported here. One $\sigma$-stream build on disk was
generated against the wrong split files, $91$ of its $1425$ rows being held-out solutes by
canonical SMILES, and per-run stream provenance was not logged, so the artifacts cannot certify
which build fed which arm (\S\ref{sec:si-repro} gives the file-order evidence, the three facts that
bound what a leak could do, and the one molecule it touches in the $n{=}44$ analysis of
\S\ref{sec:surrogate}). We therefore do not assert the scaffold guarantee for these runs.

\subsection{Encoder, heads, and the closure under study}
Solute and solvent are encoded by a \emph{shared} message-passing neural network, interact through a
bipartite cross-attention block, and are combined into a pair representation that all heads consume;
two interchangeable encoder alternatives do not outperform it (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}). A fusion
head predicts the two crystal properties under bounded parametrisations, giving the ideal term
$\Phicry(T)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sle} with the constant-$\Delta C_p$ correction omitted (audited in
\S\ref{sec:si-mech}). The activity term \lng\ comes from one of three differentiable closures. NRTL
(Non-Random Two-Liquid \cite{renon1968}), the most expressive, fits interaction parameters per pair,
optionally regularised by a UNIFAC group-contribution
prior \cite{fredenslund1975}; its symmetric one-parameter collapse gives
$\lng(x_2,T)=\lngi(T)(1-x_2)^2$, the minimal model in which the identifiability analysis is cleanest
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). The closure under study is instead parameter-free: a $\sigma$-profile head
emits a $51$-bin screening-charge-density profile $p(\sigma)$ per molecule (area per bin, summing to
the cavity area $A$), and a COSMO-SAC layer (Lin--Sandler / VT-2005 conventions,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:cosmo} in App.~\ref{sec:si-methods}) maps the profile pair to \lng\ through a damped fixed point for the segment
activity coefficient $\Gamma_{S}$ (distinct from the grounding gap $\Ggap$), with an optional
Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term---the full/residual-only distinction on which
Table~\ref{tab:claims} and \S\ref{sec:measure} turn (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}). This closure over a
network-\emph{predicted} $\sigma$-profile, and the intervention that swaps it for the external
reference, is the object of study (\S\ref{sec:paradox}).

The dispersion-off closures in the

2002, Hsieh for 2010), the specified use of each closure; an input-fixed control that varies the
kernel alone is reported in \S\ref{sec:measure}(iii). The 2002 layer reproduces the NIST reference
COSMO-SAC-2002~\cite{bell2020cosmosac} to RMSE $0.0035\,\ln\gamma$ on identical VT-2005 profiles, and
our differentiable COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp layer~\cite{hsieh2010,hsieh2014dsp}---built on the
\emph{typed} latent, three donor/acceptor-resolved $\sigma$-profiles per molecule in place of one,
read by a matching typed hydrogen-bonding kernel---reproduces the NIST 2010
reference to RMSE $1.4\times10^{-4}\,\ln\gamma$ with dispersion \emph{off}, that second figure
quoted from its validation run's console output, which deposited no artifact. The dispersion term is
\emph{not} validated to that standard --- it carries the tabulated coefficient at a single sign, and
no $\epsilon$ is tabulated for the three matched bromine- or iodine-containing pairs --- which is why
every dispersion-off contrast is computed on our layers and cross-checked against the NIST
\texttt{cCOSMO} reference while the \emph{2010, dispersion on} runs are quoted from \texttt{cCOSMO} itself. The
layer definitions, the validation figures and the full scope of the dispersion caveat are in
\S\ref{sec:si-methods}.

\subsection{Solver, oracle substitution, and training}
Given $\Phicry(T)$ and a closure for \lng, the saturated mole fraction solves
$\lnx=-\Phicry(T)-\lng(x_2,T)$, a fixed point in $x_2$ that we iterate under damping, taking
gradients through the implicit-function theorem at the converged $x_2^\star$ rather than through the
iterations. A bounded, gated ``adaptive physics correction'' perturbs the physical
parameters ($\Tm,\dHfus,\tau$) rather than the output and re-solves the SLE, so the physical
decomposition survives it.

The \emph{oracle substitution} replaces
the predicted $\sigma$-profile $\hat z$ with the reference VT-2005 profile $\zstar$ before the
closure (matched by canonical SMILES). It is applied three ways to rule out implementation
artifacts: at evaluation only; injected at training time so the model co-adapts; and as a
channel-swap control, which injects it at training under a coordinate-descent schedule that freezes
the crystal branch during the SLE phase, so that only the activity branch refits against it. The
last two are at seed $42$ only
(\S\ref{sec:si-tables}). An analogous
override substitutes reference crystal targets. The activity target for the decomposition
(\S\ref{sec:measure}) is the infinite-dilution $\lngi=\lim_{x_2\to0}\lng$, on two sets that differ in
their reference database and their regime (\S\ref{sec:measure}(i)--(ii)).

Optimisation follows a three-phase curriculum---auxiliary pretraining, SLE training,
stabilisation---under a weighted loss whose taxonomy and per-phase weights are in
\S\ref{sec:si-methods}. External single-component data enter through \emph{grounding streams}, the
training-time sense of grounding and a separate operation from the evaluation-time oracle
substitution above: each stream is a sidecar of \emph{self-solvent} rows
($\mathrm{solvent}=\mathrm{solute}$, no solubility label, only the target factor's mask active),
interleaved into training at a configurable step budget under the scaffold-leak guard above. In
Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox} the \emph{grounded} arms use the stream, the \emph{ungrounded} arm sets its
budget to zero, and the \emph{$\sigma$-oracle} arm is a grounded checkpoint re-evaluated under the
substitution. The training schedule used for those arms freezes the $\sigma$ head after phase 1,
so the $\sigma$ stream enters the warm-up and phase 1 only (per-arm schedules,
\S\ref{sec:si-tables}). The streams ground the crystal factor (melting-point pool) and the
activity factor ($\sigma$-profile database), identifying an otherwise non-identifiable factor
(\S\ref{sec:framework}) from external data a black-box predictor cannot use.

\subsection{The DirectGNN control, and reproducibility}
DirectGNN shares the encoder, interaction, readout, and pair representation, adds
a thermometer (cumulative-threshold) temperature encoding, and maps directly to \lnx\ with a
bin-weighted Huber loss and no heads, closure, or solver. The two arms therefore share
their representation but not their inputs or their tuning: the control receives an
explicit temperature encoding the physics arm does not, seeing $T$ instead through
$\Phi(T)$ and the closure, and the optimisation settings were tuned per arm and differ.
The TGNNSolv$-$DirectGNN gap is accordingly a comparison of two separately tuned
pipelines that differ in the thermodynamic bottleneck, not an isolation of the
bottleneck; DirectGNN is the reference for every asymptotic physics penalty comparison
in \S\ref{sec:results}, and those comparisons carry that caveat.
The controlled comparisons of \S\ref{sec:paradox} are run at three seeds and reported as
mean$\pm$sd on a cross-arm row set (rows supervised and finite in every arm). Single-seed, and
labelled as such where each is reported, are the training-time and channel-swap
oracle controls, the output-residual arms (\S\ref{sec:gateb}), the data-efficiency curve
(App.~\ref{sec:data-efficiency}) and the loss-simplification ablation
(App.~\ref{sec:si-methods}). Solver iteration counts and their convergence audit, the curriculum, the
loss taxonomy, COSMO-SAC constants, architecture and optimisation hyperparameters, and the compute
ledger are in \S\ref{sec:si-methods} and \S\ref{sec:si-repro}. The two audit runs behind
\S\ref{sec:measure} are named there with the script and the artifact file of each;
the repository that would let those scripts be run is a placeholder here, and not every artifact
they read is small enough to version (limitation~(x), \S\ref{sec:discussion}).
