\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
The model is a controlled comparison: a shared graph backbone feeds either an
explicit thermodynamic bottleneck (\emph{TGNNSolv}) or a direct regressor
(\emph{DirectGNN}) that emits \lnx\ with no physics. The gap between them is the cost or benefit of
the bottleneck confounded with the arms' differing inputs and tuning, stated at the control below.

\subsection{Data, splits, and labels}
Solubility measurements come from BigSolDB~2.0 \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb},
partitioned by Bemis--Murcko scaffold so every test solute is unseen at
training; row membership is set by a
melting-point-independent miscibility/structure filter, and crystal $\Tm$,
$\dHfus$, Hansen parameters, and infinite-dilution activity coefficients \lngi\
are merged on as per-solute labels where available. Two data hazards are handled
explicitly. Melting-point tables arrive in either $^\circ$C or Kelvin and are unit-detected by
column median (an undetected double conversion inflates every $\Tm$ by $273$\,K); and the seeded
scaffold split is not stable across pipeline versions, so every regeneration is treated as
a new split. Crystal supervision is
scarce: the test and validation splits contain essentially no measured $\dHfus$,
the quantitative basis of the identifiability argument
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). External single-component data used for grounding are an
open crystal-property pool ($\sim$15{,}000 melting points), the VT-2005
$\sigma$-profile database, and ThermoML infinite-dilution activity coefficients;
the stream builder excludes any pool molecule whose scaffold appears in validation
or test and aborts on a missing split file. One $\sigma$-stream build on disk was
nonetheless generated against the wrong split files: $106$ of its $1425$ rows
overlap the test$\,\cup\,$validation union by scaffold, $91$ being held-out solutes;
\S\ref{sec:si-repro} audits which arms it reaches and what it does and does not compromise.

\subsection{Graph encoder and pair representation}
Solute and solvent are encoded by a \emph{shared} message-passing neural network (MPNN); two
interchangeable alternatives (a graph transformer and a variant whose message channels are
supervised toward the Hansen parameters) are in the SI (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}) and do not
outperform it. Small hydride solvents such as water carry explicit hydrogens so the polar and
hydrogen-bond channels activate. Solute and solvent embeddings interact through a
bipartite cross-attention/message-passing block and a physics-aware readout, and
are combined into a pair representation
$[\,g_s,\,g_v,\,g_s\!\odot g_v,\,|g_s-g_v|\,]$ passed through an MLP, optionally
augmented with RDKit descriptors, Morgan fingerprints, and an ionic-context
feature. All heads below consume this pair representation.

\subsection{Crystal head and the ideal term}
A fusion head predicts the two crystal properties defining the ideal (activity-free) solubility under
bounded parametrisations ($\Tm\in[100,700]$\,K, $\dHfus>0$; optional Walden-entropy and
group-contribution $\Tm$ modes are in the SI), giving the ideal term
\begin{equation}
  \Phicry(T)=\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\left(\frac1T-\frac1\Tm\right),
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
the constant-$\Delta C_p$ correction omitted (audited in \S\ref{sec:si-mech}).

\subsection{Three interchangeable activity closures}
The activity term \lng\ is produced by one of three closures, all differentiable
and all consuming the same pair representation.

\paragraph{Two baseline closures.} NRTL (Non-Random Two-Liquid \cite{renon1968}), the most
expressive of the three, predicts two interaction energies with a $1/T$ temperature law and a
non-randomness factor, optionally regularised by a UNIFAC group-contribution prior
\cite{fredenslund1975}. Its symmetric
one-parameter collapse \texttt{gamma\_inf} gives the analytic infinite-dilution law
$\lng(x_2,T)=\lngi(T)\,(1-x_2)^2$ with $\lngi$ affine in $1/T$, the minimal model in which the
identifiability analysis is cleanest (\S\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{Differentiable COSMO-SAC.} A $\sigma$-profile head emits, for each
molecule, a $51$-bin screening-charge-density profile $p(\sigma)$ (area per bin,
summing to the cavity area $A$). A parameter-free COSMO-SAC layer (Lin--Sandler /
VT-2005 conventions) maps the pair of profiles to \lng\ by a damped fixed point
for the segment activity coefficient $\Gamma_{S}$ (distinct from the grounding gap $\Gamma$),
\begin{equation}
  \resizebox{0.98\columnwidth}{!}{$\displaystyle
  \Gamma_{S}(\sigma_m)=\Big[\textstyle\sum_n p_{\mathrm{norm}}(\sigma_n)\,\Gamma_{S}(\sigma_n)\,
  \exp\!\big(-\Delta W(\sigma_m,\sigma_n)/RT\big)\Big]^{-1}$},
  \label{eq:cosmo}
\end{equation}
with a precomputed exchange-energy matrix $\Delta W$ (electrostatic misfit plus a
hydrogen-bonding term); the restoring contribution is
$\lng^{\mathrm{res}}=(A_2/a_{\mathrm{eff}})\sum_m p_2^{\mathrm{pure}}(\sigma_m)
[\ln\Gamma_{S,\mathrm{mix}}(\sigma_m)-\ln\Gamma_{S,2}^{\mathrm{pure}}(\sigma_m)]$, and
an optional Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term uses the molecular cavity
volume and area, its size factor $r_i=V_i/r_0$ taking the \emph{reference} COSMO
cavity volume so that it shares the cavity basis of the area $A_i$. This closure --- COSMO-SAC over a
network-\emph{predicted} $\sigma$-profile, and the intervention that swaps it for
the external reference --- is the object of study in the grounding analysis
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}).

\paragraph{In-house closures are the primary instrument.} The dispersion-off closures in the
fidelity measurement (\S\ref{sec:measure}) are our own differentiable implementations, so those
arms share one code path. Each consumes its own model-prescribed $\sigma$-averaging (Mullins for
2002, Hsieh for 2010), the specified use of each closure. An input-fixed \emph{control} outside
that use---the 2002 kernel on the prescribed sum of the typed profiles (Eq.~9 of
Ref.~\cite{bell2020cosmosac})---varies the kernel alone: $\mathrm{MSE}\ 2.047$ against the 2010
kernel's $0.765$ on the matched set, both with the combinatorial term on, so the
\S\ref{sec:measure} contrast does not turn on the averaging rule. The 2002 layer reproduces the NIST reference
COSMO-SAC-2002~\cite{bell2020cosmosac} to RMSE $0.003\,\ln\gamma$ on identical VT-2005
profiles. For the modern arm we implement a differentiable COSMO-SAC-2010/dsp layer~\cite{hsieh2010,hsieh2014dsp} --- the
donor/acceptor-typed three-profile representation, the temperature-dependent electrostatic
misfit $c_{\mathrm{ES}}(T)=A_{\mathrm{ES}}+B_{\mathrm{ES}}/T^2$, the typed hydrogen-bonding
kernel, and an optional dispersion term in the tabulated parameter $\epsilon/k_{\mathrm{B}}$ ---
which reproduces the NIST COSMO-SAC-2010 reference to RMSE $1.4\times10^{-4}\,\ln\gamma$ with
dispersion \emph{off}. The dispersion term is not validated to that standard: it carries the
tabulated coefficient at a single sign, the class-dependent sign reversal of
Ref.~\cite{hsieh2014dsp} being unimplemented and unexercised on the matched set, which holds no
water or carboxylic-acid pair; and no $\epsilon$ is tabulated for the three matched pairs
containing bromine or iodine, which our layer scores at $\epsilon{=}0$. That alone accounts for
the $\mathrm{MSE}\ 7.43$ it records over all $60$; on the remaining $57$ it returns the reference
$0.785$, showing no divergence where both are defined. Every dispersion-off contrast is computed on these layers and
cross-checked against the NIST \texttt{cCOSMO} reference; the two dispersion arms are quoted
from \texttt{cCOSMO} itself (\S\ref{sec:measure}).

\subsection{The solid--liquid-equilibrium (SLE) solver and implicit differentiation}
Given $\Phicry(T)$ and a closure for \lng, the saturated mole fraction solves
$\lnx=-\Phicry(T)-\lng(x_2,T)$, a fixed point in $x_2$ because \lng\ depends on
composition. We iterate a damped map
\begin{equation}
  x_2^{(k+1)}=\lambda\,\exp\!\big(-\Phicry(T)-\lng(x_2^{(k)},T)\big)+(1-\lambda)\,x_2^{(k)},
  \label{eq:sle-iter}
\end{equation}
with closure-specific kernels. Gradients use the implicit-function theorem at the
converged $x_2^\star$ (with $\eta=\partial\lng/\partial x_2$) rather than backpropagation
through the iterations: exact at the fixed point, memory-constant in the iteration count,
and well conditioned unless $1+x_2^\star\eta\to0$. Iteration counts (train $16$, eval $30$)
follow a convergence audit: a shorter $n{=}8$ schedule fails a $5.86$-\lnx\ convergence test on
strongly non-ideal pairs (SI \S\ref{sec:si-methods}).

\subsection{Adaptive correction and the oracle substitution}
A bounded, gated ``adaptive physics correction'' perturbs the physical parameters
($\Tm,\dHfus,\tau$) rather than the output and re-solves the SLE, so the physical
decomposition survives it. The \emph{oracle substitution} replaces
the predicted $\sigma$-profile $\hat z$ with the reference VT-2005 profile $\zstar$ before the
closure (matched by canonical SMILES); it is applied three ways to rule out implementation
artifacts---at evaluation only, injected at training time so the model co-adapts, and as a
channel-swap control, the last two at seed $42$ only (\S\ref{sec:si-tables})---and an analogous
override substitutes reference crystal targets. The activity target for the decomposition (\S\ref{sec:measure})
is the infinite-dilution $\lngi=\lim_{x_2\to0}\lng$ at $298$\,K.

\subsection{Training: three-phase curriculum and single-component grounding}
Optimisation follows a three-phase curriculum: (P1) auxiliary-property
pretraining on crystal, Hansen, and \lngi\ targets with no SLE loss; (P2) full
SLE training, with the adaptive correction unfrozen partway and an optional
crystal$\to$pair weight ramp; (P3) low-learning-rate stabilisation with
monotonicity and van't~Hoff regularisers. External single-component data enter
through \emph{grounding streams}, the training-time sense of grounding and a separate
operation from the evaluation-time oracle substitution above: each stream is a sidecar
of \emph{self-solvent} rows ($\mathrm{solvent}=\mathrm{solute}$, no solubility label,
only the target factor's mask active), interleaved into P1/P2 at a configurable step
budget under the scaffold-leak guard above. In Fig.~\ref{fig:paradox} the
\emph{grounded} arms (learned $\sigma$, with and without the combinatorial term) use
the stream, the \emph{ungrounded} arm sets its budget to zero, and the
\emph{$\sigma$-oracle} arm is a grounded checkpoint re-evaluated under the substitution.
The pinned recipe behind those arms departs from the default:
it freezes the $\sigma$ head for P2/P3, so the $\sigma$ stream --- used only by the
grounded arms, which begin with a warm-up on the pool (up to $40$ epochs, early-stopped
on a held-out tenth) --- enters that warm-up and P1 only. The freeze holds the head's
weights, not the shared encoder, so the predicted profile
still moves end to end; per-arm schedules are in \S\ref{sec:si-tables}. The streams ground the crystal factor (melting-point
pool) and the activity factor ($\sigma$-profile database), identifying an otherwise
non-identifiable factor (\S\ref{sec:framework}) from external data a black-box
predictor cannot use.

\subsection{Objective}
The training loss is a weighted sum with per-phase weights. Only a few terms are
load-bearing here: a bin-weighted Huber on \lnx; auxiliary losses on the
$\sigma$-profile (an earth-mover distance), on crystal $\Tm/\dHfus$, on \lngi, and on
Hansen parameters; and structural regularisers for temperature monotonicity and the
van't~Hoff slope. The remainder (channel orthogonality, contrastive,
mixture-of-experts balance, priors) is auxiliary; the complete taxonomy and weights
are in the SI (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}), the full feature list with the code.

\subsection{The DirectGNN control}
DirectGNN shares the encoder, interaction, readout, and pair representation, adds
a thermometer temperature encoding, and maps directly to \lnx\ with a
bin-weighted Huber loss and no heads, closure, or solver. The two arms therefore share
their representation but not their inputs or their tuning: the control receives an
explicit temperature encoding the physics arm does not, seeing $T$ instead through
$\Phi(T)$ and the closure, and the optimisation settings were tuned per arm and differ.
The TGNNSolv$-$DirectGNN gap is accordingly a comparison of two separately tuned
pipelines that differ in the thermodynamic bottleneck, not an isolation of the
bottleneck; DirectGNN is the reference for every asymptotic physics penalty comparison
in \S\ref{sec:results}, and those comparisons carry that caveat.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Controlled comparisons are run at three seeds and reported as mean$\pm$sd on a
cross-arm intersection lock (rows supervised and finite in every arm); the
exceptions --- the training-time and channel-swap oracle controls and the
output-residual arms (\S\ref{sec:gateb}) --- are single-seed, labelled as such
(\S\ref{sec:si-tables}). Solver iteration counts, COSMO-SAC constants, architecture and
optimisation hyperparameters, and the compute ledger
(full-corpus GPU runs versus CPU-reproducible proxies) are in the SI
(\S\ref{sec:si-methods}, \S\ref{sec:si-repro}); the released code regenerates every figure and
table from committed artifacts.
