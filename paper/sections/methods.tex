\subsection{The composed predictor and its error split}
\label{sec:framework}
For a solute~$s$ in solvent~$v$ at temperature~$T$, the target is $y=\lnx$, the log saturated mole
fraction. Dissolving a solid trades breaking the crystal lattice ($\Phicry$) against mixing the
freed molecules into the solvent ($\lng$), and solid--liquid equilibrium (SLE) sets the
log-solubility to their negative sum:
\begin{equation}
  \lnx = -\underbrace{\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\Big(\tfrac1T-\tfrac1\Tm\Big)}_{\Phicry(T)\ \text{(crystal)}}
         \;-\;\underbrace{\lng(x_2,T)}_{\text{activity}} ,
  \label{eq:sle}
\end{equation}
% "TWICE THE SUPERVISION GAIN" WENT WITH THE FIVE-SEED RE-RUN, 2026-08-18.  The comparison was to a
% three-seed gain of 0.20; that operation now has no established sign (\S\ref{sec:paradox}), so a
% magnitude cannot be set against it at all.  The substitution comparison stands and is kept.
with no constant-$\Delta C_p$ correction ($\Delta C_p$ being the difference in heat capacity
between the liquid and the solid). That term is absent from the displayed equation because it is
absent from the deployed model, which fixes $\Delta C_p=0$, the head that would predict it being off
in the pinned configuration. The exposure is one-signed and substantial: at the group-contribution
value this corpus carries, $60$\,J\,mol$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$, the omitted term is a median $0.32$ in
\lnx\ over the $1318$ labelled test rows carrying a measured $\Tm$, positive on every one of the
$31$ solutes---the size of the substitution penalty of \S\ref{sec:paradox}. It enters the physics
arms, leaves the closure-free control untouched, and cancels exactly in the evaluation-time
substitution, where $\Phicry$ is common to the two arms (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}). It survives into
the training of the $\sigma$ head, fitted through a crystal term carrying a bias of that size and
direction, whose consequence \S\ref{sec:surrogate} reads. The activity term is produced by a
\emph{differentiable closure} $g$. A graph encoder predicts a $\sigma$-profile (the distribution of
screening charge density over a molecule, the histogram a COSMO model consumes) for each molecule,
and COSMO-SAC \cite{lin2002}, a segment-activity reformulation of COSMO-RS, maps the pair of
profiles to $\lng$. The segment-interaction energy inside that map is the closure's \emph{kernel}: the
electrostatic-misfit and hydrogen-bonding terms setting what two surface segments cost each other,
and the part the published revisions rewrite. A closure variant is therefore named for its kernel's
year --- the \emph{2002 kernel} this pipeline runs, and the \emph{2010 kernel} that types
segments as hydrogen-bond donors or acceptors instead of treating hydrogen bonding with one
parameter. Write $\zstar$ for the \emph{reference} latent, a tabulated $\sigma$-profile, and
$\hat z$ for the head's own prediction. \textbf{Two profile databases supply $\zstar$ below and they
are never interchangeable}: \emph{VT-2005}, the Virginia Tech 2005 tabulation \cite{mullins2006},
which the $\sigma$ head is supervised against and which is substituted for $\hat z$ at prediction
time, and \emph{UD}, the University of Delaware successor, which the activity-coefficient rows of
\S\ref{sec:broad} are matched to. Every set, margin and figure below names which it is computed on,
and we read no quantity across them; the one place they are deliberately set against each other is
the database-to-database bracket of \S\ref{sec:badclosure}. Feeding $\zstar$ in place of $\hat z$ (a
$\sigma$-oracle) isolates the closure from encoder error: were the closure well specified,
$g(\zstar)$ would be the best available activity estimate. Each trained configuration, and each
closure variant evaluated, is an \emph{arm}; a \emph{DirectGNN} control arm drops the closure and
emits $\lnx$ directly, and we tuned the two separately (\S\ref{sec:eval}).

We state everything below for a general \emph{composed predictor} $\hat y=g(h(x))$ in which a fixed
closure $g$ consumes a learned physical intermediate $z=h(x)$, against a matched free predictor that
shares the encoder and drops $g$. Solubility is one instance, and a second is the closure of \S\ref{sec:pka-main} for $\mathrm pK_a$,
the acid dissociation constant on a negative logarithmic scale.

Solubility alone underdetermines the crystal/activity split of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sle}. At infinite
dilution with $\Delta C_p=0$ the observable is affine in $1/T$, and the activity intercept and slope
already span that plane, so the profiled Fisher information for $\dHfus$ is exactly zero and the
indeterminacy set is two-dimensional. At finite dilution the composition factor $(1-x_2)^2$ leaves an
information of order $x_2^2$. The deficiency closes under a rank-completing external constraint and
nothing weaker: a direct crystal label, or a fixed activity slope (Lemma~\ref{prop:ident},
\S\ref{sec:si-proofs}). That is why an under-determined factor needs external single-component data
and why we expect compensation between the factors, and it is silent on whether grounding helps.

\subsubsection{The closure--insufficiency decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}

(IDAC) $\lngi$, in \S\ref{sec:pka-main} the measured $\mathrm pK_a$) and $g(\zstar)$ the closure

\begin{lemma}[Exact closure--insufficiency decomposition and its one-sided bounds]\label{prop:decomp}
Let $g$ be a \emph{fixed} (non-fitted) closure and let $\zstar$ denote its \emph{entire}
argument (the profile pair together with the temperature wherever the closure reads one). Then
\emph{(a)}~the reference-input mean squared error splits exactly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \underbrace{\Ex\big[(m-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\text{ref.-input MSE}}
  &= \underbrace{\Ex\big[\Var(m\mid \zstar)\big]}_{\Binsuf\ (\text{irreducible})} \\
  &\quad + \underbrace{\Ex\big[(\Ex[m\mid\zstar]-g(\zstar))^2\big]}_{\Bclos\ (\text{decoder bias})} ,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
the cross term vanishing identically; \emph{(b)}~$\Bclos\ge(\Ex[m]-\Ex[g])^2$;
\emph{(c)}~$\Binsuf\le\Ex[\Var(m\mid\mathrm{bin}(g(\zstar)))]$ for any binning of $g(\zstar)$; and
\emph{(d)}~$\Binsuf$ is a functional of the law of $(m,\zstar)$ alone and does not involve $g$, so
it is identical across closure conventions, while the bound in~(c) is not.
\end{lemma}

Because $\Binsuf$ is bounded above and $\Bclos$ below, a positive \emph{separation margin}
$\mathrm{MSE}-2\Binsuf^{\mathrm{up}}$ establishes that the closure's own error exceeds what input
insufficiency can account for. A margin at or below zero fails to separate and licenses nothing.
\textbf{Its size is a lower bound and not an estimate}: every margin below says the closure's error
exceeds its inputs' by \emph{at least} the number printed, and the design cannot say by how much
more. Over-estimating $\Binsuf$ therefore produces no artifact in what is reported, and a low
cluster-bootstrap frequency is loss of resolution rather than evidence for the inputs
(\S\ref{sec:si-proofs}).

\subsubsection{The decomposition and the grounding gap}
\label{sec:gap}
The decomposition is the measurement the conclusions rest on: assumption-light, needing neither a
representable encoder nor a lossless bottleneck. Beside it we report a directly observable quantity,
the \emph{grounding gap} $\Ggap:=R_{\mathrm{orc}}-R_{\mathrm{e2e}}$, the oracle risk minus the best
end-to-end risk; the paradox is $\Ggap>0$. Under a representable encoder (A1) and a lossless
bottleneck (A2) it is sandwiched $0\le\Ggap\le\Bclos$, so $\Ggap>0$ would certify $\Bclos>0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, \S\ref{sec:si-proofs}). A1 is unverified: the linear probe of
\S\ref{sec:supporting} scores an untrained encoder of the same architecture within its own spread,
so it does not bear on A1. A2 is unverifiable and physically dubious, and when it fails $\Ggap>0$
conflates closure misspecification with input insufficiency, a well-specified closure with a lossy
latent giving $\Bclos=0$ yet $\Ggap>0$. We therefore read $\Ggap$ as corroborating evidence and rest
the model-dominance conclusion on the decomposition.

\paragraph{The sign rule, and scope.}\label{sec:tax} Whether the \emph{trained} physics model
outperforms the \emph{trained} black box at sample size $n$ follows a sign rule.

\begin{remark}[Sign of the physics penalty]\label{prop:tax}
Write $\Ex[\hat R_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)]\approx R_{\mathrm{e2e}}+V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)$ and
$\Ex[\hat R_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]\approx R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}+V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)$, with
$R_{\mathrm{e2e}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}$ the two asymptotic risks, $V(n)\downarrow0$ the
estimation variance of each estimator at budget $n$, so that the \emph{physics penalty at budget
$n$} is
$\mathcal{T}(n)\approx\Delta_\infty+[V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)]$ with
$\Delta_\infty:=R_{\mathrm{e2e}}-R_{\mathrm{direct},\infty}$ its \emph{asymptotic} part and
$D(n):=V_{\mathrm{direct}}(n)-V_{\mathrm{phys}}(n)$ the variance a constrained prior saves. Then
\emph{(i)}~$\mathcal{T}(n)<0$ (physics helps) if and only if $D(n)>\Delta_\infty$: the variance a
constrained prior saves must exceed its asymptotic bias. \emph{(ii)}~If $\Delta_\infty>0$ and the
variance gap vanishes ($D(n)\to0$), there is a critical budget $n^\star$ beyond which
$\mathcal{T}(n)>0$ for all $n>n^\star$.
\end{remark}

\noindent Both clauses are rearrangements of the definitions above, which is why we state this as a
remark: given the two expansions, \emph{(i)} is an identity and \emph{(ii)} its limit. It supplies
vocabulary---a name for the asymptotic part, a name for what a constrained prior saves, and the
budget at which they change places---and no prediction that any measurement below is tested against.
The limiting refinement in which $\Delta_\infty=\Bclos-\Ggap$ when the control is asymptotically
Bayes goes beyond a rearrangement, and is in \S\ref{sec:si-proofs} with the working of both clauses.
The consequence used below is that a prior is predicted to hurt precisely in the mature-data,
low-noise, well-covered regime, and every physics-versus-control difference here is a penalty at the
budget actually trained. We prove no ``optimal grounding'' theorem, and the decomposition leaves
out-of-distribution accuracy where it was. Its variance term $D(n)$ carries the semiparametric
phenomenon in which an estimated propensity outperforms the known-true one
\cite{hirano2003,robins1997}. A fitted nuisance can be the more \emph{efficient} estimator even when
its bias is as large, so a true-valued input can miss the lower total risk at finite $n$.

predictor can achieve. Grouping the raw BigSolDB rows by (solute, solvent, temperature to $1$\,K)

functional on a different base \cite{osanloo2024} and we keep the two apart, pooling and converting
neither into the other; \S\ref{sec:si-baselines} gives the other functionals of this floor and the
comparison.

External single-component data enter through \emph{grounding streams} (the training-time sense of
grounding). Each stream is a sidecar of \emph{self-solvent} rows (no solubility label, only the target

from the melting-point pool and the activity factor from the $\sigma$-profile database, and so

black-box predictor is unable to use. Training runs a three-stage curriculum under a weighted loss, and
the stages are named by number wherever one of them is referred to below: \emph{P1}, a warm-up on

\subsection{Estimating the split: one cell, one convention, one rule}
\label{sec:estimator}

% methods passage and most of it was HOW. What stays is what a reader needs to judge a margin: what
% the instrument returns, the cell it is read at, that the cell was fixed first, how much the

% three are cited by numeral from SECT 3.5.1 and from the SI and cannot move without moving their

The instrument is a procedure taking three inputs and no others: a fixed closure $g$, a table of
reference values $\zstar$ its argument can be read at, and measurements $m$ of its target on rows
where both exist. Per chemically defined row set it returns the closure's own error $\mathrm{MSE}$,
an upper bound $\Binsuf$ on what the inputs leave unresolved, the margin between them, a cluster
interval on that margin, and a verdict of admissible or otherwise. Every margin in this paper is
the binning bound of Lemma~\ref{prop:decomp}(c); the alternative bound of part~(b) is strictly
slacker on both sets and is never used (\S\ref{sec:si-rules}).

Four analyst choices set that bound --- the bin count, where the equal-count bin edges fall, the
within-bin variance convention and the combinatorial convention --- and one setting of them, with
the row or the molecule pair as unit, is an \emph{estimator cell}. All four were fixed before any
margin was seen, a data-set contract in the sense \citet{nael2026dataset} argue for. The cell is
eight equal-count bins of $g(\zstar)$, the unbiased within-bin variance, the row unit and the
residual-only convention, applied identically to every set and stratum whatever its size. The bin
count has the most leverage of the four: it takes the broad set's aggregate margin from $+0.01$ to
$+0.96$. No finding therefore rests on the cell alone --- the same sweep is run for every stratum
the rule passes and reported with it --- and one convention governs both sets rather than the
favourable one on each, which costs the broad set's margin about half its value.

Admissibility asks three things. \emph{(a)} The stratum must be \emph{boundable} at that cell:
$n\ge40$, eight bins holding five rows. \emph{(b)} Its margin must keep its sign when each
contributing source publication is deleted in turn, in all four unit\,$\times$\,convention cells;
that clause is load-bearing, since dropping it lets the set taken whole pass. \emph{(c)} Admissible
cells holding identical rows count once. \S\ref{sec:si-rules} states all four rules in full, with
the class cascade, the demotion test and what each of them costs.
