\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
The model is a controlled comparison: a shared graph backbone feeds either an
explicit thermodynamic bottleneck (\emph{TGNNSolv}) or a direct regressor
(\emph{DirectGNN}) that shares the backbone but emits \lnx\ with no physics. The
gap between them measures what the bottleneck costs or buys. This section
describes the shared backbone, the three interchangeable activity closures, the
solid--liquid-equilibrium (SLE) solver and its implicit differentiation, the
oracle substitution, and the training protocol. Exact architecture and optimisation
hyperparameters and the load-bearing loss weights are in the SI (\S\ref{sec:si-methods}); the full
feature list and the complete ${\sim}30$-term loss taxonomy are released with the code.

\subsection{Data, splits, and labels}
Solubility measurements are drawn from BigSolDB~2.0 \cite{krasnov2025bigsoldb} and
partitioned by Bemis--Murcko scaffold so that
every test solute is unseen at training; row membership is set by a
melting-point-independent miscibility/structure filter, and crystal $\Tm$,
$\dHfus$, Hansen parameters, and infinite-dilution activity coefficients \lngi\
are merged on as per-solute labels where available. Two data hazards are handled
explicitly. Melting-point tables arrive in either $^\circ$C or Kelvin and are
auto-detected by column median (a past double-conversion inflated every $\Tm$ by
$273$\,K, which propagates directly into the ideal term); and the seeded scaffold
split is not stable across pipeline versions, so every regeneration is treated as
a new split with all downstream artifacts recomputed. Crystal supervision is
scarce: the test and validation splits contain essentially no measured $\dHfus$,
which is the quantitative backbone of the identifiability argument
(\S\ref{sec:framework}). External single-component data used for grounding are an
open crystal-property pool ($\sim$15{,}000 melting points), the VT-2005
$\sigma$-profile database, and ThermoML infinite-dilution activity coefficients;
each is filtered to exclude any molecule whose scaffold appears in the
validation or test split.

\subsection{Graph encoder and pair representation}
Solute and solvent are encoded by a \emph{shared} graph network with three
interchangeable backbones: a message-passing network (MPNN), a hybrid
local-message/global-attention graph transformer (GPS \cite{rampasek2022}), and a
physics-channelled variant, Thermodynamically-Informed Message Passing (TIMP),
that splits messages into dispersive, polar, and hydrogen-bonding channels
supervised toward the Hansen solubility parameters (the dispersive, polar, and
hydrogen-bonding components of a molecule's cohesive energy density) to prevent
channel collapse.\footnote{TIMP is a physics-motivated backbone we introduced to
test whether a thermodynamically structured encoder helps; across our runs it does
not beat the plain MPNN, consistent with the encoder being transfer-limited rather
than expressivity-limited (a linear probe reaches train $R^2\approx0.76$ but test
$R^2\approx0.45$).} Small hydride
solvents such as water are given explicit hydrogens so the polar and
hydrogen-bond channels activate. Solute and solvent embeddings interact through a
bipartite cross-attention/message-passing block and a physics-aware readout, and
are combined into a pair representation
$[\,g_s,\,g_v,\,g_s\!\odot g_v,\,|g_s-g_v|\,]$ passed through an MLP, optionally
augmented with RDKit descriptors, Morgan fingerprints, and an ionic-context
feature. All heads below consume this pair representation; the DirectGNN control
consumes the identical representation.

\subsection{Crystal head and the ideal term}
A fusion head predicts the two crystal properties that define the ideal
(activity-free) solubility, with physically bounded parametrisations
$\Tm=100+600\,\sigma(\cdot)\in[100,700]\,\mathrm{K}$ and
$\dHfus=S_H\,\mathrm{softplus}(\cdot)>0$; an optional entropy-coupled mode instead
predicts a Walden-constrained fusion entropy (Walden's rule: rigid organics have a
near-constant entropy of fusion, ${\sim}57$\,J\,mol$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$) and sets
$\Tm=\dHfus/\Delta S$. When a
group-contribution $\Tm$ prior is used it is affine-calibrated on the training
set before entering the head. The crystal properties give the ideal term
\begin{equation}
  \Phicry(T)=\frac{\dHfus}{\Rgas}\!\left(\frac1T-\frac1\Tm\right),
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
the constant-$\Delta C_p$ correction omitted (its magnitude is audited in the SI,
\S\ref{sec:si-mech}).

\subsection{Three interchangeable activity closures}
The activity term \lng\ is produced by one of three closures, selected by
configuration, all differentiable and all consuming the same pair representation.

\paragraph{NRTL (Non-Random Two-Liquid \cite{renon1968}).} A head predicts the two
interaction energies and a non-randomness factor, $\tau_{12}^{\mathrm{ref}},\beta_{12},
\tau_{21}^{\mathrm{ref}},\beta_{21},\alpha\in[0.1,0.6]$, with a temperature law
$\tau_{ij}(T)=\tau_{ij}^{\mathrm{ref}}+\beta_{ij}(1/T-1/T_{\mathrm{ref}})$; an
optional UNIFAC (UNIQUAC Functional-group Activity Coefficients \cite{fredenslund1975})
group-contribution prior regularises the $\tau$'s. This is the
default and the most expressive activity model.

\paragraph{Single-parameter (\texttt{gamma\_inf}).} Collapsing the NRTL closure to
a symmetric one-parameter form yields the analytic infinite-dilution law
$\lng(x_2,T)=\lngi(T)\,(1-x_2)^2$, with $\lngi$ affine in $1/T$. This is the
minimal activity model and the setting in which the identifiability analysis is
cleanest (\S\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{Differentiable COSMO-SAC.} A $\sigma$-profile head emits, for each
molecule, a $51$-bin screening-charge-density profile $p(\sigma)$ (area per bin,
summing to the cavity area $A$). A parameter-free COSMO-SAC layer (Lin--Sandler /
VT-2005 conventions) maps the pair of profiles to \lng\ by a damped fixed point
for the segment activity coefficient $\Gamma_{S}$ (distinct from the grounding gap $\Gamma$),
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{S}(\sigma_m)=\Big[\textstyle\sum_n p_{\mathrm{norm}}(\sigma_n)\,\Gamma_{S}(\sigma_n)\,
  \exp\!\big(-\Delta W(\sigma_m,\sigma_n)/RT\big)\Big]^{-1},
  \label{eq:cosmo}
\end{equation}
with a precomputed exchange-energy matrix $\Delta W$ (electrostatic misfit plus a
hydrogen-bonding term); the restoring contribution is
$\lng^{\mathrm{res}}=(A_2/a_{\mathrm{eff}})\sum_m p_2^{\mathrm{pure}}(\sigma_m)
[\ln\Gamma_{S,\mathrm{mix}}(\sigma_m)-\ln\Gamma_{S,2}^{\mathrm{pure}}(\sigma_m)]$, and
an optional Staverman--Guggenheim combinatorial term uses the molecular cavity
volume and area. The combinatorial size factor $r_i=V_i/r_0$ takes the
\emph{reference} COSMO cavity volume ($V_{\mathrm{cosmo}}$) so that it shares the cavity
basis of the area $A_i$. This closure --- COSMO-SAC evaluated over a network-
\emph{predicted} $\sigma$-profile, and the intervention that swaps that profile
for the external reference one --- is the object of study in the grounding analysis
(\S\ref{sec:paradox}).

\subsection{The SLE solver and implicit differentiation}
Given $\Phicry(T)$ and a closure for \lng, the saturated mole fraction solves
$\lnx=-\Phicry(T)-\lng(x_2,T)$, a fixed point in $x_2$ because \lng\ depends on
composition. We iterate a damped map
\begin{equation}
  x_2^{(k+1)}=\lambda\,\exp\!\big(-\Phicry(T)-\lng(x_2^{(k)},T)\big)+(1-\lambda)\,x_2^{(k)},
  \label{eq:sle-iter}
\end{equation}
with closure-specific kernels for the NRTL, \texttt{gamma\_inf}, and COSMO-SAC
paths. Rather than backpropagating through the iterations, gradients use the
implicit-function theorem at the converged $x_2^\star$,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \lnx^\star}{\partial\theta}
  =-\,\frac{\partial(\Phicry+\lng)/\partial\theta}{\,1+x_2^\star\,\eta\,},
  \qquad \eta=\frac{\partial\lng}{\partial x_2},
  \label{eq:implicit}
\end{equation}
which is exact at the fixed point, memory-constant in the iteration count, and
exposes an explicit stability criterion: the gradient is well conditioned unless
$1+x_2^\star\eta\to0$. Iteration counts (train $16$, eval $30$) were set by a
convergence audit --- a shorter $n{=}8$ schedule failed a $5.86$-\lnx\ convergence
test on strongly non-ideal pairs (SI \S\ref{sec:si-methods}).

\subsection{Adaptive correction and the oracle substitution}
A bounded, gated ``adaptive physics correction'' perturbs the physical parameters
($\Tm,\dHfus,\tau$) rather than the output, re-solves \eqref{eq:sle-iter}, and
blends $\lnx^{\mathrm{final}}=\lnx^{\mathrm{phys}}+(1-w)\,\mathrm{clip}(\Delta)$;
keeping the correction in parameter space preserves the physical decomposition.
The \emph{oracle substitution} (a $\sigma$-oracle) replaces the predicted
$\sigma$-profile $\hat z$ with the reference VT-2005 profile $\zstar$ before the closure,
matched by canonical SMILES. It is applied three ways to rule out wiring
artifacts: at evaluation only, injected at training time so the rest of the model
co-adapts, and as a channel-swap control; an analogous override substitutes reference
crystal targets. The activity target used for the closure/insufficiency
decomposition (\S\ref{sec:measure}) is evaluated at infinite dilution,
$\lngi=\lim_{x_2\to0}\lng$, at $298$\,K.

\subsection{Training: three-phase curriculum and single-component grounding}
Optimisation follows a fixed three-phase curriculum: (P1) auxiliary-property
pretraining on crystal, Hansen, and \lngi\ targets with no SLE loss; (P2) full
SLE training, with the adaptive correction unfrozen partway and an optional
crystal$\to$pair weight ramp; (P3) low-learning-rate stabilisation with
monotonicity and van't~Hoff regularisers. External single-component data enter
through \emph{grounding streams}: each stream is a sidecar of \emph{self-solvent}
rows ($\mathrm{solvent}=\mathrm{solute}$, no solubility label, only the target
factor's mask active), interleaved into P1/P2 at a configurable step budget, with
a mandatory scaffold-leak guard excluding any pool molecule whose scaffold is in
validation or test. The reference streams ground the crystal factor (melting-point
pool) and the activity factor ($\sigma$-profile database); this
single-component-grounding pattern is the mechanism by which an otherwise
non-identifiable factor (\S\ref{sec:framework}) is pinned by abundant external
data a black-box predictor cannot use.

\subsection{Objective}
The training loss is a weighted sum with per-phase weights. Only a small set of
terms is load-bearing for the claims in this paper: a bin-weighted Huber on \lnx;
auxiliary losses on the $\sigma$-profile (an earth-mover distance), on crystal
$\Tm/\dHfus$, on \lngi, and on Hansen parameters; and structural regularisers for
temperature monotonicity and the van't~Hoff slope. The remaining terms (channel
orthogonality, contrastive, mixture-of-experts balance, and various priors) are
auxiliary; the complete taxonomy and weights are tabulated in the SI
(\S\ref{sec:si-methods}).

\subsection{The DirectGNN control}
DirectGNN shares the encoder, interaction, readout, and pair representation, adds
a thermometer temperature encoding, and maps directly to \lnx\ with a
bin-weighted Huber loss and no heads, closure, or solver. Because it shares
everything but the thermodynamic bottleneck, the TGNNSolv$-$DirectGNN gap is
attributable to the bottleneck rather than to representation, and DirectGNN is the
reference for every ``physics tax'' comparison in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
All controlled comparisons are run at three seeds and reported as mean$\pm$sd on a
cross-arm intersection lock (rows supervised and finite in every arm). Solver
iteration counts, COSMO-SAC constants, architecture and optimisation hyperparameters, the
load-bearing loss weights, and the compute ledger (which results are full-corpus GPU runs versus
CPU-reproducible proxies) are given in the SI (\S\ref{sec:si-methods},
\S\ref{sec:si-repro}); the released code regenerates every figure and
table from committed artifacts.
