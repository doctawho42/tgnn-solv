# Adversarial peer-review digest — grounding_paradox.tex

_5-lens panel (thermo · ML/stats · theory · novelty · presentation), each concern triaged against the paper, then AC meta-review. Run wf_3c063de1-ffe, 2026-07-13._

**Verdict: MAJOR REVISION**  ·  20 surviving concerns (1 reject, 8 major, 8 minor, 3 question); 7 pre-empted/addressed.

## Meta-review

This is a negative-result-plus-method paper: it shows that feeding a differentiable COSMO-SAC solubility pipeline its true, externally measured VT-2005 sigma-profiles makes activity/solubility prediction worse than a free learned head ("the grounding paradox"), then turns that anomaly into a measurement instrument. Because the physical latent has external ground truth, the true-input excess risk splits exactly as B = B_closure + B_insuff (a Kennedy-O'Hagan bias-variance discrepancy split the paper does not claim as new), and the headline empirical finding is that on a matched n=60 low-gamma/298K/infinite-dilution corner the closure term dominates the input-insufficiency term. This is scaffolded by three elementary lemmas (non-identifiability of the crystal/activity split, a grounding-gap sandwich, a physics-tax sign rule), a synthetic fidelity dial, a second chemical closure (pKa through a Hammett LFER, with a measured OPERA sweep), a compensation-surrogate mechanism, a rank-one "correctable closure defect," and a data-efficiency null. The paper explicitly disclaims any accuracy result. The conceptual framing, the intellectual honesty, and the reproducibility discipline are genuine strengths. But the single new, load-bearing empirical claim -- "the closure is the likely binding term" -- is (a) not certified by the paper's own inference (P~0.89, decisive-margin CI90 [-0.22,+0.97] includes zero, verdict flips under the ridge estimator), (b) confined to a corner the paper concedes does not transfer to the finite-composition/high-gamma regime the paradox actually spans, and (c) exposed to two independent, currently-unguarded artifact explanations: a non-standard, unvalidated closure implementation and an unidentified conditional-variance estimation at n=60 in 102-D. The core is salvageable, but not at its current level of validation and scoping.

### Top gating issues

- 1. The keystone verdict (B_closure > B_insuff) is not statistically established. B_insuff = E[Var(m|z*)] is a conditional-variance functional estimated in 102 dimensions from n=60 over a crossed 41-solute x 17-solvent design where any two pairs sharing a solute share 51/102 coordinates exactly; every estimator is a smoother, they span 0.40-0.88, and the deployed-convention verdict flips under ridge. Near-duplicate (shared-solvent/solute) neighbors bias the small B_insuff estimates DOWNWARD -- exactly the direction that manufactures the separation -- yet the paper guards only leave-one-SOLVENT-out (never leave-one-solute-out, despite one solute contributing 12/60 pairs), and its confidence rests on a single-axis 17-cluster bootstrap (<20 clusters, ignores repeated-solute correlation) whose 90% margin already includes zero. REQUIRED before 'likely the closure' stays in the abstract/contributions: leave-one-solute-out and joint leave-one-(solute,solvent)-out; a distinct-solute-and-solvent neighbor restriction for the conditional-variance estimate; and two-way cluster-robust or solute+solvent random-effects inference. Reconcile the contradictory 'biased up' vs 'most optimistic' kNN characterizations.

- 2. The differentiable COSMO-SAC closure is non-standard and never externally validated, so B_closure is not distinguished from an implementation/HB-kernel artifact. The DEPLOYED closure is residual-only (Staverman-Guggenheim combinatorial term removed) -- an incomplete activity model, not COSMO-SAC. The res true-input MSE (~1.47, RMSE ~1.21 in ln gamma_inf) is implausibly poor for a correct VT-2005 COSMO-SAC and is concentrated in the amine/alcohol/glycol systems where COSMO-SAC's single-parameter hydrogen-bonding kernel is the most documented weak point, while pyridine/alkane pairs are near-exact. The 'independent recomputation' reproduces the authors' own output, not a reference. The Tier-2 'more-physics-worse' (1.47->1.80 on adding SG) is the well-known miscalibration of the unmodified SG combinatorial term for size-asymmetric pairs, not evidence of a general grounding paradox. REQUIRED: validate the layer against a canonical COSMO-SAC / published VT-2005 IDAC values; physically justify (or drop) residual-only as the deployed default; reconcile the SG result with the documented combinatorial-miscalibration literature (modified-SG / Kikic / free-volume).

- 3. Significance/scope: after the paper's own concessions, the residual novel-and-load-bearing payload is thin and its applicability is narrower than advertised. The instrument runs only where an external latent oracle exists (VT-2005-style tabulated/measured latent), yet the paper markets it as general over 'any composed predictor g(h(x))' including PINNs/gray-box hybrids -- precisely the class that has NO external oracle for its internal fields, so the instrument cannot run there. No instance is shown where the instrument changed a modeling outcome (the rank-1 fix is on the true-input n=60 set; end-to-end transfer is compute-gated and left open). The framing-level novelty is never positioned against existing model-discrepancy / auxiliary-data UQ that already isolates structural error with held-out physical observations. REQUIRED: a decisive rescoping that (a) restricts the instrument to fixed-closure predictors with a tabulated/measured latent (LFERs, COSMO/group-contribution, thermodynamic cycles) and distinguishes this from the PINN/gray-box settings it currently invokes; (b) positions the external-latent split against model-discrepancy/auxiliary-data UQ; and ideally (c) an in-regime or larger-set (e.g. the InChIKey-matched ~85-pair set) measurement so the headline is not confined to a corner disclaimed as non-transferable.

- 4. Label noise is assumed zero on exactly the highest-scatter pairs, and the verdict is sensitive to high-leverage, physically suspect data. B_closure is carried by amine/alcohol/glycol IDAC measurements (largest inter-lab disagreement) and by individual leverage points -- e.g. 1-hexanamine in methanol at ln gamma_inf = +2.62 (gamma_inf ~ 13.7 for two miscible H-bonders, implausibly asymmetric vs its reverse -0.97), contributing ~20% of total MSE. The 'bounds invariant to zero-mean noise' defense does not cover a single outlier/systematic-bias point, nor a systematic (non-random) conformer/tautomer/protonation z* mismatch, which inflates B_closure rather than B_insuff and breaks the 'mismatch only inflates B_insuff, hence conservative' argument; the ad hoc dropping of 2 formamide pairs is a spot-fix, not a bound. REQUIRED: a robust/trimmed-estimator + leave-one-pair-out/influence analysis showing B_closure > B_insuff survives; an external inter-source IDAC noise floor (as done for solubility); an audit of the top-leverage points against primary sources; and either a bound on or explicit scoping of systematic-z*-mismatch error.

- 5. Several load-bearing claims are overstated or internally inconsistent and must be corrected regardless of the above. (a) 'the true-input error splits---with no model fit' (abstract) and 'both components are separately estimable from data with no model fit' (Lemma 3) are false for B_insuff, which requires a fitted high-dimensional conditional-variance smoother whose choice flips the verdict; restrict 'no model fit' to the algebraic identity and the Jensen/MSE quantities. (b) The flagship decomposition figure plots the full-convention 0.72>0.62 separation the text concedes INVERTS (0.43<0.62) under the deployed residual-only model; redraw for the deployed convention or plot the load-bearing MSE-B_insuff bound. (c) The in-regime stratified table and 'strengthens toward higher gamma' claim are carried by the kNN estimator the robustness section explicitly disavows as 'most optimistic, not most trustworthy' (confirmed: binsuff_tightest == kNN in every stratum), and the small-n caveat cites the wrong (LOTV/binning) mechanism; recompute with RF/binning (under which the trend is non-monotone) or report only sign/direction. (d) The SI's 'differ slightly ... a hyperparameter effect' understates a verified 40% ridge swing (0.6245 -> 0.8772) that flips the verdict from holds to fails. (e) The data-efficiency 'matches case (ii) / n* below our smallest fraction' reads a testable prediction off a physics arm that is far more under-converged than DirectGNN under the identical budget (physics R2 to -0.79 vs direct +0.22), conflating inductive bias with optimization difficulty; either run a matched-convergence multi-seed sweep or drop the case-(ii)/n* reading and keep only 'under a fixed light budget the physics arm trails at every fraction.' (f) Delta_inf >= 0 (Prop. tax) requires the direct control be asymptotically Bayes, not merely V_direct -> 0; condition the sign explicitly. (g) The compensation-surrogate section's only non-tautological leg (16x/26x transferability) is sourced from confounded/two-model analyses while the confound-free one-model control's transferability (~1.7x, near identity) is omitted; report it or drop the 'systematic transferable surrogate' framing.

### Rationale

Judged as a negative-result-plus-method paper, this is not a reject: the conceptual contribution, the honesty, the theory, and the multiple corroborating instruments (synthetic dial, pKa measured sweep, compensation surrogate, rank-one correctability) constitute a coherent package that is more than 'one measurement,' and the paper correctly refuses to make the accuracy claim it cannot support. But it is well past minor revision, because its single genuinely-new, load-bearing empirical payload -- 'the closure dominates' -- is currently defeasible three ways at once: it is not certified by the authors' own inference (margin CI includes zero; verdict flips under ridge), it lives in a corner the paper concedes does not transfer to the regime the paradox spans, and it has two live artifact explanations (an unvalidated, non-standard residual-only closure whose error concentrates exactly in the associating systems where COSMO-SAC is known to fail; and an unidentified conditional-variance estimate at n=60 in 102-D whose small values may be downward-biased by near-duplicate neighbors, guarded only along the solvent axis). Any one of these, if it resolves against the paper, would hollow the keystone. The remedy is substantial but well-defined: validate the closure against a reference implementation, redo the insufficiency inference with leave-one-solute-out / distinct-pair neighbors / two-way-clustered or random-effects confidence and a robust/leave-one-pair-out check on the leverage points, and either extend the measurement in-regime (or to the larger matched set) or rescope the abstract/contributions so the paper advertises exactly the bounded instrument it has -- while fixing the 'no model fit,' inverting-figure, kNN-in-regime, ridge-'differ slightly,' data-efficiency case-(ii), Delta_inf-sign, and one-model-transferability overstatements. If the re-analysis and validation hold, the paper clears the bar for a negative-result-plus-method venue; if they collapse the closure-dominance verdict, it converges to a reject. Major revision is the decisive and fair call.

### Genuine strengths (panel consensus)

- Exceptional, self-adversarial intellectual honesty that is rare in the literature: the paper concedes the decomposition is textbook, labels the grounding gap a 'symptom not a certificate,' tiers convention-dependent vs convention-independent evidence, disavows its own pKa 'win,' reports the four hedges in full, and repeatedly states 'we make no accuracy claim.' A reader is rarely misled about what is and is not established.

- The central reframing is a genuine conceptual service to physics-informed ML: turning the anomaly 'truer physical inputs make the model worse' into an externally grounded procedure that attributes a physics prior's ceiling to the closure vs the inputs is a clean, reusable lens, and the negative result -- a well-argued caution against the widely assumed 'truer inputs -> better OOD' premise -- is a real contribution when properly scoped.

- The theory scaffold is coherent and well-integrated with the empirics: the non-identifiability of the crystal/activity split, the collinearity/Fisher-conditioning story (conditioning ~1e16 as Delta T -> 0), the grounding-gap sandwich, and the physics-tax sign rule form a consistent account of why the ceiling sits where it does, and the proofs are elementary and mostly correct in their load-bearing core (the flagged theory issues are precision/well-definedness, not fatal).

- The paper does not rest on a single thread: the synthetic fidelity dial (four non-chemistry teacher families, six misspecification forms), the second real closure (pKa/Hammett with a measured OPERA fidelity sweep), the compensation-surrogate mechanism, and the rank-one correctable-defect result each corroborate the story from a different angle, so the qualitative picture has multiple independent supports even where individual quantitative claims are contested.

- Strong reproducibility discipline: every figure and table is tied to a named script and a committed intermediate artifact, with number-provenance comments in the source and deterministic regeneration paths, which made the reviewers' verification (and this meta-review's) tractable.


## Surviving concerns (ranked)

### [1] REJECT · novelty · significance / aggregate contribution — triage: PARTIAL

**After the paper's own concessions, the residual novel payload is one uncertain, self-disclaimed-non-transferable measurement on a 60-pair corner**

- *Detail:* This is the single strongest reason to reject, and it is a whole-paper significance judgment, not an attack on any single hedged claim. The paper honestly concedes away nearly every component: the composed-predictor theory is 'textbook'/'elementary' (Contributions bullet 6; sec:framework); the B=B_closure+B_insuff split is a Kennedy-O'Hagan bias-variance discrepancy split it explicitly 'do[es] not claim as new' (Lemma 3 discussion); the paradox 'will not surprise a thermodynamics specialist' (Intro); the sigma-surrogate is 'nearly tautological in-distribution' (compensation-surrogate); and accuracy is a stated non-claim (Discussion). What remains as genuinely new AND load-bearing is a single empirical finding -- that B_closure dominates B_insuff -- and by the authors' own accounting it is (a) not statistically certified (P~=0.89, CI90 on the margin [-0.22,+0.97]), (b) measured on n=60 pairs (41 solutes, 17 solvents) in one low-gamma/298K/infinite-dilution/one-closure corner, and (c) explicitly disclaimed as NOT transferring to the finite-composition/high-gamma regime the headline paradox actually spans. So the advertised deliverable -- 'a general method for locating a physics prior's ceiling' -- has, on the paper's own evidence, been decisively applied zero times in the regime it claims to explain and once inconclusively in a corner. The novelty of the 'affordance' (use an external measurement of the latent to bound the split) is also never positioned against existing model-discrepancy / auxiliary-data UQ that already isolates structural error with held-out physical observations, so even the framing-level novelty is asserted rather than demonstrated.

- *Where:* Abstract; Contributions (grounding_paradox.tex 117-167); sec:measure P~=0.89 (651) and Conclusion (871); Discussion 780-807; Lemma 3 discussion (382-388)

- *Residual after triage:* The paper does not deliver — and by its own admission cannot yet deliver — a certified, transferable, in-solubility-regime (finite-composition/high-γ) application of the instrument: the shared-cause attribution there remains an explicitly labeled conjecture (Discussion 782-784), and its one trained second-system "win" (pKa) is self-hedged as a permissive-closure/per-scaffold-prior effect rather than a closure-fidelity confirmation (pka-domain.tex 46-59). Separately, the specific positioning the reviewer asks for — what the external-latent split-measurement adds beyond auxiliary-data/held-out-physical-observation UQ — is not written; the paper engages only Kennedy–O'Hagan/Brynjarsdóttir model-discrepancy calibration. Both are legitimate residuals for a significance-level judgment, though the reviewer's "reject: residual payload is one uncertain measurement" overstates the gap by omitting the pKa/OPERA measured sweep, the synthetic dial, the sign-rule phase picture, and the rank-one correctability result.

- *Suggested response:* Either (i) deliver at least one certified, transferable, decision-changing application of the instrument -- extend the matched activity set well beyond n=60 and out of the low-gamma/298K corner, or measure and act on the split in a second real system in the regime the paradox spans -- or (ii) reframe honestly as a focused measurement/negative-result note and target a venue scaled to that scope. In either case, add an explicit positioning paragraph stating precisely what the 'external-latent split-measurement' move adds beyond Kennedy-O'Hagan discrepancy calibration and auxiliary-data UQ.

### [2] MAJOR · thermo · COSMO-SAC treatment: convention + implementation validity — triage: PARTIAL

**The differentiable COSMO-SAC is non-standard (deployed residual-only, no Staverman-Guggenheim term) and never validated against a reference implementation; the 'more-physics-worse' and B_closure effects are textbook COSMO-SAC HB/combinatorial deficiencies, not an established closure ceiling**

- *Detail:* Two thermodynamic problems undercut attributing the ceiling to 'the COSMO-SAC closure.' (1) The DEPLOYED closure is residual-only (Table 2 caption: 'res = residual-only, the deployed default'), i.e. COSMO-SAC with the Staverman-Guggenheim combinatorial term REMOVED. That is not COSMO-SAC; it is an incomplete activity model. The paper's Tier-2 'adding the physically more complete SG term increases error (1.47->1.80)' is then not evidence of a general grounding paradox but the well-known behavior that the unmodified SG combinatorial term is miscalibrated for size-asymmetric pairs (the reason modified-SG / Kikic-exponent / free-volume corrections exist); the residual and combinatorial terms are simply miscalibrated in opposite directions. (2) The measured true-input error (res MSE 1.47, RMSE 1.21 in ln gamma_inf on 298 K organics) is implausibly poor for a correct VT-2005 COSMO-SAC, and it is concentrated exactly in the amine/alcohol/glycol systems (mean residual^2 2.24, with outright sign errors) where COSMO-SAC's single-parameter hydrogen-bonding kernel (c_hb, sigma_hb cutoff) is the most documented weak point, while pyridine/alkane pairs are near-exact (0.045). Nowhere is the differentiable layer validated against a canonical COSMO-SAC / published VT-2005 IDAC predictions; the 'independent recomputation' in the limitations reproduces the authors' OWN output, not a reference. So B_closure is not distinguished from (a) the residual-only convention choice, (b) the crude HB term, or (c) an implementation artifact (grid, damping/iteration count, area normalization). Directly answering the framing question: as measured, 'true sigma-profiles hurt COSMO-SAC' is the long-known limited IDAC accuracy of COSMO-SAC on associating systems, not a novel general result.

- *Where:* methods.tex COSMO-SAC closure (L90-110); SI.tex COSMO-SAC constants (L9-17); grounding_paradox.tex Tier-2 'more-physics-worse' (L683-699); Table tab:decomp caption (L598-605)

- *Residual after triage:* No external validation of the differentiable COSMO-SAC layer against a reference implementation or published VT-2005 IDAC values exists anywhere in the manuscript/SI, so the ~1.47 residual-only true-input MSE (B_closure) is not provably closure misspecification rather than an implementation artifact concentrated in the HB kernel/grid/damping; the near-exact non-associating strata rule out a global bug but not a localized HB-term one. Additionally, the Tier-2 more-physics-worse (1.47->1.80) is not reconciled with the well-documented unmodified-SG combinatorial miscalibration for size-asymmetric pairs, and residual-only is not physically justified as the deployed default. These are the fixes the reviewer's suggested_response items 1-3 request; only the framing/novelty and convention-tiering pieces are already handled.

- *Suggested response:* Validate the differentiable COSMO-SAC against a reference implementation (or published VT-2005 IDAC values) on a held-out set and report the agreement; make the FULL COSMO-SAC (residual + combinatorial) the default rather than residual-only, or justify residual-only physically. Explicitly separate 'COSMO-SAC's known HB-term inaccuracy on associating systems' from the paper's claimed general 'closure ceiling,' and temper the more-physics-worse claim as a combinatorial-term calibration issue.

### [3] MAJOR · thermo · Label noise / data quality on IDAC targets — triage: PARTIAL

**Label noise is assumed zero on exactly the pairs with the largest experimental scatter, and the verdict is sensitive to high-leverage, physically suspect data points**

- *Detail:* The decomposition assumes sigma_eta^2 = 0 because the matched set has no replicates (Table 2 last row; decomposition.json label_noise). But the B_closure signal is carried by amine/alcohol/glycol IDAC measurements — precisely the class with the largest inter-lab disagreement — and by individual high-leverage points that look physically wrong: 1-hexanamine in methanol reads ln gamma_inf = +2.62 (gamma_inf ~ 13.7) for two mutually H-bonding, fully miscible liquids, while the reverse pair (methanol in hexylamine) is -0.97, an implausible asymmetry; this single point contributes ~20% of the total MSE. The paper's defense that 'the bounds are invariant to zero-mean noise' does not apply here: an outlier or a systematic measurement bias is not zero-mean noise on that point, and it directly inflates both the MSE-minus-B_insuff bound and the Jensen mean-offset (mean_m = 0.75 is itself set by a few large positive values). The same issue applies to z*: VT-2005 profiles are single-conformer/single-protonation (SI provenance), and the paper already had to drop 2 formamide pairs for a tautomer mismatch — a systematic conformer/tautomer/protonation mismatch produces systematic g(z*) error that the paper attributes to B_closure, so the 'mismatch only inflates B_insuff, hence conservative' argument fails for any correlated (non-random) mismatch.

- *Where:* grounding_paradox.tex Table tab:decomp label-noise row (L617) and 'assumed zero' text (L664-665); SI.tex data provenance / conservativeness argument (L354-358); results/b_insuff/matched_pairs.csv

- *Residual after triage:* Two genuine, still-open residuals. (1) IDAC outlier/leverage: the paper's own defense is expressly zero-mean, so it does not cover the single 1-hexanamine/methanol point that is confirmed to carry ~20% of the MSE and looks physically suspect (gamma_inf~13.7 for two miscible H-bonders, implausibly asymmetric vs its reverse); there is no robust/downweighted estimator, no leave-one-pair-out or influence analysis, no external inter-source IDAC noise floor (as was done for solubility in sec:supporting), and no audit of the top-leverage amine/glycol points against primary sources. The solvent-clustered bootstrap only resamples whole solvents and never downweights an aberrant point, and mean_m=0.748 (which sets the Jensen offset) is itself driven by a few large positive values. (2) Systematic z* mismatch: the SI "mismatch inflates B_insuff, hence conservative" claim (L356-358) is stated unconditionally but is valid only for *random* mismatch; a correlated/systematic conformer/tautomer/protonation error makes the fixed closure g(z*) systematically wrong at the input it is given, which by construction inflates B_closure, not B_insuff -- so the conservativeness argument fails exactly for the non-random case, and the ad hoc dropping of 2 formamide tautomer pairs (L590) is a spot-fix, not a bound. The authors should add a robust/trimmed-estimator + leave-one-pair-out check showing Bclos>Binsuff survives, and either bound or explicitly scope the systematic-z*-mismatch error.

- *Suggested response:* Estimate an IDAC label-noise floor from an external replicate/inter-source IDAC compilation (as done for solubility in sec:supporting), audit the top leverage points against primary sources, and show the B_closure > B_insuff verdict survives a robust/outlier-downweighted estimator. Justify (or bound the error from) the single-conformer/protonation assumption for the ionizable amines and acids in the set.

### [4] MAJOR · ml-stats · statistics / keystone measurement (B_closure vs B_insuff) — triage: PARTIAL

**The load-bearing closure-dominance verdict rests on conditional-variance estimates that are unidentified at n=60 in 102-D over a crossed design, with a downward-bias risk that is not guarded**

- *Detail:* The entire attribution reduces to a single inequality: since B_closure = MSE − B_insuff exactly, the verdict B_closure>B_insuff holds iff B_insuff < MSE/2 = 0.74 (res). The candidate B_insuff upper bounds straddle that boundary (kNN 0.40, binning/LOTV 0.53-0.62, RF-OOF 0.62, ridge 0.88), so certification is entirely a function of which estimator is trusted. B_insuff = E[Var(m|z*)] is not identified here at all: there are zero replicates at a fixed z*, and z* is the 102-D concatenation of the solute and solvent σ-profiles, so any two pairs sharing a solute (or solvent) share 51 of 102 coordinates exactly (e.g. 20 pyridine pairs share an identical solvent half). Every 'irreducible-variance' estimator (1-NN, RF-OOF, LOTV binning of g(z*)) therefore borrows strength across near-duplicate rows; a 1-NN or a bin can pick a same-solvent near-twin whose m is close, which biases the conditional-variance estimate DOWNWARD and makes the upper bound anti-conservative in exactly the direction that manufactures the separation. The paper guards only leave-one-SOLVENT-out (and even there the RF estimate fails 15/17 folds), never leave-one-solute-out, and labels kNN simultaneously 'biased up' (conservative) and 'most optimistic' (anti-conservative) — contradictory characterizations that reveal the estimator's behavior is not actually understood. The clustered bootstrap that quantifies confidence (P≈0.89) resamples 17 solvent clusters only, on a crossed 41-solute×17-solvent design: <~20 clusters is far too few for reliable bootstrap tail probabilities, and single-axis (solvent-only) clustering ignores the solute-level correlation from repeated solutes, so P≈0.89 is itself optimistic and the decisive-margin 90% CI [−0.22, +0.97] already includes zero. In short, the paper's central measured contribution (R2, 'the closure dominates') is not established at conventional confidence, and even the honest 'likely' (P≈0.89) is not a trustworthy number.

- *Where:* paper/grounding_paradox.tex Table 2 (tab:decomp, ll.592-620) and §sec:measure ll.641-666; SI §sec:si-tables ll.168-198; results/b_insuff/{decomposition,robustness,regime}.json; results/b_insuff/matched_pairs.csv (60 pairs, 41 solutes, 17 solvents, 0 replicates, pyridine=20 pairs)

- *Residual after triage:* Not addressed and genuinely open: (a) no leave-one-SOLUTE-out or joint leave-one-(solute,solvent)-out despite heavy solute-side leverage (one solute = 12/60 pairs) — only leave-one-solvent-out is run; (b) no two-way/crossed cluster-robust inference or solute+solvent random-effects model — confidence rests entirely on a 17-solvent single-axis bootstrap that ignores repeated-solute correlation and whose <20-cluster tail reliability the paper does not defend; (c) the zero-replicate identification of E[Var(m|z*)] in 102-D and the near-duplicate (51/102 shared-coordinate) downward-bias direction that would make the small B_insuff estimates anti-conservative is never engaged — no distinct-solute-and-solvent neighbor restriction is shown; (d) the "biased up" vs "most optimistic" kNN labels are not reconciled at the level of bias direction. Because these are undone, the paper's surviving "likely the closure (P≈0.89)" claim still rests on the very inference the reviewer argues is optimistic; a strict area chair should require at minimum the leave-one-solute-out + two-way-clustered (or random-effects) re-analysis and the distinct-pair neighbor check before the "likely binding term" phrase stays in the abstract/contributions.

- *Suggested response:* Do not present closure-dominance as measured/likely at a specific probability until the estimator is validated against the crossed design: (a) report leave-one-SOLUTE-out and joint leave-one-(solute,solvent)-out alongside the solvent version; (b) use crossed/two-way cluster-robust inference (or a solute+solvent random-effects model) rather than a 17-solvent bootstrap; (c) demonstrate the small B_insuff estimates are not driven by same-solute/same-solvent near-duplicates (e.g. restrict neighbors to distinct-solute-and-solvent pairs); and (d) reconcile the 'biased up' vs 'most optimistic' descriptions of kNN. If the separation cannot be made robust at n=60, reframe the contribution as 'a method to measure the split, illustrated on a corner where it is suggestive but under-powered,' and drop the 'closure is the binding term' framing from the abstract/contributions/conclusion.

### [5] MAJOR · ml-stats · ML methodology / physics-tax proposition — triage: PARTIAL

**The physics-tax proposition is not actually tested: the data-efficiency evidence is single-seed and confounded by a badly under-converged physics arm**

- *Detail:* Prop. tax(ii) predicts a critical budget n* below which the physics prior HELPS (variance savings exceed Δ∞). The sweep is offered as consistent with it, but (1) it is single-seed (seed 42), and (2) the physics arm is not converged at the 'lighter per-fraction budget': its R^2 runs from −0.09 (5%) to −0.98 (50%) — worse than predicting the mean — and its fraction-1.0 MAE is 2.98 vs the properly-trained headline of 1.85 (results grounded_a). The DirectGNN control degrades far less under the same light budget (R^2 0.42→0.22, MAE 1.70→1.97). So the 'positive tax at every fraction, growing with n' is confounded by the physics arm requiring more optimization to converge — an optimization-error gap, not the statistical estimation-variance-vs-asymptotic-bias tradeoff the proposition is about. Crucially, no helping regime (n*) is ever observed; the data are declared 'consistent with n* below our smallest fraction,' which is unfalsifiable. The proposition's one distinctive, testable prediction (a low-data crossover) is therefore untested, and the existing caveat ('single-seed, non-monotone') does not cover the convergence confound. A comparison of a non-converged model to a converged one cannot adjudicate an inductive-bias claim.

- *Where:* paper/sections/data-efficiency.tex Table 1 (tab:data-eff, ll.42-49) and ll.13-19; results/data_efficiency/summary.json; Prop. tax (grounding_paradox.tex ll.465-482)

- *Residual after triage:* Two real residuals survive the hedging. ### [20] QUESTION · theory · physics-tax Proposition (iii) — triage: PARTIAL
